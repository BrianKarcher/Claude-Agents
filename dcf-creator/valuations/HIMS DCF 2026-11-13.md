## Valuation Framework

**Approach:** a 10-year explicit unlevered-FCF forecast (2026E–2035E). 2027E is discount year 1, end-of-year convention, unchanged from August for comparability. Two terminal-value methods, then averaged:
- **Method 1:** perpetuity growth (Gordon) on 2035E UFCF.
- **Method 2:** EV / adj. EBITDA exit multiple on 2035E adj. EBITDA.

**UFCF = NOPAT + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC**, where NOPAT = (Adj. EBITDA − SBC − D&A) × (1 − 15%) in years with positive operating income, and = operating income (no tax benefit) in loss years. SBC is treated as a real pre-tax cost, and shares are then held flat.

**Q4 2026 stub — decision:** 2026E is now three quarters actual. Because the equity bridge uses the **Sep 30, 2026** balance sheet, Q4's cash flow belongs to the valuation. Excluding it, as August did for the whole of 2026, would leave a one-quarter gap between the balance-sheet date and the first forecast year.
- I therefore **include only the Q4 2026E UFCF as an undiscounted stub** (~6 weeks away; discounting it would change it by <$1M): **Base -$42M, Bull -$38M, Bear -$46M**.
- Q1–Q3 2026 cash flows are already reflected in the 9/30 balance sheet, so they are not counted again.
- Base Q4: adj. EBITDA $75M − SBC $66M − D&A $39M = EBIT -$30M (no tax credit) + D&A $39M − CapEx $45M − ΔNWC $6M = **-$42M**.
- Full-year 2026E is still shown in the tables for continuity.
- *Why the stub is negative while management guides positive Q4 FCF:* the model charges SBC as a cash-equivalent cost and holds working capital to the model's -$25M/yr. Company-defined FCF adds SBC back and has been helped by receivable sales.

**Tax convention (changed):** a flat **15%** on positive operating income, no benefit in loss years. This is the dcf-creator house rule (the agent ruleset). It replaces the August model's 5%→25% ramp, which predated the house rule. 15% is a reasonable mid-term cash rate given HIMS's NOLs/DTAs ($121M of net deferred tax assets at 9/30; 9M 2026 cash taxes were a net refund of $2.2M). It is generous for a long-run US statutory payer. **At August's ramp-to-25% schedule, values would be Bull ~$101, Base ~$50, Bear ~$6.**

**Discount rates (unchanged):** Base **12.0%**, Bull 10.5%, Bear 13.5%.
- I considered raising the Base to 12.5% for the larger debt-like stack and the expanded FTC case.
- I kept 12.0% because those risks are now modeled explicitly in the bridge ($1.49B of claims) and the cash flows (lower margins, higher SBC). Raising the rate as well would double-count them.
- A 13% WACC lowers Base IV to ~$38 (perpetuity) / ~$58 (exit). See the sensitivity tables.

## Assessment

**On the base-case premise:** if HIMS still reaches $8.0B of revenue in 2030 at a 19% EBITDA margin, intrinsic value is **~$54/share**. That is ~2.7x the current ~$20 price, with a method range of ~$45 (perpetuity) to ~$63 (11x exit). As in August, every cell of the perpetuity WACC × g grid sits above the price. **Even on management's own $6.5B-by-2030 path, the value is ~$41.**

**But Q3 made the premise harder to defend.** The model hits $8B only by having growth *re-accelerate* to ~28% in 2028–2030 from a 2027 base that starts with fewer subscribers than mid-2026. Margins moved the wrong way again (gross margin -7 pts QoQ; EBITDA guide cut a third time). The new balance-sheet math also makes the downside much deeper: the **bear case is ~$7**, 63% below the price, versus ~$18 (38% below) in August. The asymmetry from ~$20 is roughly **down ~$13 / up ~$34 to the base**.

The market is pricing roughly the bear-case *shape*: ~10% growth at base margins, or base revenue at ~11–14% margins. That is why the probability-weighted value (~$51) still sits ~2.5x above the price.

**Rating: SPECULATIVE BUY on the base-case premise (unchanged), but position-size for a ~$7 downside, not ~$18.** The stock is cheaper and the base-case upside wider, but the bear case is closer to zero and the premise has less support than in August. Confirmations to watch in Q4 2026 / the Feb-2027 report:
1. Subscriber count returning to sequential growth in Q1 2027 (management's own claim).
2. The 2027 outlook and the 2030 targets: reaffirmed, raised or withdrawn. If $6.5B/$1.3B is withdrawn, the $8B premise should be revisited.
3. Gross margin stabilizing at ≥57%.
4. Whether the Eucalyptus installments are settled in cash or stock.
5. RPA usage and the true working-capital funding need.
