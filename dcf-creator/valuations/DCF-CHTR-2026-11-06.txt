WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
------------------------------
This values the NEW Charter: Charter + Cox Communications (closed Aug 19,
2026) + the Liberty Broadband collapse, as reported in its first combined
quarter. Three things make this valuation unusual, and you need all three
to read the numbers correctly.

1. THE EQUITY IS A THIN, HIGHLY LEVERED STUB.
   At $118.07 the common equity (all Charter Holdings common units, Class A
   plus A/N plus Cox) is worth ~$19.4B. Ahead of it sit $115.4B of claims:
     $108.6B debt principal less $0.6B cash;
     $6.0B Cox convertible preferred;
     $1.2B supply-chain finance;
     $0.2B CHTRP preferred.
   Equity is ~14% of enterprise value. Every $1B change in EV is ~$6.08 per
   share; every 1.0x turn of EBITDA is ~$164 per share. Small changes in
   operating assumptions therefore produce very large changes in per-share
   value, in both directions. Read the scenario range with that in mind.

2. THE CORE PRODUCT IS SHRINKING AND MANAGEMENT WON'T SAY WHEN THAT STOPS.
   Pro forma residential Internet revenue fell 3.7% YoY in Q3. The combined
   company lost 233K Internet customers: legacy Charter -156K vs -109K a
   year ago, and Cox -77K. Management "is not providing any guidance or
   outlook for exactly when we return to growth" and booked a $6.3B
   franchise impairment because its own long-range cash-flow projections
   were cut.

3. THE COST LEVER IS NOW ENORMOUS, BUT UNPROVEN.
   Management is targeting:
     ">$1B" Cox opex synergies (up from $500M at signing);
     a new $2B company-wide opex program over 18 months.
   That's $3B+ against a ~$20B addressable opex pool. Capex steps down from
   ~$13.0B (2026 PF) to "at or below $11B" (2027) and lower after that.
   The DCF hinges on how much of the $3B lands in EBITDA rather than being
   reinvested or competed away. The Base case credits only HALF of the
   $2B program (Section 8C).

Every judgment call that drives the answer is listed in Section 16, with the
per-share effect of the less conservative alternative.

HEADLINE FINDINGS
  (a) The Base case, built conservatively, is worth ~$162, about 37% above
      the price. But that whole gap is ~2.6% of EBITDA:
        - The price is fully explained by the Base case with a permanent
          ~$690M/yr shortfall in EBITDA (Section 14).
        - Equivalently, by Internet revenue declining 2.4% EVERY year for
          a decade instead of my Base path.
      This is not a "the market is crazy" finding. The market is pricing a
      modestly worse version of my Base case. The price looks cheap only
      if you are confident Internet stabilizes.

  (b) If the Q3 rate of Internet revenue decline (-3.7%/yr) simply
      persisted for ten years, the Base model is worth ~$54. At -5%/yr the
      equity is worth roughly zero. The broadband trend is the whole
      ballgame. Synergies and capex are second-order next to it.

  (c) The Bear case is not exotic: Internet revenue -4.0% in 2027, easing
      to -1.5%, with only $1.3B of the $3B+ cost program landing. Its value
      is negative, which says leverage at 3.9x is still too high for a
      business whose core line is shrinking. Every dollar of free cash flow
      that goes to debt rather than buybacks reduces that tail.

  (d) Levered FCF per economic share, Base: ~$41 in 2027, ~$50 in 2029-30
      (Section 15). That is a ~35-43% levered FCF yield on $118. The market
      is not ignoring that cash. It is doubting the terminal value behind
      it, and it is right to discount it heavily.

  (e) The Q3 print was operationally WORSE on the metric that matters most
      (Internet net adds) and BETTER on the controllables (cost program,
      2027 capex at or below $11B, Cox synergies raised to >$1B, the
      debt-exchange gain). The -6% move says the market weights the former
      more. This model agrees.

SECTION 1 - BUSINESS SNAPSHOT AND Q3 2026 TAKEAWAYS
================================================================================

Charter (Spectrum) is the second-largest US cable operator, and with Cox the
largest by passings: 70.7M passings and 36.7M customer relationships at
9/30/26. Revenue is overwhelmingly residential connectivity:
  - Internet, ~43% of PF revenue;
  - mobile service (MVNO on Verizon plus CBRS offload), ~7%;
  - a shrinking video business, ~22%, with programming costing ~65% of
    video revenue.
It is one reportable segment. There is no segment operating income, so
product-line revenue is the segment proxy (Section 3).

THE MOST IMPORTANT Q3 TAKEAWAYS
  1. Broadband got worse, again.
     - Legacy Charter Internet losses were -156K vs -109K, the fifth
       straight quarter worse than the prior-year quarter.
     - Churn was "essentially flat"; the problem is lower gross connects.
     - Management cites FWA expansion, weak lower-income sales, mobile
       substitution, fiber overlap, and "aggressive competitor promotions."
     - PF residential Internet revenue fell 3.7%.
  2. Peers confirm it is structural, not Charter-specific. Q3:
       Comcast  -144K (vs -91K)
       AT&T     +769K (442K fiber, 327K FWA, a record)
       Verizon  +314K (155K fiber, 159K FWA)
       T-Mobile no longer discloses the number but claims industry-leading
                FWA adds
     Cable is the share donor.
  3. Management cut its own long-range plan. The $6.3B franchise impairment
     reflects "lower estimates for future customer and revenue growth,
     given recent results and market perception of risk" (10-Q).
  4. A new cost program, much bigger than expected:
     - $2B of annualized opex savings over 18 months, ON TOP OF >$1B of Cox
       synergies (nearly half in 1H'27, 90% within 24 months).
     - "Not targeted at frontline service and sales employees."
     - The reinvestment share and the cost to achieve are NOT disclosed.
     - A new COO, Nick Jeffery (ex-Frontier, ex-Vodafone UK), leads it.
  5. Capex is peaking.
     - 2026 PF capex ~$13.0B excluding transition.
     - 2027 "at or below $11 billion."
     - Network investment is "set to decline after next year."
     - The subsidized rural build is winding down (Q3 rural capex $292M vs
       $580M).
  6. The balance sheet is behaving.
     - Net leverage 3.93x at 9/30; "below 3.9x" at YE26; 3.5x by ~Aug 2029.
     - A debt exchange retired ~$5.5B of notes for $622M cash plus ~$3.4B
       of new notes, booking a $1.4B gain and ~$1.5B of deleveraging.
     - Debt trades at 85.3% of par.
     - Most Q4 FCF goes to debt, with "some" buybacks.
  7. Cox early read.
     - Spectrum pricing launched Sep 16 and reportedly halved Cox's Internet
       loss run-rate.
     - Cox video grew slightly in October.
     - But Cox churn is ~10% higher than Charter's and Cox revenue fell
       4.5% YoY.
  8. Restatements: none.
     - New actual vs PF presentation.
     - Minor PF discrepancies between the release and the 10-Q (Section 18).
     - The release highlights say the debt exchange raised "approximately
       $4.0 billion in cash and new secured notes"; the 10-Q says $622M
       cash. $622M is correct.

WHAT THE LINE HISTORY SAYS
  - Internet, the margin engine, has swung from +1.7% to -3.7% in about
    four quarters. Through FY2025, price/ARPU more than offset subscriber
    losses. In 2026 it doesn't. PF residential ARPU was -1.6%, though
    "essentially flat" excluding the streaming-app programming allocation.
  - Mobile is the only real grower, decelerating from +37% to +17%. It is
    low-margin relative to Internet (MVNO costs plus device subsidies) but
    it lowers churn: management has said Internet+mobile customers churn
    "nearly 40% less."
  - Video is shrinking ~10%/yr but is low-margin: programming is ~65% of
    video revenue. Its decline costs much less EBITDA than its revenue
    suggests, and the PF programming expense fell 10.3% in Q3.
  - Commercial is flat. Cox business is slightly negative.

      Cox's ~$4.9B EBITDA.

SECTION 10 - DISCOUNT RATE (WACC)
================================================================================

Inputs:
  - Risk-free rate: 4.87% (US 10-yr, Nov 6, 2026; tradingeconomics).
  - Equity risk premium: 5.0%.
  - Cost of debt, pre-tax: ~7.1%.
      - Market-implied: 5.3% coupon, 85.3% of par, ~11-yr WAL, gives a
        yield of ~7.1-7.2%.
      - Cross-check: the Aug 2026 secured issues at 6.05-7.85% and the
        exchange notes at 7.09-7.34%. Unsecured CCOH paper is wider.
      - After-tax at 21%: ~5.6%.
  - Asset (unlevered) beta: 0.75, the HIGH end of the ~0.5-0.75 cable/
    telecom range (conservative).

Two methods:
  (1) Unlevered cost of capital minus the debt tax shield:
      Ku = 4.87% + 0.75 x 5.0% = 8.62%
      WACC ~= Ku - t x Kd x D/V = 8.62% - 0.21 x 7.1% x 0.79 = ~7.45%
  (2) Hamada relevering at market weights:
      Weights: E $19.4B (16.3%), debt at FV + SCF $93.8B (78.7%),
      preferred at FV $6.0B (5.0%)
      Levered beta = 0.75 x (1 + 0.79 x 93.8/19.4) = ~3.61
      Ke = 4.87% + 3.61 x 5.0% = ~22.9%
      WACC = 16.3% x 22.9% + 78.7% x 5.6% + 5.0% x ~7.0% = ~8.50%
      (Hamada with zero debt beta overstates at this leverage.)
  Range: ~7.45% to ~8.50%.

USED: Base 8.0% | Bull 7.5% | Bear 9.0%
  Base at 8.0% is near the middle of the range, not the top. I did not go
  to 8.5% because the Base already stacks several conservative calls:
    - Internet never returns to growth;
    - only 50% of the $2B program;
    - capex above guidance;
    - debt at principal;
    - supply-chain finance treated as debt.
  8.5% gives ~$125 (-$37). The Bear's 9.0% is ABOVE the range, reflecting
  distress risk if EBITDA keeps falling at 3.9x leverage.

Terminal growth (nominal): Base 0.0% (~-2.5% real) | Bull +0.5% |
Bear -1.0%.
Exit EV/EBITDA on 2036 EBITDA: Base 5.0x | Bull 6.0x | Bear 4.0x.
  - Comcast ~4.7x trailing / ~5.0x forward (Oct 2026); CHTR today ~5.0x.
  - Cox was signed at 6.44x transaction EBITDA in 2025.
  - Base assumes NO re-rating from today's depressed cable multiple.

Consistency checks (Base):
  - The perpetuity TV implies 5.84x 2036 EBITDA.
  - The 5.0x exit TV implies a -1.22% perpetual FCF growth rate.
  - The two methods bracket each other reasonably; their average is used.

SECTION 17 - KEY RISKS (RANKED)
================================================================================

 1. Broadband share loss to FWA and fiber does not slow. This is the
    dominant risk. The Base needs Internet decline to ease from -3.7% to
    ~-2% by 2029; the price needs ~-2.4%/yr forever; -3.7% forever is
    ~$54/share.
 2. Leverage plus a shrinking EBITDA base. At 3.9x, a ~4%/yr EBITDA
    decline (the Bear) leaves the equity under water. Refinancing the
    $16.6B 2030 wall at 6-8% vs ~4.3-6.2% coupons raises interest expense.
 3. The cost program is reinvested or competed away. $3B+ of savings
    announced in a quarter of accelerating losses invites the question of
    how much must go back into price.
 4. Cox integration:
      - higher churn (~10% above Charter);
      - -4.5% revenue trend;
      - customer-service onshoring over 18 months;
      - brand/pricing migration (Spectrum pricing launched Sep 16, only
        ~6 weeks of data);
      - the parent is renaming itself Cox Communications within ~1 year.
 5. Mobile economics and the Verizon MVNO. Mobile is the growth engine,
    but it runs on Verizon's network (a competitor in FWA/fiber) with CBRS
    offload. Mobile line adds are decelerating (legacy Charter +440K vs
    +482K).
 6. Video/programming. Programming is ~65% of video revenue, and
    streaming-app bundling is shifting cost into the revenue line ($286M
    netted in Q3).
 7. Governance and capital-allocation conflicts:
      - Cox (~21% of common units plus the preferred) and A/N (~9%) have
        both suspended pro-rata sell-downs.
      - Cox has a Class C voting share and the Chairmanship.
      - The TRA pays them 50% of exchange tax benefits.
 8. Credit/rates. Debt at 85% of par signals spread risk. A downgrade
    would raise the marginal cost of debt and the WACC.
 9. Delinquency rose: 60+ days 101.8K at 9/30/26 vs 87.1K a year earlier.
    The 9/30/26 figure includes Cox, so it is not like-for-like, but the
    direction bears watching (lower-income sales weakness was cited).
10. Upside risks: FWA capacity limits, a successful go-to-market reset,
    full $3B savings, capex below $9.5B, or debt bought back well below
    par. Any combination moves value toward the Bull quickly because of
    leverage.
