WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
-------------------------------
This values ONLY Tesla's Robotaxi ride-hailing business, treated as if it were a
separate company ("RobotaxiCo") rather than a Tesla division. The request's
modeling instruction is: "it would have to buy vehicles from the auto division
at market rates." That one instruction changes four things relative to the
September "inside Tesla" segment DCF in tech-buffett/personal/:

  1. VEHICLES AT MARKET PRICE, NOT TESLA'S BUILD COST. The September file
     capitalized each Cybercab at $20,000, a deliberately aggressive internal
     cost. An arm's-length buyer pays Tesla Auto's price, which includes Auto's
     gross margin. Anchor: Musk's own "$30,000 or less" Cybercab price
     target. Base path is $35k (2026 early-production mix) to $30k (2027-28) to
     $27k (2032+); see Section 4.
  2. THE AUTONOMY SOFTWARE HAS TO BE LICENSED. The instruction says "buy
     vehicles at market rates"; it does not say RobotaxiCo owns Tesla's
     FSD/AI stack. A ride-hail operator that buys its cars from Tesla Auto is
     in the position Uber or Lyft would be in, so it has to license the
     driverless software from whoever owns it. This file's judgment call is
     that Tesla (Auto/AI) keeps the stack, because the same software ships in
     every consumer Tesla as FSD, and RobotaxiCo pays a market-rate license.
     This turns out to be the BIGGEST single lever in the model (Section 7B).
     The alternative, where RobotaxiCo owns the stack and bears all of
     Tesla's AI R&D and training compute, is discussed in Section 3. It is not
     obviously more favorable, because RobotaxiCo would then carry billions a
     year of AI capex and R&D that Tesla currently spreads across its whole
     FSD-capable consumer fleet.
  3. NO CAPTIVE INSURANCE PRICING. The cost-per-mile file prices insurance at
     Tesla's captive loss cost. A standalone fleet operator either buys
     commercial cover (with a ~25-35% expense/profit load) or self-insures
     without Tesla's balance sheet behind it. A load is added.
  4. ITS OWN COST OF CAPITAL. RobotaxiCo is a pre-scale, cash-burning
     business with no Auto/Energy cash flows to fund the ramp. It needs
     ~$14-28B of external funding in Base/Bull (Section 5) and is discounted
     at a standalone rate (Base 12.0%), not Tesla's blended rate.

There is no RobotaxiCo balance sheet, share count, or market price. Headline
outputs are ENTERPRISE VALUE in dollars, translated into "$ per TSLA share" only
as a scale reference. This is NOT a TSLA price target: it excludes Auto,
Energy, Optimus, FSD consumer subscriptions, AND the license income Tesla would
EARN from RobotaxiCo under this structure (see Section 7B: under independence
most of the value moves into Tesla's other segments rather than disappearing).

Three findings, stated plainly:
  (a) As a standalone company, Robotaxi in the Base case is worth roughly HALF
      the ~$342B the September "inside Tesla" segment DCF found. About two-
      thirds of the gap is the autonomy license, i.e. value that moves to Tesla's
      AI/Auto side rather than disappearing. The other third is the
      higher standalone WACC, the vehicle markup, and the insurance load.
  (b) The probability-weighted value is ~81% driven by the Bull case. The Base
      case alone covers only ~12% of TSLA's market cap. To justify even HALF
      of TSLA's market cap from robotaxi alone (Section 8), the Base fleet would
      need to be ~4x larger (~21M cars by 2036) or fares ~$0.40/mi higher every
      year. Both are well outside the Base case.
  (c) The standalone Bear case is not "small"; it is value-DESTROYING
      (-$33.5B). Once the vehicle is priced at market and software is licensed,
      a sub-scale Cybertruck-like outcome never reaches positive EBITDA. A
      real standalone RobotaxiCo would shut down long before 2036, so the
      "abandon at $0" floor is shown alongside (Section 7).

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT: WHAT'S BEING VALUED
================================================================================

RobotaxiCo = Tesla's driverless ride-hail network. It owns and operates the
fleet, runs the Robotaxi app, dispatch, depots, charging, cleaning, teleop,
field response, rider support, permits and insurance, and collects fares.
It BUYS from affiliated or unaffiliated parties:
  - vehicles (Cybercab, plus the small legacy robotaxi-spec Model Y fleet)
    from Tesla Auto, at market price
  - the unsupervised driving software, as a license from Tesla
  - insurance cover, at commercial or standalone self-insured cost

Current state (as of 2026-10-12, see extract Section 4):
  - Driverless public service in 6 metros (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami,
    Orlando, Tampa); SF Bay Area runs with safety drivers; Phoenix staged;
    Nevada approval for up to 5,000 AVs (Aug 2026).
  - Texas fleet: 739 vehicles = 420 Model Y (held flat) + 319 Cybercabs
    registered. Tesla's Oct 10 claim: "300+ Cybercabs serving Austin, an 8x
    increase in 30 days." Cybercab line capacity at Giga Texas: >125k/yr.
  - ~2.4M cumulative PAID miles through 2026-06-30 (~0.9M in Q2 alone).
  - Austin fare: $3.00 base + $1.40/mi (raised from $1.00/mi in March 2026).

Revenue today is immaterial: ~0.9M paid miles in Q2 at roughly $1.80-2.00 per
paid mile (base fee spread over a ~4-5 mile trip) comes to ~$2M per quarter.
Everything in this valuation is the ramp.

SECTION 3 — STRATEGIC CONTEXT: WHAT "MARKET RATE" MEANS HERE
================================================================================

3A. THE VEHICLE
Tesla has never sold a Cybercab to a third party, so there is no observed
market price. The anchors:
  - Musk: Cybercab at "$30,000 or less," before 2027 (stated as a consumer
    price target, not a fleet price).
  - The cost-per-mile file estimated Tesla's internal build cost at ~$25-28k at
    scale. The September segment DCF used $20k, explicitly labeled as a
    "meaningfully cheaper than estimated" case.
  - Tesla's automotive gross margin is ~16-17%, so a price that covers
    Auto's cost plus its normal margin is cost / 0.83.
  - A ~$27k long-run Base price therefore implies a ~$22.4k Tesla cost, which is
    already BELOW the file's own $25-28k build-cost estimate. A higher price
    would be the more conservative call, but $27k is held because volume
    discounts to the largest fleet buyer are realistic. Bull goes to $25k; Bear
    holds ~$31k (a sub-scale line never gets cheaper, the Cybertruck pattern).
  - Early units carry low-volume pricing: Base $35k in 2026, falling to $30k
    by 2027 in line with Musk's target date.

3B. THE AUTONOMY LICENSE: THE CENTRAL JUDGMENT CALL
There is no market price for unsupervised robotaxi software either. The
reference points are:
  - Consumer FSD (Supervised): $99/month, subscription-only since Feb 2026.
    This does NOT remove the driver, so it is a floor, not a comparable.
    There are unconfirmed reports of a $199/mo unsupervised tier.
  - Ride-hail networks keep ~25-30% of gross bookings for demand plus
    network. Tesla's own floated "owners add their cars to the network" model
    reportedly keeps a cut in that range for software, network and demand
    together.
  - A software-only license to an operator that brings its OWN network,
    demand and fleet ops is worth well under that bundle, but the licensor has
    pricing power: the software is the only reason the car needs no driver.
    The driver it replaces is the single largest cost in human ride-hail,
    typically more than half of the fare.
  - BASE: 10% of gross fare revenue. BULL: 8%. BEAR: 12% (a licensor
    facing a weak operator prices for value, not volume).
  - CONSERVATIVE-CALL DISCLOSURE: a flat $200/vehicle/month license
    ($2,400/yr, the reported unsupervised consumer tier) would cost about
    half as much at scale ($12.5B vs $25.9B in 2036) and RAISES Base EV by
    +$55.8B, to ~$227B. 10% was chosen because a licensor of a
    driver-replacing product would not price it like a consumer
    subscription. A reader who thinks Tesla would license cheaply to grow
    volume should use the ~$227B figure.
  - The ALTERNATIVE STRUCTURE (RobotaxiCo owns the stack) was not used: the
    software is inseparable from consumer FSD, and a stack owner would carry
    Tesla's full AI training-compute capex and autonomy R&D. Tesla's total R&D
    is now ~$9.5B/yr annualized and its capex ~$23B/yr annualized. That would
    swamp the ride-hail P&L for years.

3C. WHAT INDEPENDENCE DOES NOT CHANGE
Fleet size, production ramp, utilization, fares and the operating cost per
mile (energy, maintenance, cleaning, depots, teleop, field ops, support,
payment processing) are carried over unchanged from the tech-buffett/personal
scenario files. Those costs belong to the operator whether or not it is inside
Tesla. They were NOT re-derived here, and their caveats carry over, including
that the fare path is BELOW today's observed Austin pricing (see 4D).

FARE CONSERVATISM NOTE: today's Austin pricing ($3 + $1.40/mi, roughly $1.10-
1.20 per total vehicle-mile after ~40% deadhead) is ABOVE the model's 2026-27
fares. The model assumes fares fall as Tesla scales and competes with Waymo
and Zoox. Table E shows every +$0.05/mi of sustained fare is worth ~+$71B of
Base EV.

4E. TAX: 15% flat on positive EBIT only. No benefit in loss years and no NOL
carryforward (house convention). An NOL shield would modestly raise Base,
since cumulative pre-2030 losses are ~$3.4B.

4F. VEHICLE D&A AND CAPEX (rebuilt on market price)
  Vehicle capex = Produced x market price (same year).
  D&A = straight-line over 4 years to 10% residual, half-year convention
        (half in the production year, full years 1-3, half in year 4 when
        retired), matching the fleet file's 4-year retirement.
  Salvage = 10% x original price on retired units (cash inflow).
  Infrastructure capex = $4,000 per net-add vehicle (unchanged).
  This fixes a minor inconsistency in the Sept file, which depreciated over
  ~4.5 years while retiring at 4.

4G. FREE CASH FLOW
  UFCF = NOPAT + D&A − vehicle capex − infra capex + salvage
  SBC-adj FCF = UFCF − SBC   ← THIS is what is discounted (house convention).
  The R&D, SG&A and opex lines above are cash costs EXCLUDING SBC; SBC is
  subtracted once, separately.

WACC rationale: Tesla's own WACC is roughly 9-10% (high beta, no net debt).
A standalone pre-scale RobotaxiCo has no Auto/Energy cash flow behind it, a
~$28B Base funding hole, and binary regulatory and safety-case risk. That
earns a venture-to-growth-equity-like premium. The Sept file used 10% Base;
using 10% here would add +$56.1B to Base EV (conservative call, disclosed).

Exit multiple rationale: by 2036 RobotaxiCo is an asset-heavy fleet operator
that replaces ~25% of its fleet every year. Base maintenance D&A is ~40% of
EBITDA, and normalized FCF is ~40% of EBITDA. At 12% WACC and 3% growth the
PERPETUITY method implies only ~4.6x EBITDA, while a still-growing (~29% revenue
growth in 2036) network deserves more. 10x splits the difference toward the
conservative side. 12x (the first draft's figure) would add +$25.0B; it was
cut because it implied ~30x normalized FCF for a 3%-perpetual-growth business.

TERMINAL FCF NORMALIZATION (methodology call, disclosed): 2036 FCF is still
depressed by GROWTH capex (Base produces 2.0M cars to retire 0.65M). Applying
3% perpetual growth to that figure would assume the growth capex continues
forever while growth stops. The perpetuity method therefore uses a
NORMALIZED terminal FCF: 2036 EBITDA, less steady-state D&A (avg fleet x price
x 90% / 4), less tax, less SBC, less capex = replacement + 3% fleet growth.
Base: $31.8B normalized vs $10.8B as-reported. Using the un-normalized figure
would cut Base EV to $133.5B. That version is internally inconsistent, so it
is shown only as the bound.

PROBABILITY WEIGHTS: 35% Bear / 45% Base / 20% Bull. These are the same
weights as the Sept file. Bear gets 35% because Cybercab is ~4 weeks into
public service in one city, the vehicle has no steering wheel (regulatory
exposure), and every Tesla volume target to date has slipped 2-5 years.

AI/Auto side. Independence reallocates value between Tesla's divisions; it
mostly does not shrink the consolidated pie. Only the insurance load (a real
third-party cost or capital charge) and the higher standalone cost of capital
are true leakage. So:
  - "What is the Robotaxi BUSINESS worth on its own?"   ~$171B (this file)
  - "What does robotaxi add to TSLA, consolidated?"   ~$308B at 12% WACC;

(car plus software) rather than as the OPERATOR. In this model the license
alone is worth ~$107B, about 62% as much as the entire standalone operator
($171B). Add the vehicle margin (~$22B) and the supplier side captures ~$129B
against the operator's $171B, i.e. ~43% of the combined value.

subtraction of SBC, and $22.4k (cost/0.83) vehicles vs $20k.

SECTION 8 — REVERSE DCF: WHAT WOULD ROBOTAXI HAVE TO BE TO CARRY TSLA?
================================================================================

The bull narrative for TSLA is that robotaxi is most of the company's value.
TSLA market cap is ~$1.48T at $375.23. Holding all Base inputs fixed (12%
WACC, 10% license, market-price cars), this section solves for the single
change needed to reach each target EV:

Target EV         Fare uplift needed (all yrs)    OR    Fleet scale needed (2036 avg)
  $250B           +$0.06/mi (2036 fare $0.72)             1.4x Base  (7.4M cars)
  $500B           +$0.23/mi (2036 fare $0.89)             2.7x Base (14.1M cars)
  $741B (½ mkt cap) +$0.40/mi (2036 fare $1.06)           4.0x Base (20.6M cars)
  $1,482B (=mkt cap) +$0.93/mi (2036 fare $1.58)          7.8x Base (40.7M cars)

(The fleet-scale solve holds R&D/SG&A fixed, so it is slightly generous.)

Plain conclusion: if robotaxi is to justify the whole of TSLA's current
market cap as a STANDALONE operator, it needs either ~41M cars on the road by
2036, about 3.5x the Bull case (which already assumes Musk's 4M/yr stretch
target is fully met), or 2036 fares of ~$1.58 per vehicle-mile, ~35-45%
ABOVE Tesla's own current Austin pricing (~$1.10-1.20 per vehicle-mile)
after ten years of competition. Neither is plausible.
The bull thesis for TSLA only works if Tesla ALSO captures the supplier
economics (the vehicle margin and especially the software license) at scale.
That would include licensing to OTHER operators and to consumer FSD, which
is a different and larger valuation than this file's.

SECTION 9 — KEY RISKS (SPECIFIC TO THIS ANALYSIS)
================================================================================

1. LICENSE TERMS ARE INVENTED. No market exists for unsupervised robotaxi
   software. 10% of revenue is a reasoned judgment (Sec 3B), and Table D
   shows the range. This is the largest single uncertainty specific to the
   standalone framing.
2. FARE PATH. Table E: ±$0.10/mi ≈ ±$140B. The model already sits below
   today's Austin pricing, but competition (Waymo ~4,000 cars, Zoox, and
   Uber/Lyft AV partnerships) could push fares lower faster.
3. REGULATORY / SAFETY-CASE RISK for a vehicle without controls. One serious
   incident can freeze deployment nationally (cf. Cruise, Oct 2023). This
   risk is inherited from the fleet file and drives the 35% Bear weight.
4. FLEET RAMP DEPENDS ON MUSK TARGETS. Base reaches 2M/yr production by 2036
   and Bull 4M. Every prior Tesla volume target has slipped (Model 3,
   Cybertruck, Semi). The Q1 2026 "mass production in 2026" language was
   dropped from the Q2 deck.
5. UTILIZATION. Base assumes 75k mi/vehicle/yr at maturity, roughly 2x Waymo's
   current ~30-35k. This was not re-derived here.
6. FUNDING. A standalone RobotaxiCo needs ~$28B (Base) to ~$89B (Bear) of
   external capital before it self-funds. Access and dilution risk are only
   implicit in the WACC.
7. INSURANCE TAIL. A nuclear-verdict year lands on a standalone balance sheet
   with no Tesla backstop.
8. TERMINAL DEPENDENCE. TV is 106% of Base EV, and the two TV methods differ
   by 2.3x. The headline is a midpoint of two very different worldviews.

SECTION 10 — ASSESSMENT / WHAT THIS MEANS
================================================================================

- As an independent company paying market prices for its cars and its brain,
  Tesla's robotaxi network is worth ~$171B in the Base case: a real, large
  business (~$259B revenue and ~$80B EBITDA by 2036) whose economics look like
  a capital-intensive fleet operator, not a software company. It burns ~$28B
  before turning cash-positive in 2035.
- The probability-weighted $348B is mostly the Bull case (81% of the
  weighted value). Read it as "Base, plus a 20% chance of something
  enormous," not as a central estimate.
- The most important insight from the carve-out: IN DRIVERLESS RIDE-HAIL,
  THE SUPPLIER SIDE (SOFTWARE PLUS CAR) CAPTURES NEARLY AS MUCH VALUE AS THE
  OPERATOR. At a 10% license, ~$107B of Base value goes to the software
  owner and ~$22B to the vehicle maker, against $171B for the operator. At a
  15-20% license (Table D) the software owner would capture MORE than the
  operator. Investors valuing TSLA on "robotaxi" are valuing Tesla as an
  autonomy supplier as much as a network operator.
- Relative to TSLA's ~$1.48T market cap, robotaxi as a standalone operator
  covers 12% (Base) to 24% (prob-weighted). The rest of the market cap has
  to come from Auto, Energy, FSD software, Optimus, and Tesla's supplier
  economics on robotaxi itself.
