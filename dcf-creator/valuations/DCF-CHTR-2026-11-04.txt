WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
------------------------------
This values Charter as it exists TODAY:
  - Cox Communications combined in (closed Aug 19, 2026);
  - Liberty Broadband folded in (closed concurrently);
  - Q3 2026, the first quarter with Cox in the numbers, NOT yet reported.
    It is due Friday Nov 6, two days after this file. Q2 2026 is the
    latest reported quarter.

Three things dominate the answer, in this order:
  1. LEVERAGE. Senior claims ahead of the common are ~$115.6B (net debt
     ~$109.4B est. plus $6.2B of preferred). The equity is ~$19.8B at
     $120.25, about 15% of enterprise value. Every 1% change in EV moves
     the equity ~7%. Every 0.1x of EV/EBITDA is worth ~$17/share. This
     equity is effectively a long-dated option on the cable EV.
  2. THE CAPEX ROLL-OFF vs. THE BROADBAND BLEED. Charter's standalone
     capex is ~$11.4B in 2026 and is guided to "below $8 billion per year"
     once network evolution and the subsidized rural build finish (~2028).
     That is ~$3.5B+/yr of FCF, or ~$20+/share. Against it:
       - Internet customers are shrinking faster each quarter (-117K Q2'25
         -> -172K Q2'26);
       - residential Internet revenue has turned negative YoY (-3.2% Q2'26);
       - 2026 EBITDA guidance was CUT to ~-1%.
     The whole valuation is a race between these two.
  3. THE TERMINAL DEBATE. Is cable broadband a slowly-shrinking utility
     worth ~5x EBITDA, which is where Comcast and Charter trade, or a
     melting ice cube? The market is pricing something close to the
     latter (Section 12).

Charter has ONE reportable segment and discloses no segment profit.
Section 3 therefore presents the revenue-by-product-line, customer, and
capex history that Charter does disclose, plus Cox as an acquired
component. That history drives the forecast.

HEADLINE FINDING: $120.25 sits well BELOW the Base case and only ~1/3 of
the way from the Bear case ($0) to the Base case. The reverse DCF
(Section 12) shows the price is consistent with ANY ONE of:
  - revenue declining ~3.4% in 2027, easing only to a PERMANENT ~-1.2%/yr
    decline (2036 revenue ~$54B vs ~$66B PF today), with Base margins
    and capex;
  - EBITDA margins ~2.9 points below Base in every year (~38.4%
    steady-state vs 41.3%);
  - a terminal exit multiple of ~4.2x (vs 5.0x Base);
  - a WACC of ~9.6% (vs 8.25%).
Most tellingly, today's EV equals the 2027 Base SBC-adjusted FCF
(~$10.7B, a heavy-capex year) capitalized at 8.25% with ~0% growth. The
market is pricing as if the capex roll-off never shows up as higher FCF,
because EBITDA erosion eats all of it.

That is a coherent bear thesis, not an irrational one:
  - AT&T added 442K fiber + 327K FWA in Q3'26 (a record);
  - Verizon added 314K; Comcast lost 144K (worse YoY);
  - Charter's own losses accelerated through Q2'26.
But it requires the deterioration to continue rather than stabilize.
The Base case here is ALREADY conservative versus management:
  - Charter standalone capex settles at ~$8.0-8.2B, not "below $8B", and
    a year later;
  - synergies are $800M, not the $1B management floated;
  - underlying margins erode 1.6 points before synergies;
  - the 15%-tax house convention cuts the other way (Section 14).

THE CATCH, STATED PLAINLY: with ~85% of EV owed to creditors, a modest
miss is enough to push the equity toward zero. The Bear case is not
exotic:
  - revenue -3%/yr for two years, then -1.5% to -2.5%;
  - margin to 38%;
  - 4.0x exit, below where Comcast trades today (~4.7x);
  - 9.25% WACC.
It produces an EV of ~$103B against ~$116B of claims. The bear weight is
therefore a real 35%. The probability-weighted value still roughly
doubles the price because the payoff is convex: the downside is capped
at $0 and the upside is levered.

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT AND MATERIAL CORPORATE EVENTS
================================================================================

Charter (Spectrum) is the largest US cable operator after the Cox
combination. It is a hybrid fiber-coax network passing ~71M locations
(58.98M Charter at 6/30/26 + ~12.3M Cox). It sells:
  - residential broadband, video, voice;
  - Spectrum Mobile (an MVNO on Verizon's network, with Wi-Fi/CBRS
    offload of ~87% of traffic);
  - SMB and mid-market/enterprise connectivity;
  - local advertising.

Charter standalone, FY2025:
  Revenue $54,774M; Adj. EBITDA $22,708M (41.5%); capex $11,659M;
  FCF $5,004M; 29.68M Internet customers; 11.77M mobile lines.

Cox Communications, FY2025 standalone:
  6ex-D&A, 39.8%); ~6.3M customer relationships; ~12.3M passings.
  Q1 2026: revenue $3,059M (-3.9%), EBITDA ~$1,319M (43.1%).

CORPORATE EVENTS THAT MATTER FOR THE MODEL (all closed as of today):
  1. COX COMBINATION, closed Aug 19, 2026. Charter paid ~$4.15B cash
     ($3.5B + $0.65B) and issued:
       - ~33.6M Charter Holdings common units, exchangeable 1:1 into
         Class A;
       - $6.0B of 6.875% convertible preferred units (conversion $477.41,
         deep out of the money);
       - one Class C voting share.
     It assumed ~$12.4B of Cox net debt and finance leases. Cox
     Enterprises owns ~26% fully diluted (as-converted). Management's
     current synergy target is "at least $800 million" run-rate, possibly
     $1B (the original was $500M). Spectrum pricing launched in Cox
     markets mid-Sep. The parent will be renamed Cox Communications
     within ~1 year.
  2. LIBERTY BROADBAND COMBINATION, closed concurrently.
       - Net -4.7M Charter shares (38.6M retired, 33.9M issued);
       - ~$840M of LBRD net debt assumed;
       - $180M of LBRD preferred became Charter preferred.
  3. LEVERAGE TARGET cut to "a flat 3.5 times" within three years of
     close ("not aspirational"). It was 4.18x at 6/30; expected "just
     above 3.9x" at end Q3.
  4. BUYBACKS paused through Q3; "expect share repurchases to restart in
     the fourth quarter."
  5. GUIDANCE CUT (Jul 24): 2026 standalone EBITDA ~-1% (was "slight
     growth"). Capex ~$11.4B (reaffirmed).
  6. GOVERNANCE: Alex Taylor (Cox) is Chairman; Chris Winfrey remains CEO.

WHY NO MULTIPLES/SOTP COMPANION: Charter and Cox are the same business
(HFC cable broadband + MVNO mobile + SMB/enterprise) in different
geographies. Charter reports one segment. Mobile is a bundled retention
tool riding on the broadband network, not a separable asset with its own
cost base. A sum-of-the-parts would invent allocations Charter does not
disclose. Sector multiples (Comcast ~4.7-5.2x EV/EBITDA) are used as a
cross-check on the exit multiple instead. If you want a comps
cross-check (CMCSA, CABO, ATUS, plus telco fiber), that can be built
separately on request.

WHAT THE SEGMENT-EQUIVALENT HISTORY TELLS YOU GOING INTO THE FORECAST
  1. The profit pool is Internet: 43% of revenue, and most of the
     incremental margin, since video carries heavy programming costs and
     mobile is an MVNO paying Verizon. Internet REVENUE has now joined
     Internet SUBSCRIBERS in decline (-3.2% Q2'26), partly from retention
     offers that management says normalize in Q3. Penetration of passings
     fell from 55% (FY21-23) to 49.8%. The rural build is adding passings
     faster than customers.
  2. Losses are accelerating, not stabilizing. Same-quarter comparisons
     (Q2'25 -117K -> Q2'26 -172K; Q1'25 -59K -> Q1'26 -120K) are the
     cleanest read. Management attributes it to softer gross adds, with
     churn "essentially flat", meaning share of new movers lost to FWA
     and fiber. The FY24 loss was distorted by the ACP end; FY25-26 is
     not.
  3. Mobile is decelerating: the 545K/quarter peak in Q3'24 fell to
     368-406K in 2026. Mobile service revenue still grows ~19%. But
     mobile is ~7% of revenue on an MVNO cost structure. It is a churn
     reducer (mobile+Internet customers churn "nearly 40% less"), not a
     second profit engine big enough to offset Internet.
  4. Video is in managed decline (-9 to -10%/yr) and still 25% of revenue.
     Its EBITDA contribution is much smaller than its revenue share.
     Video customer losses improved sharply (-294K Q3'24 -> -21K Q2'26).
     The cause (e.g. streaming-app bundling) was not re-verified in this
     pull. Video revenue still falls ~10%.
  5. Commercial (SMB + enterprise, 13% of revenue) is roughly flat to
     slightly up, the steadiest piece.
  6. Implication for the model: there is no separable high-margin
     segment to value separately, and no segment profit disclosure to
     build one. The model forecasts consolidated revenue and EBITDA
     margin, but the revenue path is built from the line-level drivers
     above (Section 5).

SECTION 4 — COMPETITIVE CONTEXT: FIXED WIRELESS AND FIBER
================================================================================

Q3 2026 peer prints (already reported):
  AT&T     +769K internet (442K fiber + 327K FWA, a record)
  Verizon  +314K broadband (155K fiber + 159K FWA)
  Comcast  -144K broadband (vs -91K in Q3'25); +368K wireless lines
  T-Mobile Q3'26 FWA not retrieved (T-Mobile changed disclosure formats)

Charter's own read (Q2'26 call):
  - "expanded fixed-wireless competition versus a year ago";
  - "fiber overlap growth at a rate similar to prior quarters";
  - no "meaningful share loss to Starlink";
  - "Internet customer growth is taking longer to reverse."

Telco broadband net adds of ~1M+/quarter (AT&T + Verizon alone ~1.08M in
Q3'26, plus T-Mobile) against a roughly flat US broadband household base
must come from cable. Cable's structural defenses:
  (a) FWA is capacity-constrained per sector and is being sold as a
      cheap, lower-performance tier;
  (b) DOCSIS 4.0/high-split upgrades close the symmetric-speed gap with
      fiber;
  (c) mobile bundling ("converged" pricing) lowers churn.
Those defenses have not yet shown up in Charter's numbers. This model
assumes they partially work in Base (losses moderate after 2027), work
well in Bull, and fail in Bear.

SECTION 12 — REVERSE DCF: WHAT IS $120.25 PRICING?
================================================================================

Market EV at $120.25 = 164.5M x $120.25 + $115.58B claims = ~$135.4B
                     = ~4.93x 2026 PF EBITDA.

Holding everything else at Base, the price is reached by ANY ONE of:
  (1) REVENUE: shift the Base growth path down ~1.68 points every year:
      -3.4% (2027), -2.9%, -2.7%, -2.2%, -1.9%, -1.7%, then -1.2%
      FOREVER through 2036. 2036 revenue ~$54.0B (vs Base $64.1B; PF
      2026 $66.0B), EBITDA ~$22.4B.
      This is close to the Bear revenue path, with Base margins kept.
  (2) MARGIN: Base all-in EBITDA margin ~2.85 points lower in every year
      (~38.4% steady-state vs 41.3%). That is roughly Charter's
      2019-2020 margin level, with no synergy benefit.
  (3) EXIT MULTIPLE (exit method alone): ~4.17x 2036 EBITDA, vs Comcast
      ~4.7x today.
  (4) PERPETUITY (PG method alone): ~-2.9% perpetual nominal decline
      from 2036.
  (5) DISCOUNT RATE: WACC ~9.6% (vs 8.25%).
  (6) SIMPLEST FRAMING: $135.4B = 2027E Base SBC-adjusted FCF ($10.7B)
      / (8.25% - ~0.4%). The market capitalizes Charter's HEAVY-CAPEX-year
      FCF as roughly flat forever. It gives no credit for the ~$2B+ capex
      roll-off, because it expects EBITDA to shrink by at least as much.

Levered cross-check:
  - Base 2028 SBC-adjusted unlevered FCF $11.9B;
  - less ~$5.0B after-tax interest (~$5.9B pre-tax on ~$109B);
  - less $0.41B preferred dividends;
  - = ~$6.5B to the common, ~$39/share, a ~33% FCF yield on $120.25.
  - 2029E is ~$44/share.
  - Management's "nearly 50%" 2028 FCF yield comment (made at a lower
    price, on a different basis) is directionally consistent.
  - A 33% equity FCF yield is what a market charges when it doubts the
    FCF will last.

CONCLUSION: the market is pricing something close to the Bear revenue
trajectory, or a persistent ~3-point margin squeeze. That is a
defensible fear given four straight quarters of worsening broadband
losses and a guidance cut. But the price does not require anything
WORSE than the Bear case. It sits ~1/3 of the way from Bear ($0) to
Base ($224). If losses merely stabilize around the 2025 run-rate
(~-400K/yr) and the capex roll-off arrives roughly on management's
schedule, the price is too low. If the 2026 acceleration continues for
two more years, the price is fair to generous.

SECTION 15 — KEY RISKS (TWO-SIDED)
================================================================================

DOWNSIDE
  1. Broadband share loss accelerates further. FWA capacity keeps
     expanding (mid-band spectrum, cheaper CPE). AT&T, Verizon and
     T-Mobile fiber footprints are growing via builds and acquisitions,
     and overbuild overlap grows each quarter. The Bear case is the
     2026 trend extrapolated, not a tail.
  2. Pricing/ARPU compression. Defending share with retention offers
     already pushed Internet revenue negative. A price war in the
     converged bundle hits the highest-margin line.
  3. Leverage and refinancing:
       - ~$116B of senior claims;
       - bonds trading ~89% of par (market yields ~6.5-7%+ vs older
         coupons);
       - the 3.5x target consumes nearly all Base FCF through 2029.
     Rolling cheap debt into a ~7% market raises interest by hundreds
     of millions a year. That is not in the unlevered DCF, but it
     reduces equity FCF and buyback capacity. The debt maturity ladder
     was not retrieved (gap).
  4. Cox integration. Cox's revenue is shrinking ~4%/yr, faster than
     Charter's, and its customers are moving to Spectrum pricing (a
     possible near-term ARPU hit) during a rebrand and system
     migrations. Synergy delivery is unproven.
  5. Capex overrun. Network evolution timing slips, or Cox's plant needs
     a full upgrade on top. "Below $8B" has not been demonstrated.
  6. Cash taxes normalize. 2026 cash taxes are only $500-800M. The model
     uses 15% (Section 14, item 13).
  7. Governance and overhang. Cox Enterprises (~26% fully diluted) holds
     the Chair. Cox and A/N can exchange units into Class A, creating
     potential selling pressure. The convertible preferred ranks ahead
     of the common at 6.875%.
  8. Video cost/carriage disputes. MVNO terms with Verizon (mobile
     economics depend on them). Shareholder-litigation noise around the
     LBRD merger (a plaintiff-firm "investigation" only, so far).

UPSIDE
  1. Losses stabilize as FWA capacity limits bind and DOCSIS 4.0 /
     high-split speeds land. The 2025 run-rate (~-400K/yr) would already
     be consistent with Base or better.
  2. The capex roll-off is real and near. Line extensions are already
     -19% YoY. $3B+ of annual capex is physically close to ending, as the
     subsidized rural build obligations and network evolution wind down.
  3. Buybacks at $120 are highly accretive if IV is anywhere near Base:
     $1B retires ~8.3M shares (~5% of the economic count). They are NOT
     in the per-share values above.
  4. Synergies above $800M (management: "I think it'll grow to $1
     billion"), plus unquantified capex and revenue synergies.
  5. Strategic/industry events. Reuters reports Jio is exploring
     tech-for-equity talks with Comcast and Charter (unconfirmed).
     Further cable consolidation. Any re-rating toward 6x+ is enormous
     for a 15%-of-EV equity.
  6. The Q3 print (Nov 6) could reset the narrative either way, two days
     after this valuation.

SECTION 16 — ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

On these numbers, CHTR at $120.25 trades at a large discount to a Base
case that is already conservative versus management on capex, synergies
and margins. The price sits ~1/3 of the way between a Bear-case $0 and a
Base-case ~$224. Probability-weighted value is ~$205-231. The reverse DCF
shows the market is underwriting something close to the Bear revenue
path: revenue shrinking ~3%/yr near-term and ~1%/yr forever.

But this is NOT a "cheap, safe" stock, and the gap should not be read
that way:
  - The equity is a levered residual (~15% of EV). The distribution of
    outcomes is extremely wide: $0 to $650+ across plausible scenarios.
  - The Bear case is the current trend, not a tail event. The Base case
    REQUIRES broadband losses to moderate from the 2026 pace within
    ~12-18 months. Nothing in the reported data yet shows that
    happening.
  - The one input the house convention gets generous (15% tax) is worth
    ~$55 of Base value.
  - Management's capital allocation record (~$35B of buybacks, mostly
    at several hundred dollars a share, over 2021-2025) argues for skepticism about how the FCF
    ramp gets used.

What would change the view:
  - Q3/Q4 2026 Internet losses back below ~-120K/qtr, with ARPU up
    sequentially as guided: Base gains credibility; shift weight toward
    Bull.
  - 2027 capex guidance near or below $9B standalone, given in Jan 2027:
    the capex leg of Base is confirmed.
  - Losses at or above -170K/qtr through 2027, or another EBITDA guide
    cut: treat Bear as the central case. On that path the equity is
    worth little.

Bottom line: the numbers say UNDERVALUED relative to a conservative Base
case, with an unusually fat left tail. This is a position-sizing problem
(a levered option on cable stabilizing), not a margin-of-safety problem.
Q3 results on Nov 6 are the next real data point, and this file should
be refreshed once they are out.
