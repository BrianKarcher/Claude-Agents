WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
------------------------------
This values Micron at what is very likely the most extreme peak in the
history of the memory industry. FY2026 (ended Sep 3, 2026) produced:
  - revenue of $133.2B, 3.6x FY2025;
  - a GAAP gross margin of 80.7%, and 86.8% in FQ4 alone. The prior record
    was 58.9% for full-year FY2018;
  - GAAP operating income of $99.3B, more than the $52.7B of CUMULATIVE
    operating income for FY2016-FY2025;
  - FCF of $62.3B, 3.3x the cumulative FCF of the previous ten years.
FQ1 FY27 is guided higher again: $61.5B revenue at ~86% gross margin.

The question is therefore not "what is the current run-rate worth." It is
"how long does this last, where do margins settle when it ends, and what do
the intervening years of enormous cash generation add up to?" The model:
  (a) takes the FY2027 anchor from management's guidance;
  (b) models the downcycle explicitly instead of extrapolating the peak.
      Revenue is driven by bits x cost-per-bit x a price premium over
      mid-cycle pricing;
  (c) puts the terminal year at a NORMALIZED, mid-cycle margin anchored to
      Micron's own FY2016-FY2025 through-cycle history, plus an explicit,
      separately flagged allowance for structural change (HBM, Strategic
      Customer Agreements, three-player oligopoly).

Every judgment call that drives the answer is listed in Section 13, with
the dollar effect of making the less conservative choice.

HEADLINE FINDING: $913.75 sits above all three scenarios, including a Bull
case built as a genuine "memory is structurally different now" outcome. In
that Bull case:
  - the shortage runs through FY2029 (FY2028 revenue ~$312B);
  - the downcycle is a soft landing in FY2030-31;
  - margins then settle permanently at ~44% GAAP operating margin, close to
    the FY2018 all-time-peak margin (49.3%), as the new NORMAL.
Even that is worth ~$833.

Per the reverse DCF (Section 11), the price requires one of two things:
  (1) post-cycle pricing settles at ~3.1x mid-cycle levels forever, which
      means a ~66% operating margin in perpetuity; or
  (2) a terminal SBC-adjusted FCF of ~$184B/yr, about 3x what Micron
      generated in FY2026, its best year ever.
In plain terms, the market price only works if the memory cycle has been
abolished and today's shortage economics are the permanent state.

That may be what the market believes, and there are real arguments for it:
  - take-or-pay SCAs covering ~35% of revenue through 2030, with floor
    pricing;
  - $12.9B of customer cash deposits;
  - ~$134B of RPO at minimum prices;
  - HBM's wafer intensity;
  - management saying it has "no line of sight" to supply-demand balance.
But every prior memory upcycle in which Micron raised capex this hard ended
in a downturn within 2-3 years, and the new capacity here has dates on it:
  - Tongluo and ID1: mid-CY2027
  - Hiroshima, ID2 and Singapore NAND: late CY2028
  - New York: CY2030
  - Samsung, SK hynix and CXMT expansions on top of that.

WHERE WE ARE IN THE CYCLE: AT THE PEAK, OR ON THE PEAK PLATEAU.
  - FQ4 FY26 gross margin (86.8%) is ~28 points above any prior annual
    peak.
  - Management calls FQ1 FY27's ~86% the "floor" for FY27, but also
    guides a "more moderate rate of price increases."
  - Capex is roughly doubling again (net $13.8B in FY25, $27.4B in FY26,
    ~$58B FY27E), and construction-weighted.
  - Customer prepayments and long-term agreements are proliferating.
  - DIO rose to 129 days.
Those are classic late-upcycle signatures. The model treats FY2027-FY2028
as the peak plateau and FY2029-FY2030 as the downcycle in the Base case.

The single most important number in this file: ~69% of Base-case
enterprise value comes from FY2027-FY2028 cash flow alone. Micron's value
over the next 24 months is overwhelmingly about how much cash it banks
before the cycle turns, and whether it can return that cash. The CHIPS
buyback restrictions loosen on Dec 9, 2026, and the authorization rises to
$35.16B. The terminal value is small in every scenario except Bull.

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

Micron is one of three global DRAM producers (with Samsung and SK hynix;
China's CXMT is a rising fourth) and a top-five NAND producer. It owns its
fabs, unlike AMD or NVIDIA. That makes it a capital-intensive, cyclical
commodity-plus business, and capex and depreciation dominate the economics.

FY2026 revenue by technology:
  DRAM  $100,679M  (76%)   includes HBM (not separately disclosed)
  NAND   $31,785M  (24%)   data center SSD ~ $10B in FQ4 alone
  Other     $724M  (NOR)

FY2026 revenue by business unit (new structure; Section 3):
  CMBU  Cloud Memory (hyperscale DRAM + ALL HBM)   $43,085M  32.3%
  CDBU  Core Data Center (DC SSD + OEM/ent DRAM)   $37,592M  28.2%
  MCBU  Mobile and Client                          $36,601M  27.5%
  AEBU  Automotive and Embedded                    $15,886M  11.9%
  Data center (CMBU + CDBU)                        $80,677M  60.6%

FQ4 FY26 (14 weeks ended Sep 3, 2026), GAAP:
  - Revenue $54,229M: +31% QoQ, +379% YoY.
  - Gross margin 86.8%; operating income $43,751M (80.7%).
  - Net income $37,701M; diluted EPS $32.87 on 1,147M shares.
  - Non-GAAP EPS $33.42.
  - OCF $43,973M; net capex $10,774M; adjusted FCF $33,199M.

FQ1 FY27 guide:
  - Revenue $61.5B +/- $1.5B.
  - GAAP gross margin ~85.95%; GAAP opex ~$2.31B.
  - GAAP EPS $37.84 +/- $1.00.
  - "Sequential revenue growth each quarter" in FY27.
  - FY27 opex up ~$2.5B; FY27 tax ~15.5%.
  - Net capex ~$25B in H1 FY27, "higher in the second half."

Balance sheet (Sep 3, 2026):
  - Cash + investments $73,453M; debt $5,179M; reported net cash $68,274M.
  - Debt-like items, deducted in the bridge (Section 8):
      SCA customer deposits     $12,895M
      Noncurrent taxes payable   $9,820M
  - Adjusted net cash used in this model: $45,559M.

The thesis in three sentences, both directions:
  BULL: AI made memory the bottleneck. HBM consumes roughly 3x the wafer area
        per bit of standard DRAM (Micron's earlier-call figure, not
        re-verified here), cleanroom space can't be added fast enough,
        and multi-year take-or-pay contracts with floor prices have changed
        the industry's pricing structure for good.
  BEAR: This is the 2017-18 and 2021-22 cycle with the amplitude turned up.
        Every producer is building, the new fabs have dates, AI capex
        digestion will arrive, and an 87% gross margin in a commodity
        manufacturing business is not a steady state.
  THIS MODEL: weights the bear view of the cycle's SHAPE, but gives
        Micron a structural uplift over its history on the post-cycle
        margin (Base) and a large one (Bull).

WHAT THE SEGMENT HISTORY TELLS YOU GOING INTO THE FORECAST
  1. THE BOOM IS NOT JUST HBM. This is the most important segment finding.
     CMBU (where all HBM sits) earned a 76% operating margin in FQ4 FY26.
     MCBU, phones and PCs, the most commoditized end market Micron serves,
     earned 88%, and AEBU earned 79%. As recently as FQ2 FY25, MCBU's
     margin was 1%.
     When the lowest-differentiation BU posts the highest margin, that is
     scarcity pricing across the whole bit supply, not product
     differentiation. The structural-change argument (HBM, SCAs) covers
     part of the business, and the margin is being earned everywhere.
     This is why the model normalizes margins at the consolidated level
     instead of building a high-margin HBM segment forecast. HBM's
     structural premium is the part of the Base case's normalized
     premium over history; the rest of the bit supply is assumed to
     revert.
  2. THE OLD SEGMENTS SHOW THE AMPLITUDE OF REVERSAL.
       CNBU (closest analog to CMBU):  64% (FY18) -> 22% (FY20)
                                       43% (FY22) -> -10% (FY23)
       MBU:                            46% -> 19%; 30% -> -48%
       SBU (closest analog to CDBU's   lost money in 3 of 7 years
            storage half)
     The model assumes nothing like an -48% segment margin, but a 60-80
     point compression in BU margins from peak has happened in this
     company twice in seven years.
  3. AEBU/EBU is the steadiest BU: it held positive margins through FY23.
     It is also the smallest, at about 12% of revenue.
  4. The data-center share went from 26% to 61% in three years. That is a
     real mix shift toward the customers that sign SCAs and buy HBM.
     The Base case's structural premium (Section 7) is effectively a bet
     that this mix shift partially persists through the downcycle.

  - Bear: the downturn begins in FY2028.

4C. NORMALIZATION METHOD
Revenue in every forecast year (except the guided FY2027 anchor) is
    Revenue = FY2025 revenue x bit index x cost-per-bit index x P
where P is the price premium over MID-CYCLE pricing.
  - FY2025 is the mid-cycle reference year. Its gross margin of 39.8% was
    close to the 36.6% through-cycle average, and its operating margin of
    26.1% slightly above the 21.6% average.
  - P = 1.00 means a FY2025-like gross margin on that year's cost
    structure.
  - Cash cost of goods = FY2025 cash cost ($14.15B: revenue less operating
    income, D&A and opex) x bit index x cost index.
  - D&A is modeled separately from the capex schedule (Section 6).
  - Opex is modeled in dollars.
The FY2026 actual sits at P ~ 3.1x mid-cycle, and the FY2027 guide implies
P ~ 4.3x (absolute price per bit ~4.8x the FY2025 level). The NORMALIZED
P used in the terminal years is the key judgment call (Section 7, Table C).

6D. D&A METHOD
D&A is built from the capex history rather than assumed as a % of revenue,
because the FY27-29 capex binge drives the FY30-33 margin squeeze:
  - Equipment share (80% historically; 55% in FY27-28 because those years
    are construction-heavy) is depreciated straight-line over 5 years,
    starting the year after spend.
  - Construction share is depreciated over 25 years, starting 2 years
    after spend.
  - Legacy building depreciation is $1.35B/yr.
  - Calibration: reproduces FY25 ($8.35B) and FY26 ($9.5B) D&A within ~2%.
Base D&A peaks at ~$30B in FY2031, about 41% of that year's revenue.
This is the mechanism that turns a price normalization into near-zero
operating income in FY2030-31, exactly what happened in FY2023, when D&A
was 49.9% of revenue.

MODELING CONVENTIONS (same as the rest of this folder)
  - 10-year explicit forecast, FY2027E-FY2036E. The FY2027E anchor comes
    from guidance (Section 5).
  - Unlevered FCF = NOPAT + D&A - net capex. SBC-adjusted FCF = FCF - SBC.
    SBC-adjusted FCF is what gets discounted.
  - NOPAT = operating income x (1 - 15%) on POSITIVE operating income only.
    Loss years get no tax benefit; NOPAT equals operating income. Micron
    guides 15.5% for FY27 (Pillar Two now applies in Singapore), so 15% is
    0.5pt generous (~$1.6/share).
  - Working capital changes are ignored. This omits a receivables release
    of perhaps $25B in the downcycle (~+$14/share) and a build in FY27
    (~-$5-6/share); net, mildly conservative.
  - End-of-year discounting; no mid-year convention. Mid-year would add
    ~+$14/share to the Base case.
  - Terminal value: perpetuity growth and exit multiple (on SBC-adjusted
    FCF), averaged. FY2036 is constructed as a MID-CYCLE year in every
    scenario, never a peak or trough, so both terminal methods sit on
    normalized cash flow.
  - Shares: 1,150M diluted, Micron's FQ1 FY27 guidance basis (1,147M GAAP
    diluted in FQ4; 1,131.4M basic as of Oct 2, 2026). Held flat; SBC is
    charged as a cost. The $35.16B buyback authorization is not modeled.
    Buybacks at intrinsic value are value-neutral; above it, they destroy
    value.

SECTION 11 — REVERSE DCF: WHAT IS $913.75 PRICING?
================================================================================

At $913.75 x 1,150M shares = $1,050.8B market cap. Less $45.6B of adjusted
net cash, implied EV = $1,005.3B.

(1) Holding the Base-case path through FY2031 (peak FY27-28, downturn
    FY29-30), what normalized price premium P from FY2032 justifies the
    price?
      -> P = ~3.09x mid-cycle, permanently
         That implies:
           FY2036 revenue ~$314B
           GAAP operating margin ~66% in perpetuity
           SBC-adjusted FCF ~$124B/yr in FY2036
      i.e. the post-downturn steady state looks like FY2026, a year with
      a 74.6% margin.

(2) Holding Base FY2027-FY2036 cash flows, what terminal-year SBC-adjusted
    FCF justifies the price (at 11.5%, 2.5% and 12x)?
      -> Required PV of terminal value: $723.0B (vs $45.9B in Base)
      -> Required terminal SBC-adj FCF: ~$184B/yr (vs $11.6B in Base;
         FY2026 actual was ~$61B)

(3) Timing lens (Table D): even four years of shortage (FY27-FY30), a soft
    landing, and a permanent 1.6x premium only reach ~$738.

INTERPRETATION: the market is NOT pricing a cycle. At ~5.9x FY27E GAAP EPS
(~$156), the stock looks cheap on the traditional "buy memory at a low P/E
near the peak" heuristic. But that heuristic historically meant buying at
4-6x PEAK earnings that were 2-3x mid-cycle earnings. Here peak earnings
are ~9x the Base mid-cycle level: FY27E operating income of $210B vs
~$24B normalized. A 5.9x P/E on earnings that are 9x normal is not a
cyclical discount. It is a bet that FY2026-27 economics are the new
normal.

The bet is not crazy. SCAs, deposits, take-or-pay and HBM are new. But it
is a bet on the end of the memory cycle, and this model's Bull case, which
already grants most of that, does not get there.

SECTION 14 — KEY RISKS (TWO-SIDED)
================================================================================

RISKS TO THIS VALUATION BEING TOO LOW (upside risks)
1. STRUCTURAL BREAK IS REAL (HIGH). Take-or-pay SCAs with floors on ~35%
   of revenue through 2030-31, $32B of customer financial commitments,
   HBM wafer intensity, and cleanroom scarcity could make this cycle's
   trough far shallower and its normal far higher than history. This is
   the Bull case, and Table C/D quantify it.
2. DURATION (HIGH). Each additional year of peak conditions is worth ~$90/
   share. Management sees CY2027-28 as tighter than CY2026.
3. CAPITAL RETURN (MEDIUM). From Dec 9, 2026, Micron intends to return
   "100% of excess cash." Retiring shares while the price is below what
   the cash flows are actually worth would be accretive. This model holds
   shares flat.

RISKS TO THIS VALUATION BEING TOO HIGH (downside risks)
4. AI CAPEX DIGESTION (HIGH). Hyperscaler capex is the demand source for
   ~61% of revenue, so a pause hits memory first and hardest. That is the
   Bear case.
5. INDUSTRY SUPPLY RESPONSE (HIGH). Micron alone is spending ~$58B net in
   FY27, and Samsung, SK hynix and CXMT are all expanding. Memory pricing
   has never survived a coordinated capacity wave.
6. SCA FLOORS ARE LESS PROTECTIVE THAN THEY LOOK (MEDIUM). In prior
   cycles, LTAs were renegotiated when spot fell below contract. Deposits
   are returnable, and RPO is only ~25% recognized in the next 12 months.
   Customers with deposits at stake have leverage, too.
7. CHINA / CXMT (MEDIUM). Chinese DRAM capacity at commodity nodes caps
   mid-cycle pricing in mobile, client and auto, the BUs earning
   88%/79% margins today with the least structural protection.
8. CHIPS STRINGS (LOW-MEDIUM). Milestone-based grants carry clawback
   risk, and the 48D construction-start deadline (Dec 31, 2026) limits
   credits on later NY fabs.
9. EXECUTION (MEDIUM). HBM4/HBM4E qualification at NVIDIA (Micron has the
   thinnest HBM4 allocation of the three, per secondary reporting) and
   ramping 5+ new sites at once.

SECTION 15 — INVESTMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

POSITIVES
  + The best balance sheet in Micron's history: $68.3B net cash (~$45.6B
    after deducting deposits and taxes payable), BBB+ across all three
    agencies, debt cut to $5.2B.
  + FY2027 is near-certain to be another record year (~$256B revenue,
    ~$132B of SBC-adjusted FCF in Base). That cash is close to "in the
    bank."
  + Genuine structural changes: SCAs, take-or-pay, deposits, HBM
    customization (NVHBM), and a data-center mix that went from 26% to 61%
    in three years.
  + Technology position is strong (1-gamma DRAM, G9 NAND,
    record data-center SSD share).

NEGATIVES
  - The price ($913.75) exceeds every scenario in this model, including a
    Bull case that grants a shortage through FY2029 and a permanent
    near-record margin.
  - The current margin is being earned across ALL business units,
    including phones and PCs (MCBU 88%). That is the signature of scarcity
    pricing, which reverses, not of differentiation, which persists.
  - Capex is doubling into the peak, and the capacity has dates.
  - Historical through-cycle FCF margin was ~8% of revenue. Even the Base
    case's normalized ~11.5% is an upgrade on that.

BOTTOM LINE
Micron is a far better business than it was three years ago, and FY2026-27
will likely produce more cash than the company earned in its prior 20
years combined. But at $913.75, the market is capitalizing that peak
cash as if it were permanent. Base value is ~$325, probability-weighted
~$389, and Bull ~$833. On a cycle-aware DCF, the stock is priced for the
end of the memory cycle.

The stock becomes interesting on this framework in the ~$400-450 range,
where you would be paying for the weighted outcome plus some credit for
the SCA structure. It would be a high-conviction buy near ~$325 (Base),
which would essentially be paying for FY27-28 cash plus net cash and
getting the post-cycle business for free.

What would change the view:
  - Evidence that SCA floor prices actually hold through the first real
    supply additions in H2 CY2027-CY2028, i.e. Tongluo and ID1 ramp with
    no price break.
  - Two consecutive quarters of rising prices after new capacity starts.
  - Disclosure that RPO (min-price) revenue covers a much larger share of
    FY29-31 than the ~$33B/yr it implies now.

Conviction: HIGH on business quality and near-term cash. LOW on valuation
at $913.75. The answer is most sensitive to Table C (normalized premium)
and Table D (downturn timing). Those are the two dials a reader should
challenge.
