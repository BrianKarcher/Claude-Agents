WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
------------------------------
Charter closed the Cox Communications transaction on Aug 19/20, 2026 and the
Liberty Broadband merger the same day. Q3 2026 (reported Nov 6) is the first
quarter that includes Cox. In the same release Charter:
  - took a $6.3B non-cash impairment of legacy Charter franchises, citing
    "lower estimates for future customer and revenue growth" - management's
    own long-range plan was cut;
  - launched a "new operating model" with an incremental $2B run-rate opex
    savings program over 18 months, on top of >$1B of Cox synergies;
  - guided 2027 capex "at or below $11 billion" and declined to give any
    EBITDA outlook or any date for a return to growth.

The stock fell 6.2% on the day to $118.07, after having rallied 23% off the
Oct 26 low of $102.18.

The defining feature of this valuation is LEVERAGE. At $118.07:
  equity market value (164.6M economic diluted shares) ...  ~$19.4B
  senior claims (debt, SCF, Cox preferred, CHTRP)  ........ ~$115.4B
  equity as a share of enterprise value  .................. ~14%
Every $1B of enterprise value is worth $6.08 per share. A 10% change in EV
moves the equity value by roughly 80%. The DCF output is therefore a wide
range, and the most useful section of this file is the reverse DCF
(Section 13): it shows how small a change in the operating outlook
separates the market price from the Base case.

HEADLINE FINDING: the Base case is about double the price. But that is a
statement about leverage more than about a big disagreement with the market:
  - The market price is reproduced by cutting every Base-case cash flow
    (and the terminal value) by just 13.2%.
  - It is also reproduced if EBITDA declines ~2.2% a year forever from the
    FY2026E level (to ~$21.9B by 2036, against $25.6B in Base and $19.4B in
    Bear), or if the Base cash flows are discounted at 9.65% instead of
    8.0%.
  - At $118 the market is pricing an outcome between Base and Bear, nearer
    Bear on EBITDA.
  - A 25.9% cut to Base cash flows sets the equity to zero.
This is a high-variance, levered bet on whether Charter can stop the
broadband share loss and actually bank the cost program. It is not a
"cheap, safe" stock even though the Base case is far above the price.

Simple cross-check, Base 2027:
  pre-SBC unlevered FCF  ...................................  ~$12.2B
  less cash interest ($5.8B annualized run-rate per the call) ..  ($5.8B)
  less Cox preferred dividend  .............................  ($0.4B)
  plus the interest tax shield the unlevered build ignores ... ~+$0.9B
  = levered FCF  ...........................................  ~$6.9B (~$42/share)
That is a ~36% levered FCF yield on $118. The market is not pricing
2027-2028. It is pricing what happens to that cash stream after 2028, and the
risk that it shrinks while $108.6B of debt does not.

SECTION 1 - BUSINESS SNAPSHOT (POST-COX)
================================================================================

Charter (Spectrum) is the largest US cable operator by passings after the
Cox acquisition. Pro forma (PF) 9/30/26:
  - ~70.7M passings, 36.7M customer relationships (51.9% penetration)
  - 34.3M Internet customers, 13.5M mobile lines, 13.8M video, 6.1M voice
  - PF LTM revenue ~$66.5B; PF LTM Adj. EBITDA $27.3B ($27.5B ex-transition)

Capital structure: Charter Communications Inc. (Class A, ticker CHTR) owns
~70% of Charter Holdings. A/N (~15.5M units, ~9%) and Cox Enterprises
(33.6M units, ~21%) hold the rest as units exchangeable into Class A. Cox
also holds $6.0B of 6.875% convertible preferred units.

  Adj. EBITDA                   5,943    6,587    -4.0%  (-3.0% ex-transition)
  Capex                         3,027    3,235   -11.2%
  Free cash flow (actual)         674
  GAAP op. income (loss)       (3,526)           (incl. $6,326M impairment)
  Net loss attrib. Charter     (2,397)           EPS $(20.50)

Operating trend (PF):
  - Internet net adds -233K vs -171K PY. Legacy Charter -156K vs -109K.
    Legacy Charter Internet losses were worse YoY in each of Q1-Q3 2026
    (-120 vs -59, -172 vs -117, -156 vs -109).
  - Mobile +508K lines. Legacy Charter +440K vs +482K, decelerating.
  - Video -77K vs -121K, improving.
  - Residential ARPU -1.6% ("essentially flat" ex the streaming-app
    allocation).
  - Cox churn is "about 10% higher" than legacy Charter's. Spectrum pricing
    went live in Cox markets Sep 16 and roughly halved Cox's Internet-loss
    run-rate (call).
  - Competition: AT&T +769K, Verizon +314K and T-Mobile (undisclosed, likely
    ~400-500K) are adding ~1.4-1.6M broadband subs a quarter between them.
    Charter plus Comcast lost ~377K.

THE THESIS IN THREE SENTENCES, BOTH DIRECTIONS
  BULL: Cable's HFC network is fully depreciated and being upgraded on a
        budget; capex falls ~$2B+ from 2026 to 2028. More than $3B of
        identified cost removal (synergies plus the program) and a mobile
        bundle (13.5M lines, growing ~15%) can hold EBITDA flat-to-up even
        as broadband subs erode, and every dollar of FCF accrues to an equity
        that is only 14% of EV.
  BEAR: Fiber overbuild and fixed wireless are structural, not cyclical.
        Charter's own impairment says management believes it. Losses
        accelerated through 2026, and ~14% equity on $108.6B of debt turns a
        slow decline into a zero for shareholders.
  THIS MODEL: The Base assumes the decline slows, but Internet revenue does
        not return to growth until 2034, and only +0.5%. Cost savings mostly
        stick and capex falls as guided. The result is roughly flat EBITDA
        through 2029, eroding slowly afterward.

Observations:
  - Standalone Charter revenue has been flat for four years ($54-55B), and
    margin expansion (39.9% -> 41.5%) did all the work on EBITDA. That
    margin lever is now being pulled again, harder (synergies plus the $2B
    program).
  - FCF fell from $8.7B (2021) to $3.5-5.0B (2023-25) as capex rose from
    14.8% to 21.3% of revenue (rural subsidized build, network evolution).
    The 2027-28 capex decline is the single most important positive FCF
    driver in the model.
  - OCF has run $1-2.8B below (Adj. EBITDA - cash interest) every year
    since 2022: cash taxes, mobile device receivables (EIP) and other
    working capital. The model carries an explicit NWC/other line for this
    (Section 5.7).

3.8 Reconciliation notes
------------------------
  - No segment revenue exceeds consolidated revenue here; product lines sum
    exactly to the consolidated total in every period shown.
  - The ACTUAL series has a structural break at Q3'26 (Cox from Aug 20).
    Every growth rate in this model is anchored to the PF series.
  - Customer relationships were redefined in Q4'25 to include mobile-only
    customers. FY2024 was restated.
  - The Q3'25 legacy Charter mobile comparative is +482K in the Q3'26
    release vs +493K in the Q3'25 release. This is a SOURCE DISCREPANCY
    and does not affect the model.
  - How the segment data shapes the model: there is no structurally
    separate profit pool (no "growth segment bolted to a legacy one" with
    separate margins), so a single consolidated DCF with a LINE-LEVEL
    revenue build and line-level contribution margins is the right lens.
    A sum-of-the-parts companion would mostly re-cut the same cable
    economics and is not built. Separating Cox from legacy Charter is
    possible only on revenue and subs, not on profit.

5.3 Why 75% retention on the $2B program (Base)

5.6 SBC treatment
-----------------
EBIT is computed AFTER SBC (Adj. EBITDA - D&A - SBC - CTA/other). So:
  - SBC-adjusted UFCF = NOPAT + D&A - capex - NWC/other. SBC is a real
    cost, and this is the stream that is discounted.
  - Raw UFCF = SBC-adjusted UFCF + SBC. It is shown for reference only.
SBC is not double-counted.

5.7 NWC / other cash items
--------------------------

  - Mobile device installment receivables grow with line adds. The EIP
    facility that funds them ($1,696M) is EXCLUDED from debt in the bridge,
    so the receivable build must be a cash use here, to stay consistent.
  - Supporting history: OCF has trailed (EBITDA - cash interest) by
    $1-2.8B a year since 2022. Part of that gap is cash taxes, which the
    model already charges at 15%.
  - JUDGMENT CALL. Zeroing this line adds +$19.96/share.

    but those targets are typically near-term EV/EBITDA multiples. The
    reverse DCF shows the difference is ~13% of cash flow.


================================================================================
SECTION 15 - KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. Broadband share loss to fiber and FWA does not slow.
   - This is the dominant risk and the Bear case.
   - The telcos added ~1.4-1.6M broadband subs in Q3'26 alone.
   - Every additional -1%/yr on Internet revenue for a decade costs roughly
     $50+/share.
2. Leverage and refinancing.
   - $108.6B of principal at a 5.3% average coupon, against new secured
     paper at 6.05-7.85%. Refinancing the 2030 tower ($6.55B fixed at
     4.31% plus ~$10B already-floating) at market rates costs roughly
     $0.15-0.3B a year in extra interest.
   - Net leverage of 3.93x is above the 3.5x target, which is not due until
     ~Aug 2029. EBITDA decline makes deleveraging a treadmill.
3. Execution on the operating-model change and the Cox integration all at
   once.
   - A new COO (Sep 2026) and an interim CFO.
   - Cox customer service is being onshored within 18 months.
   - Cox churn is 10% higher than legacy Charter's.
4. Cost savings competed away.
   - Retention of the $2B is assumed at 75%. If competitive promotions
     force reinvestment, every 10 points of retention is worth ~$11/share.
5. Mobile is an MVNO.
   - The economics depend on the Verizon agreement.
   - Line-add deceleration (legacy Charter +440K vs +482K) suggests the
     bundle tailwind is maturing.
6. Video and programming.
   - Carriage disputes and the streaming-app allocation ($286M in Q3)
     depress reported video revenue.
   - The low contribution margin limits the EBITDA damage.
7. Capital allocation.
   - Management favors buybacks "as leverage falls".
   - The low-coupon debt is being repurchased below par, which is
     value-accretive.
   - Buybacks at prices below IV help per-share value; the reverse would
     hurt it.
8. The convertible preferred and the units.
   - Cox (~21%) and A/N (~9%) are large holders with suspended sell-down
     agreements. Future exchanges or sales are a supply overhang.
   - The tax receivable agreement leaks 50% of exchange tax benefits.
9. Regulatory and subsidy.
   - Subsidized rural (RDOF/BEAD) build obligations.
   - The Affordable Connectivity Program is gone, and low-income sales
     are already called out as weak.
