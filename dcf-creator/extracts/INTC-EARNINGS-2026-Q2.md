# Intel — Q2 2026 Financial Results (extract)

Source: Intel Q2 2026 earnings press release, https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1776/intel-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results (pulled live via web, Sept 27, 2026). Quarter ended June 27, 2026.

## Income statement (Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025)

**GAAP**
- Revenue: $16,128M (+25% YoY, vs $12,859M) — strongest YoY growth in 15+ years
- Gross profit: $6,509M vs $3,542M
- Gross margin: 40.4% vs 27.5%
- Operating income (loss): $1,796M vs $(3,176)M
- Net income (loss) attributable to Intel: $(11,033)M vs $(2,918)M — large GAAP net loss despite positive operating income. **Corrected Oct 1, 2026:** the driver is a **$12,529M non-cash mark-to-market loss on Escrowed Shares** (booked in interest and other, net), not tax items. Escrowed Shares are Intel shares held in escrow for release to the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS Act Secure Enclave agreement. The derivative liability tracks Intel's share price: its fair value rose from $2.7B (Dec 27, 2025) to $15.6B (Jun 27, 2026), and H1'26 losses totalled $13.6B. Non-GAAP excludes it; non-GAAP net income was positive, see below.
- EPS (diluted): $(2.16) vs $(0.67)

**Non-GAAP**
- Gross margin: 41.8% vs 29.7%
- Operating income: $2,770M vs $(503)M
- Net income attributable to Intel: $2,197M vs $(441)M
- EPS (diluted): $0.42 vs $(0.10)

## Cash flow — CORRECTED Oct 1, 2026

Re-tied to the Q2 2026 10-Q cash-flow statement and the Q1/Q2 2026 releases' non-GAAP reconciliations (sources below). **Two errors in the original version of this section:** (1) the "$(8,419)M vs $(1,050)M" adjusted FCF was the **Q2 three-month** figure, mislabeled as six months; (2) "$(6,192)M capital expenditures" was only the **investing-section** capex line. Intel's "gross capital expenditures" also includes capex paid on extended payment terms, which the 10-Q reports in the **financing** section ($(1,423)M in H1'26).

### Adjusted free cash flow reconciliation ($M; Intel's non-GAAP definition)

Adjusted FCF = operating cash flow − additions to PP&E (gross capex) + capital-related government incentives + SCIP partner contributions, net − payments on finance leases.

| $M | Q1'26 | Q2'26 | **H1'26** | Q1'25 | Q2'25 | **H1'25** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 1,096 | 7,006 | **8,102** | 813 | 2,050 | **2,863** |
| Gross capex (additions to PP&E) | (4,963) | (2,652) | **(7,615)** | (6,203) | (4,492) | **(10,695)** |
| Capital-related government incentives | 107 | 60 | **167** | 819 | 145 | **964** |
| Partner contributions, net | 1,959 | (12,216) | **(10,257)** | 897 | 1,250 | **2,147** |
| Payments on finance leases | (215) | (617) | **(832)** | (6) | (3) | **(9)** |
| **Adjusted free cash flow** | **(2,016)** | **(8,419)** | **(10,435)** | **(3,680)** | **(1,050)** | **(4,730)** |

Quarterly columns are as reported in each release. H1 columns are the sums, and they tie to the 10-Q six-month cash-flow statement:
- Gross capex H1'26 $7,615M = investing "additions to PP&E" $(6,192)M + financing "additions to PP&E" (extended payment terms) $(1,423)M. H1'25: $(8,733)M + $(1,962)M = $(10,695)M.
- Partner contributions, net H1'26 $(10,257)M = partner contributions $4,082M − partner distributions $(14,339)M. H1'25: $2,238M − $(91)M = $2,147M.

### The $14.2B Ireland SCIP buyback drives the H1'26 FCF number

On **April 8, 2026**, Intel reacquired Apollo's 49% interest in Ireland SCIP (Fab 34) for **$14.2B in cash, including transaction costs**. Funding came from cash, short-term investments, and a $6.5B term loan, which was refinanced in Q2 with $6.5B of senior notes. Intel now owns 100% of Ireland SCIP. Consequences:
- The payment sits in **partner distributions**, so it flows through Intel's "partner contributions, net" and **adjusted FCF**. Q2'26 adjusted FCF of $(8.4)B and H1'26 of $(10.4)B are therefore **not** an operating or capex deterioration.
- **Excluding the buyback** (approximate: $14.2B includes transaction costs that may not all sit in the distributions line), H1'26 adjusted FCF was roughly **+$3.8B**, and Q2'26 roughly **+$5.8B**.
- Accounting: $142M of non-controlling interest was eliminated and a $532M derivative liability (delay-related liquidated damages) extinguished. The residual **$13.5B was charged to capital in excess of par value**, a large part of the Dec'25 → Jun'26 drop in stockholders' equity.
- Arizona SCIP (Brookfield, 49%) remains. Its partner contributions continue: $4,082M of gross partner contributions in H1'26.

### Capex derived on the DCF's "net capex" basis

The DCF defines net capex as gross capex − government incentives − partner contributions (FY2025: 17,700 − 1,600 − 4,900 = 11,200). For H1'26, excluding the Apollo buyback:
- Gross capex $7,615M − incentives $167M − partner contributions net of ordinary (non-Apollo) distributions of ~$3.9–4.1B ⇒ **net capex ≈ $3.4–3.6B in H1'26.** The range exists because the 10-Q does not say exactly how much of the $14,339M of distributions was the Apollo payment.
- Gross capex fell sharply in Q2'26 ($2,652M vs $4,963M in Q1). The 10-Q MD&A cites "lower capital expenditures" and "lower financing payments for capital expenditures with extended payment terms" vs H1'25, without a further reason.

### Full-year 2026 capex outlook (from the Q2 call, not the release)

The Q2 release itself contains no capex guidance. On the **July 23, 2026 earnings call**, CFO David Zinsner said: "Due to strong customer demand signals, we're raising our outlook for 2026 and now expect our CapEx to be more than $20 billion." That is up ~$3B from the prior outlook, mostly tooling for Intel 3, 18A and 18A-P. He flagged 2027 capex as "significantly above" 2026, mostly in the US.
- **Implied H2'26 gross capex: more than ~$12.4B** (>$20B − $7.6B H1), vs $7.6B in H1. That is a steep back-half ramp.
