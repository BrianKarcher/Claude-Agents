## Notes

- **Fiscal calendar.** AMD used a 52-to-53-week fiscal year ending on the **last Sunday in December** in this era (not the last Saturday, which is the later convention). **Fiscal 2000 was a 53-week year ended December 31, 2000**; fiscal 1999 (ended December 26) and 1998 (ended December 27) were 52-week years. FY2000's revenue and expenses therefore contain one extra week relative to every other year in the FY1998–FY2005 range — worth a footnote on any chart that leans on the FY1999→FY2000 jump.
- **FY2000 operating income of 888,736 is clean of one-time items** — no restructuring charge, and the $336.9M Legerity gain sits *below* the operating line. Net income of $983M, by contrast, is heavily flattered by that gain; do not describe FY2000 net income as operating performance.
- **Legerity was consolidated only through July 31, 2000**, so FY2000 net sales include roughly seven months of the voice-communications business and FY2001 include none. AMD's MD&A explicitly cites "the reduction of gross margin as a result of the sale of Legerity, effective July 2000."
- FY1998 and FY1999 are captured here for completeness because the filing presents three years; **they are not currently rows in `annual-financials.md`** (each rests on this single filing plus, for FY1999, the FY2002 10-K Item 6 table). Verify against a second filing before promoting them to cache rows.
- Metric-label note: the caption is "Operating income (loss)". There is no "Gross margin" or "Gross profit" caption anywhere on the face of the statement in this era — AMD's first dollar-denominated gross-margin line for these years appears in the **FY2006 10-K's** Item 6 table (which starts at FY2002). See `AMD-10K-FY2005.md` for how that affects FY2002 and FY2004 rounding.
