01 · Company Snapshot

A data company built for the hardest problems first.

Founded in 2003 in Palo Alto by Peter Thiel, Alex Karp, Stephen Cohen, Joe Lonsdale, and Nathan Gettings — with early backing from In-Q-Tel, the CIA's venture capital arm. Its first software helped intelligence analysts find patterns buried in data nobody could connect. Two decades later it's one of the most valuable software companies trading publicly, and the pitch has barely changed: connect the data, then act on it.

02 · How It Works

Palantir doesn't sell an app. It sells a map of your reality.

Most organizations' data is scattered — spreadsheets, sensors, supply-chain systems, satellite feeds — that don't talk to each other. Palantir's core idea, the ontology, links all of it into one digital model of an organization: its people, assets, events, and decisions, connected like a living graph. Analysts, operators, and now AI agents work off that single model instead of hunting across a dozen disconnected systems.

03 · The Product Stack

Four platforms, one underlying idea.

Gotham

The original

Built for defense and intelligence. Fuses satellite, sensor, and field data in real time so analysts can see a battlefield or a threat as one picture instead of a dozen feeds.

Used by: militaries, intelligence agencies
Foundry

The commercial sibling

Connects a company's ERP, supply chain, IoT, and financial data through 200+ prebuilt connectors, so every team works off the same live picture.

Used by: airlines, automakers, hospitals, banks
AIP

The AI layer

Plugs large language models into an organization's own ontology, so AI can reason about real operations — without the underlying data ever leaving the customer's control.

Launched 2023 · today's growth engine
Apollo

The delivery engine

Continuously and securely ships software into any environment — cloud, on-prem, classified, even disconnected edge sites — with no manual deployment.

The plumbing that makes the rest work anywhere

08 · The Debate

Bull case vs. bear case.

The Bull Case

  • Revenue growth has accelerated four straight quarters — the fastest since the 2020 listing.
  • U.S. commercial revenue up 133% YoY, proving Palantir can win well beyond government.
  • 88% gross margin and 60% operating margin — hypergrowth and profitability rarely coexist like this.
  • AIP "bootcamps" are shortening enterprise sales cycles industry-wide.
Street-high target: $255 (BofA Securities)

The Bear Case

  • Priced for perfection: ~80x trailing sales — the richest multiple in large-cap software.
  • Any deceleration, even in an otherwise "good" quarter, can hit the stock hard.
  • Heavy reliance on U.S. government contracts tied to political and budget cycles.
  • Stock-based compensation dilution remains elevated.
Bear target: $70 (Jefferies, Sell rating)
