## Government / strategic-investor stakes (recap; see also INTC-HISTORICALS-2021-2025.md)

- **US government**: 9.9% stake, 433.3M shares, announced Aug 23, 2025, funded by CHIPS Act grants ($5.7B unpaid-but-committed) + Secure Enclave program ($3.2B), on top of $2.2B already-received CHIPS grants ($11.1B total government support to date). Passive — no board seat, no governance rights (per Intel's own clarification).
- **SoftBank**: $2B investment, ~2% stake, ~Aug 2025.
- **Nvidia**: $5.0B private placement, agreement Sept 2025, completed Dec 2025.
- Combined effect: Intel's balance sheet and equity cushion materially strengthened through 2025–2026 (cash + ST investments roughly doubled from Dec'24 to Dec'25), at the cost of meaningful share-count dilution (+15% in 2025 alone) and ~12% of the company now held by strategic/government investors with idiosyncratic incentives (US industrial policy, Nvidia's own AI-supply-chain interests) rather than pure return-seeking.

## Intel Foundry — the central swing factor

- **Foundry segment revenue is overwhelmingly internal.** External (third-party) foundry revenue was just **$293M in a recent quarter, up from $22M a year earlier** — a real increase, but tiny relative to segment revenue of $5.4–5.8B/quarter, and most of the increase reflects Altera becoming an internal-turned-external customer post-deconsolidation (Sept 2025) rather than genuine new external foundry wins. The bulk of Intel Foundry's ~$17–18B/yr segment revenue is intersegment (Intel Products buying its own wafers at transfer prices), which nets out entirely at the consolidated-revenue level.
- **18A** is in high-volume manufacturing (Arizona, Oregon fabs) as of late 2025, with two headline innovations: RibbonFET (gate-all-around transistors) and PowerVia (backside power delivery). Reported yield improvements through 2026 are cited as a driver of the Q2 2026 margin inflection.
- **14A — zero committed external customers as of early-to-mid 2026.** Intel has publicly stated it will NOT build out 14A capacity for outside customers until they commit — "a signed name is what unlocks the next stage of the build-out." Management says it has two prospective customers and expects firm decisions "in the second half of 2026 and extending into the first half of 2027." Reported interest/design-win chatter names Nvidia, AMD, Apple, Microsoft, Google, Marvell, Micron, and OpenAI as evaluating 18A/14A — but as of the most recent quarterly disclosures, none had converted to a committed, disclosed external customer at scale.
- **Disclosed risk factor**: Intel has explicitly flagged the risk that it may **pause or discontinue investment in Intel 14A and future leading-edge nodes** if it cannot secure sufficient committed external demand — i.e., management itself has put a going-concern-adjacent risk on the foundry strategy in its own filings.
- **Management's stated foundry breakeven target: 2027**, on the back of 14A, and CFO Zinsner has said Foundry needs only "low- to mid-single-digit billions" of ANNUAL EXTERNAL revenue to reach breakeven (i.e., the bar for "breakeven" is modest — this is a cost-absorption/utilization story more than a revenue-scale story). Target foundry gross margin on the path to breakeven: ~40%.
- 2025 restructuring also included **scrapping planned European manufacturing projects** and a reported delay to the Ohio 14A-related buildout, consistent with capital discipline under CEO Lip-Bu Tan (see below) rather than an unconstrained buildout.

## Capex

- FY2026 gross capex guidance was **raised from $18B to "more than $20B"** at the Q2 2026 release, citing stronger foundry customer demand signals; tooling spend flagged up ~40% vs 2025.
- **2027 capex flagged as "significantly above" 2026 levels**, predominantly US-based spend.
- Net capex (after government incentives + SCIP/partner contributions) has run well below gross: FY2024 gross $23.9–25.1B vs net $10.5B; FY2025 gross $17.7B vs net $11.2B. Partner (SCIP) contributions fell sharply from $12.7B (2024) to $4.9B (2025) as early joint-venture funding commitments (Brookfield/Apollo-style co-investment structures) matured/declined — a genuine open question is how much further partner-funding support continues into 2026–2027 as gross capex reaccelerates.

## Leadership / turnaround narrative

- **Lip-Bu Tan** became CEO effective March 18, 2025. Stated strategic pillars: refocus on the core product franchise (CCG/DCAI), refine AI strategy, rebuild trust with foundry customers, strengthen the balance sheet (accelerated government funding, Nvidia and SoftBank investment), and **monetize non-core assets**.
- **Headcount**: core Intel workforce cut by ~25% in 2025 (~25,000 jobs), targeting ~75,000 core employees by end-2025 (~15% of the reduction sometimes cited as the "core" cut, with the 25% figure reflecting the full scope including contractor/adjacent reductions — sources use both framings; treat "~25% workforce reduction through 2025" as the headline).
- **Mobileye monetization**: Intel sold 63.7M Mobileye Class A shares (~$1.02B) in July 2025, cutting its stake from ~85% toward ~80%, and has continued trimming — by August 2026 Intel's remaining Mobileye stake (~50M shares) was reported at only ~$484M in value, i.e. Intel has sold down the large majority of its original Mobileye position over the past year-plus. Altera was 51%-divested (deconsolidated) Sept 12, 2025. Both moves are balance-sheet-strengthening / non-core-asset-monetization under Tan.
- Overall framing in press coverage as of Sept 2026: "strong AI and data-center demand, improving foundry momentum, and a still-demanding valuation" — i.e., the operational turnaround narrative is real and improving, but coverage explicitly flags valuation as stretched after the 220% YTD rally.
