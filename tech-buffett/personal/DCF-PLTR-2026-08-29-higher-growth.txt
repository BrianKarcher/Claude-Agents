SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

Palantir builds software platforms — Gotham (government/defense), Foundry
(commercial), and the Ontology/AIP layer that wires large language models
into an organization's live operational data, actions, and workflows
("operational AI"). Two reporting segments: Government and Commercial, each
split U.S. vs. international. The U.S. business is the growth engine.

Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026):
  - Total revenue:      $1,935M   +93% YoY, +19% QoQ
  - U.S. commercial:      $764M   +149% YoY
  - U.S. government:      $809M   +90% YoY
  - International:        $362M   (~19% of total; slow-growing)
  - GAAP operating margin ~47%; adjusted operating margin ~62%
  - Rule of 40 score: 155%
  - ~$9.4B cash + marketable securities; zero debt

The thesis in three sentences:
  1. Palantir is the current leader in operational AI for large enterprises
     and Western governments; its Ontology/AIP layer is a real
     differentiator competitors have not matched at the operational-
     decision level.
  2. Revenue growth has ACCELERATED four years running: +17% (2023) ->
     +29% (2024) -> +56% (2025) -> +82% guided (2026) — rare above $4B of
     revenue.
  3. Economics are elite: ~85% gross margin, ~60% adjusted operating
     margin, fortress balance sheet, GAAP-profitable every quarter.

FY2026 guidance (raised at Q2, the largest raise in company history):
  - Revenue $8.150-$8.158B (~+82% YoY)
  - U.S. commercial revenue >$3.424B (>=+134%)
  - Adjusted operating income $4.889-$4.897B (~60% margin)
  - Adjusted free cash flow $4.5-$4.7B
Q3 2026 guidance: revenue $2.160-$2.164B (~+83% YoY).

Valuation context: at ~$185 and ~2.57B diluted shares, market cap is
~$476B — roughly 55x FY2026E revenue and >100x FY2026E adjusted EPS, among
the richest large-cap software multiples in the market.

SECTION 3 — DCF ASSUMPTIONS
================================================================================

METHODOLOGY
- 10-year explicit forecast FY2027E-FY2035E, off an FY2026E anchor built
  from company guidance (two actual quarters + one guided quarter + one
  estimated quarter tying to the full-year guide).
- Unlevered FCF. To avoid double-counting SBC (which is ~13% of revenue),
  the model forecasts a PRE-SBC (adjusted) operating margin, then subtracts
  SBC once, as its own line:
      Adjusted operating income = Revenue x (adjusted operating margin)
      NOPAT = Adjusted operating income x (1 - 15% tax)
      FCF (SBC-adjusted) = NOPAT + D&A - CapEx - SBC
  Company-reported adjusted operating margin (62% in Q2 2026, ~60% guided
  FY2026) anchors the pre-SBC margin line.
- Net cash of +$9,409M (cash + marketable securities, zero debt, 6/30/2026)
  added to enterprise value.
- Shares held flat at 2,569M diluted (SBC is charged in full as a cost
  above; a second penalty via rising share count would double-count
  dilution). Historical share growth of ~3-5%/yr is an unmodeled downside —
  see Section 7.

Rationale:

    growth halves quickly toward a normal post-hypergrowth software curve
    as hyperscaler tooling, Databricks/Snowflake, and in-house builds
    capture much of the operational-AI budget. FY2035E revenue ~$37B
    (~4.5x FY2026E), still more than double today.

SECTION 7 — KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. VALUATION / MULTIPLE COMPRESSION (VERY HIGH). ~55x forward revenue and
   >100x forward adjusted EPS. Even flawless execution has historically not
   protected multiples this high over a multi-year horizon. All three
   scenarios here sit below the current price; the risk is that the market
   re-rates the multiple toward software norms.

2. GROWTH DECELERATION FASTER THAN THE BASE CASE (HIGH). The base case
   assumes an unusually slow, shallow deceleration from an 82% peak. The
   empirical norm after a hyper-growth peak is a sharper step-down; the
   bear case captures that outcome (~$74/share).

3. COMPETITION IN OPERATIONAL AI (HIGH, longer-duration). Microsoft/Azure,
   Google, AWS, Snowflake, Databricks, and systems integrators are all
   building agent/operational-AI frameworks; many enterprises will build
   in-house. Palantir's Ontology lead is real today but not permanent.

4. SBC AND DILUTION (MEDIUM-HIGH). SBC is ~13-15% of revenue and share
   count has historically risen ~3-5% a year. This model charges SBC as a
   cost and holds shares flat; if shares keep rising ~4%/yr, per-share IV
   in every scenario is ~25-30% lower over the forecast.

5. GOVERNMENT / BUDGET-CYCLE AND HEADLINE RISK (MEDIUM). ~50%+ of revenue
   is government — durable and high-barrier, but procurement-paced,
   politically sensitive, and periodically the subject of negative press.

6. REVENUE / DEAL CONCENTRATION AND LUMPINESS (MEDIUM). A meaningful share
   of revenue and signed TCV comes from a limited number of very large
   contracts; the market reacts sharply to a single weak signings quarter.

7. TAX-RATE NORMALIZATION (LOW-MEDIUM, two-way). The 15% modeled rate is
   above Palantir's current cash rate (NOLs) but its GAAP rate is volatile.
   Single-digit cash rates for most of the forecast would make every IV
   here ~8-10% conservative; normalization to ~21%+ would make them ~7%
   high.

SECTION 8 — ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

Palantir is one of the highest-quality growth stories in software, and
this model treats a slow, shallow growth deceleration — revenue compounding
at ~24% for nine years off an $8B base to ~$56B — as the central case, not
the optimistic one. Even so, valued at an 11% discount rate with a premium
25x terminal multiple, intrinsic value is ~$107/share, roughly 42% below
the market. The bull case (revenue ~$78B by FY2035E) reaches ~$143 —
still ~23% under the current price.

For the stock to be worth ~$185 today on a cash-flow basis, you need
revenue growth beyond even this model's bull path, OR the bull revenue path
combined with margins expanding past ~65%, SBC falling toward ~5% of
revenue, a discount rate below ~10%, and a terminal multiple well above
25x — several optimistic assumptions stacked together.

Bottom line: strong business, and a durable-hyper-growth view is a
reasonable premise, but at ~$185 there is no margin of safety on a
cash-flow basis under any scenario in this model. A ~15% cushion to the
base case would be an entry around $90; a ~15% cushion to the bull case
would be around $120.

Conviction: the output is only as good as the premise that Palantir
compounds revenue at ~24%/yr for nine years off an $8B base — a rate with
very little precedent at this scale. This model shows what the valuation
looks like if that holds with otherwise-conservative inputs; it is not
evidence that it will.
