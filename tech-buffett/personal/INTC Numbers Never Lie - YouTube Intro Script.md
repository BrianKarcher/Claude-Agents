### [0:15–0:45] THE SETUP: WHY THE HYPE IS REAL

**B-ROLL:** fabs, Xeon chips, logos for the U.S. government, SoftBank and Nvidia

> To be clear, this turnaround isn't fake. Last quarter, revenue grew 25%, the fastest in over fifteen years. The data-center business jumped 59%. Intel posted a real GAAP operating profit for the first time in years. And the U.S. government, SoftBank and Nvidia all wrote checks to keep the lights on.

> But Intel isn't just a chip designer. It's betting the company on becoming a foundry that builds chips for everyone else, like TSMC. Last year, that foundry lost **ten billion dollars**. Its revenue from outside customers was a rounding error. And for its next big node, 14A, it has **zero** committed outside customers.

---

### [0:45–1:15] THE STAKES: WHAT THE MARKET IS PRICING IN

**ON SCREEN:** three bars: Bear ~$0 | Base $12.79 | Bull $38.71, with a red line at $123 towering over all of them

> So I ran three scenarios. In the bear case, about twenty billion dollars of net debt wipes out the equity entirely. In the base case, it's worth about thirteen dollars. And even in the bull case, where the foundry actually wins big outside customers and revenue nearly triples, I get under forty dollars a share.

> To justify $123, Intel would need to generate about **four times** the free cash flow of my *bull* case.
