HEADLINE FINDING: Under this model, Palantir at ~$185 does not clear a
purchase threshold under ANY scenario — not even the bull case. This is the
same qualitative outcome the AMD model in this folder reached, and for a
similar structural reason: the business is genuinely excellent, but the
stock price already embeds a decade-plus of near-flawless hyper-growth AND
no multiple compression. A 10-year explicit DCF — even a generous one with
a 35x terminal FCF multiple and a sub-10% discount rate — cannot reproduce
the current price. To justify $185, the market is implicitly assuming a
free-cash-flow trajectory roughly 2.6x higher than this model's base case
(i.e., at or beyond the bull-case revenue path of ~$56B by 2035) WHILE
still applying a 25x terminal multiple — or, equivalently, this model's
base-case cash flows capitalized at ~65x. Both are aggressive.

This is NOT a claim that Palantir is a bad business or that the stock will
fall. It is a claim that at this price the stock offers no margin of
safety on a cash-flow basis, and a buyer is paying today for execution
that, if delivered, still only gets them back to roughly break-even versus
the bull case.

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

Palantir builds software platforms for integrating, managing, and acting on
data at enterprise and government scale. Two historical platforms — Gotham
(government/defense) and Foundry (commercial) — now sit under the Palantir
"Ontology" and AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform), which connect large
language models to an organization's real operational data, actions, and
workflows. The pitch is "operational AI": not chatbots, but LLM-driven
decision loops wired into logistics, underwriting, manufacturing, targeting,
claims, supply chains, etc.

Reporting structure: two segments — Government and Commercial — each split
U.S. vs. international. The U.S. business is the growth engine.

Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) revenue mix:
  - U.S. commercial:   $764M    +149% YoY, +28% QoQ   (fastest-growing line)
  - U.S. government:    $809M    +90%  YoY, +18% QoQ
  - U.S. total:       $1,573M    +115% YoY
  - International:      $362M    (slow-growing; ~19% of total)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Total Q2 2026 revenue: $1,935M, +93% YoY, +19% QoQ
  By segment: Government $990M (+79%), Commercial $945M (+110%)

The thesis in three sentences:
  1. Palantir is the current leader in "operational AI" for large
     enterprises and Western governments — its Ontology/AIP layer is a
     genuine differentiator that competitors (hyperscaler AI tooling,
     Snowflake, Databricks, systems integrators, in-house builds) have not
     matched at the level of wiring AI into live operational decisions.
  2. Growth is ACCELERATING at scale: total revenue YoY growth went
     +17% (2023) -> +29% (2024) -> +56% (2025) -> +82% guided (2026) —
     an extraordinarily rare pattern for a company already above $4B of
     revenue, driven by U.S. commercial AIP adoption and a re-accelerating
     U.S. government/defense budget cycle.
  3. The economics are elite: ~85% GAAP gross margin, ~60% adjusted
     operating margin, a Rule of 40 score of 155% in Q2 2026, ~$9.4B of
     cash and marketable securities, and zero debt.

Key near-term dynamics (as of the August 3, 2026 Q2 print):
  - FY2026 revenue guide RAISED to $8.150-$8.158B (~+82% YoY) — the
    company's largest-ever guidance raise (+11 points on the growth rate in
    a single quarter). U.S. commercial guided to >$3.424B (>=+134%).
  - Adjusted operating income guided to $4.889-$4.897B (~60% margin);
    adjusted FCF guided to $4.5-$4.7B.
  - Q3 2026 revenue guided to $2.160-$2.164B (~+83% YoY).
  - Q2 contract signings: TCV closed $3.373B (+49% YoY); U.S. commercial
    TCV $2.132B (+153%); U.S. commercial remaining deal value $6.238B
    (+124% YoY). RPO $4.9B.
  - Balance sheet: $9.4B cash + marketable securities (6/30/2026), no debt,
    undrawn $500M revolver. A ~$5.6B multi-year cloud-hosting purchase
    commitment through Feb 2036 is disclosed.
  - GAAP net income ($1,066M in Q2) runs ABOVE GAAP operating income
    ($912M) because of interest income on the cash pile plus favorable tax
    items — GAAP EPS overstates core operating earnings power somewhat.
  - Valuation context: at ~$185 and ~2.57B diluted shares, market cap is
    ~$476B (some data providers show ~$447B on a lower basic share count).
    That is roughly 55x FY2026E revenue and >100x FY2026E adjusted EPS —
    among the richest large-cap software multiples in the market.

SECTION 3 — STRATEGIC CONTEXT: WHY THE BUSINESS IS EXCELLENT AND WHY THE
STOCK IS STILL HARD TO OWN AT THIS PRICE
================================================================================

WHAT IS GENUINELY WORKING
  - AIP / Ontology has found real product-market fit in U.S. commercial.
    U.S. commercial revenue +149% YoY and U.S. commercial TCV +153% YoY in
    Q2 2026 are not "story" numbers — they are signed contracts. The
    "bootcamp" go-to-market (get a customer to a working AI use case in
    days, then expand) has compressed sales cycles and is landing
    mid-market and Fortune-500 logos alike.
  - U.S. government re-accelerated. After years of ~15-30% government
    growth, U.S. government revenue grew +90% YoY in Q2 2026, helped by a
    defense-modernization budget cycle, the Army enterprise agreement, and
    expanding civilian-agency work. Palantir is one of very few software
    vendors positioned as a prime (not a subcontractor) in defense
    software.
  - Operating leverage is real and proven. Adjusted operating margin ~60%,
    GAAP operating margin ~47% and rising, Rule of 40 score 155%. The
    business converts incremental revenue to profit at a very high rate.
  - Fortress balance sheet: ~$9.4B cash + securities, no debt. Interest
    income alone is a meaningful earnings contributor.
  - Growth ACCELERATION at scale is the rarest thing here. Almost no
    software company sustains rising YoY growth as it crosses $4B -> $8B.

WHY THE STOCK IS STILL HARD TO OWN AT ~$185
  - The multiple. ~55x forward revenue and >100x forward adjusted EPS is a
    level historically associated with the top ~1% of software valuations,
    and one that has almost always compressed over a multi-year horizon
    regardless of how well the business executed (cf. the 2021 SaaS cohort).
  - Concentration and lumpiness. A large share of revenue and of TCV comes
    from a modest number of very large U.S. commercial and U.S. government
    deals. Signings are lumpy quarter to quarter; a single slow signings
    quarter tends to hit the stock hard given the expectations embedded.
  - Government exposure cuts both ways. It is durable and high-barrier, but
    also politically and budget-cycle sensitive, procurement-paced, and
    periodically headline-risk-prone.
  - SBC. At ~13-15% of revenue, stock comp is a real cost and a real
    source of share-count growth (shares have historically risen ~3-5% a
    year). The company's "adjusted FCF" ignores this.
  - Competition is not standing still. Hyperscalers (Microsoft/Azure AI,
    Google, AWS), Snowflake, Databricks, and large systems integrators are
    all pushing "operational AI" / agent frameworks, and many large
    enterprises will attempt to build rather than buy. Palantir's lead is
    real today but not permanent.
  - Key-person / narrative risk. The stock trades with unusually high
    retail ownership and narrative sensitivity; realized volatility is far
    above the large-cap software norm.

SECTION 4 — DCF ASSUMPTIONS
================================================================================

MODELING APPROACH
- 10-year explicit forecast FY2027E-FY2035E, off an FY2026E anchor built
  directly from company guidance (two actual quarters + one guided quarter
  + one estimated quarter that ties to the full-year guide).
- Unlevered FCF (FCFF). To avoid the double-counting of SBC that a
  mechanical "GAAP-EBIT-then-subtract-SBC-again" build would create for a
  company whose SBC is ~13% of revenue, this model forecasts a PRE-SBC
  (adjusted) operating margin, then subtracts SBC exactly once, as its own
  line:
      Adjusted operating income = Revenue x (adjusted operating margin)
      NOPAT = Adjusted operating income x (1 - 15% tax)
      FCF (SBC-adjusted) = NOPAT + D&A - CapEx - SBC
  This is structurally the same "SBC is a real cost" treatment used in the
  NVDA/AMD/CHTR/HIMS models in this folder (SBC is a cash-equivalent
  deduction), but starts from a pre-SBC margin so SBC is charged once, not
  twice. Company-reported adjusted operating margin (62% in Q2 2026, ~60%
  guided FY2026) is the natural anchor for the pre-SBC margin line.
- Shares held flat at 2,569M diluted. Justification: because SBC is
  subtracted in full as a cost above, adding a second penalty via rising
  share count would double-count dilution. The RISK that shares keep rising
  ~3-5%/yr anyway (as they historically have) is flagged in Section 8 and
  sized in Section 6.
- Net cash added to enterprise value: +$9,409M (cash + marketable
  securities, zero debt, as of 6/30/2026). No pension/lease adjustments
  made; the ~$5.6B cloud-hosting purchase commitment is treated as an
  operating cost already inside the margin assumptions, not as debt.
- Each scenario uses its own WACC, terminal growth rate, and exit multiple.

DISCOUNT RATE DERIVATION
This folder anchors a mature mega-cap software compounder (MSFT) at 10%
Base WACC, a semiconductor challenger (AMD) at 11.5% Base, and NVDA at
10.5% Base. Palantir gets a HIGHER base WACC than any of those:
  BASE:  11.0% — a high-quality, high-margin software business, but far
         smaller than MSFT, with lumpier revenue, heavy government exposure,
         a shorter track record of durable cash generation, elevated SBC,
         and realized stock volatility well above the large-cap norm.
  BULL:  9.5% — a world where AIP/operational-AI proves to be a durable,
         widening moat, government revenue proves steady rather than
         cyclical, and the market comes to treat PLTR as a MSFT-like
         compounder.
  BEAR:  13.5% — a world where competition commoditizes "operational AI,"
         growth decelerates hard, and the market re-rates the name the way
         it re-rated the 2021 high-multiple SaaS cohort.

PROBABILITY WEIGHTS: Bull 30% / Base 45% / Bear 25%. Slightly more
optimistic than the AMD model's 25/45/30, reflecting Palantir's genuine,
signed-contract growth acceleration and elite margins — but not more,
because the valuation starting point leaves no room for error. A reader who
weights the multiple-compression risk more heavily could reasonably move
10-15 points into the Bear bucket.

as a cost, with shares still held flat (an aggressive but not unheard-of
approach) — every year's FCF rises by that year's SBC amount. Base-case
FY35E FCF would be ~$19,560M instead of $16,972M, and the whole stream
scales up similarly. Base-case IV under that treatment is roughly
$95-$100/share. Still ~48% below the current price. The SBC methodology
choice does not change the conclusion; it changes the size of the gap.

TABLE E: REVERSE DCF — WHAT IS PRICED IN
To justify the current ~$185.42 (equity ~$476B; EV ~$467B after net cash),
holding the base-case cost structure, 11.0% WACC, and 25x exit multiple
constant, the entire FCF stream would need to be ~2.6x this model's base
case. That corresponds to:
  - roughly the BULL revenue path (~$56B by FY2035E) AND the base-case
    terminal multiple, or
  - the base-case revenue path with a ~65x terminal FCF multiple, or
  - the base-case cash flows discounted at ~6% instead of 11%.
Each of these is at or beyond the edge of what this model considers
defensible.

SECTION 8 — KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. VALUATION / MULTIPLE COMPRESSION (VERY HIGH — the dominant risk here)
   ~55x forward revenue and >100x forward adjusted EPS. Even flawless
   execution has historically not protected multiples this high over a
   multi-year horizon. This model's entire "overvalued" conclusion is
   really a statement that the current multiple is unsustainable on a
   cash-flow basis, and the reverse DCF (Table E) quantifies how much has
   to go right just to justify today's price.

2. GROWTH DURABILITY / DECELERATION (HIGH)
   Growth is currently accelerating; the base case assumes it decelerates
   in an orderly way. A sharp, early deceleration (one or two weak signings
   quarters) would both cut the cash-flow forecast AND likely trigger
   multiple compression at the same time — a double hit given how much
   is priced in.

3. COMPETITION IN "OPERATIONAL AI" (HIGH, longer-duration)
   Microsoft/Azure AI, Google, AWS, Snowflake, Databricks, and systems
   integrators are all building agent/operational-AI frameworks, and many
   enterprises will build in-house. Palantir's Ontology lead is real today
   but not permanent; the bear case is largely a competition story.

4. STOCK-BASED COMPENSATION AND DILUTION (MEDIUM-HIGH)
   SBC is ~13-15% of revenue and share count has historically risen ~3-5%
   a year. This model charges SBC as a cost and then holds shares flat; if
   shares instead keep rising ~4%/yr, per-share IV in every scenario is
   ~25-30% lower over the forecast than shown.

5. GOVERNMENT / BUDGET-CYCLE AND HEADLINE RISK (MEDIUM)
   ~50%+ of revenue is government. Durable and high-barrier, but
   procurement-paced, politically sensitive, and periodically the subject
   of negative press (surveillance, immigration, defense) that can affect
   sentiment and multiple independent of fundamentals.

6. REVENUE / DEAL CONCENTRATION AND LUMPINESS (MEDIUM)
   A meaningful share of revenue and TCV comes from a limited number of
   very large contracts. Signings are lumpy; the market reacts sharply to
   any single-quarter signings air-pocket.

7. TAX-RATE NORMALIZATION (LOW-MEDIUM, and a two-way risk)
   The 15% modeled rate is above Palantir's current cash rate (NOLs) but
   the GAAP rate is volatile. If the cash rate stays single-digit for most
   of the forecast, every IV here is ~8-10% conservative; if it normalizes
   to ~21%+, IVs are ~7% high.

8. DATA QUALITY (LOW-MEDIUM, a modeling caveat)
   This model was built from press releases, a third-party 10-Q
   transcription, and secondary coverage — SEC.gov could not be read
   directly (HTTP 403). Segment splits and some prior-year comparatives
   are derived by subtraction. Treat every figure as corroborated-but-
   not-primary until checked against the actual filings.

SECTION 9 — INVESTMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

POSITIVES (WHY THE BUSINESS DESERVES A PREMIUM)
  + Growth is ACCELERATING at $8B scale — +17% -> +29% -> +56% -> +82%
    guided — which almost no software company achieves.
  + The numbers are signed, not narrative: U.S. commercial revenue +149%
    YoY and U.S. commercial TCV +153% YoY in Q2 2026.
  + Elite unit economics: ~85% gross margin, ~60% adjusted operating
    margin, Rule of 40 score of 155%.
  + Fortress balance sheet: ~$9.4B cash + securities, zero debt.
  + A genuine product lead in "operational AI" (Ontology/AIP) that
    competitors have not matched at the operational-decision layer.
  + GAAP-profitable every quarter with rising GAAP operating margin.

NEGATIVES (WHY IT SHOULD NOT BE BOUGHT AT ~$185 ON THIS ANALYSIS)
  - No margin of safety at any scenario. Base case implies ~60% downside;
    even the bull case implies ~11% downside. A DCF that has to reach for
    a 35x terminal multiple and a sub-10% discount rate and STILL lands
    below the current price is telling you the price is the problem.
  - The reverse DCF requires roughly the bull-case revenue path AND a
    premium terminal multiple simultaneously just to justify today's price.
  - SBC at ~13-15% of revenue and historical ~3-5% annual share growth is
    a real, recurring drag this model may still be treating too kindly.
  - Competition in operational AI is intensifying from the best-capitalized
    companies in software.
  - The stock's realized volatility and narrative sensitivity make it a
    poor fit for a "buy and hold for a decade with a margin of safety"
    mandate at this entry price.

BOTTOM LINE
Palantir is one of the highest-quality growth stories in software right
now, and this analysis does not dispute that. What it disputes is the
price. At ~$185 the stock is discounting a decade of near-flawless
hyper-growth plus permanent premium multiples, and a cash-flow model — even
a deliberately generous one — cannot get there. On this framework PLTR is a
"wonderful company, wrong price" situation: no action warranted at the
current quote. The price at which the base case would offer even a modest
(~15%) margin of safety is roughly $60-65/share; the bull case supports
roughly $165. A disciplined entry would wait for a drawdown into at least
the low $100s (bull-case-supported with a real cushion) before this becomes
an interesting risk/reward, and ideally lower.
