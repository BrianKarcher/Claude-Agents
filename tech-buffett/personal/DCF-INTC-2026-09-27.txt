WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
-----------------------------
Intel is not a TAM-driven growth story like the AMD/NVDA/PLTR models in this
folder — it is a LEVERAGED TURNAROUND. The stock is up ~220% year-to-date in
2026 on: (1) a real, reported operating inflection (Q2 2026 GAAP operating
income positive for the first time in years, DCAI +59% YoY, 18A yields
improving), (2) balance-sheet rescue capital from the US government (9.9%
stake), SoftBank ($2B) and Nvidia ($5B), and (3) optimism that Intel Foundry
finally becomes a real external competitor to TSMC on 14A. This model builds
a bottom-up consolidated DCF around three scenarios that differ primarily on
ONE question: does Intel Foundry ever become a real EXTERNAL business, or
does it stay what it mostly is today — an internal, loss-making cost center
that happens to also manufacture Intel's own chips? Section 3 explains why
that distinction, not a TAM assumption, is the central lever in this model.

This analysis does NOT independently endorse or reject the turnaround. It
builds a standard, conservative-to-optimistic three-scenario DCF off Intel's
own reported numbers and government/company guidance, and compares the
result to where the stock actually trades. Not investment advice.

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

Intel is an integrated device manufacturer (IDM) — it designs AND
manufactures semiconductors, unlike fabless peers (AMD, NVIDIA). It reports
three segments since the Altera deconsolidation (Sept 12, 2025) and the Q1
2025 folding of the old "Network and Edge" segment into CCG/DCAI:

  Client Computing Group (CCG) ....... PC/laptop CPUs (Core, Core Ultra)
  Data Center & AI (DCAI) ............ Server CPUs (Xeon) + AI accelerators
  Intel Foundry ....................... Wafer fabrication — for Intel's own
                                         chips (overwhelmingly) and, in
                                         theory, for external customers
  All Other ........................... Mobileye (majority-owned, being sold
                                         down), Intel Capital, other

FY2025 (GAAP): revenue $52.9B (flat YoY), gross margin 34.8%, operating LOSS
$(2.2)B (vs $(11.7)B FY2024 — that year's loss driven by a $15.9B Q3 2024
impairment/DTA charge), net loss $(267)M, EPS $(0.06). Non-GAAP: gross margin
36.7%, operating income +$2.9B, net income +$1.9B, EPS $0.42 — i.e. Intel was
solidly profitable on a non-GAAP basis in 2025 even as GAAP showed a loss.

Q2 2026 (quarter ended Jun 27, 2026), GAAP: revenue $16.1B (+25% YoY, fastest
growth in 15+ years), gross margin 40.4%, operating income +$1.8B (vs a
$(3.2)B loss a year earlier — the real inflection), net LOSS $(11.0)B (large
GAAP-only tax/valuation-allowance item; non-GAAP net income was +$2.2B, EPS
$0.42). DCAI revenue +59% YoY to $6.3B; CCG +13% to $8.9B. Q3 2026 guide:
revenue $15.8-16.8B (+~20% YoY at midpoint).

Balance sheet (Q2 2026): cash + ST investments $29.7B; total debt $50.5B;
NET DEBT ~$20.8B. Unlike AMD/NVDA-style fabless peers, Intel carries real net
leverage, not a net-cash cushion — a structural difference that matters a
great deal to the equity bridge in this model (Section 6).

THE TURNAROUND IN THREE SENTENCES:
  1. Intel Products (CCG+DCAI) is a real, profitable, competitively pressured
     business: $49.1B revenue and $12.7B operating income in FY2025 (26%
     segment margin), with DCAI now growing fast again on AI-server demand.
  2. Intel Foundry lost $(10.3)B in FY2025 (improving from $(13.3)B in
     FY2024) and is overwhelmingly an INTERNAL manufacturing arm today —
     genuine third-party (external) foundry revenue was ~$293M in a recent
     quarter versus ~$5.4-5.8B of total segment revenue. Management targets
     foundry operating BREAKEVEN in 2027, but has zero committed external
     customers for its next node (14A) as of mid-2026, and has explicitly
     disclosed the risk that it may pause or cancel 14A investment if it
     can't land committed external demand.
  3. The balance sheet has been rescued, not organically repaired: the US
     government (9.9%), SoftBank (~2%) and Nvidia ($5B) together supplied
     ~$15B+ of capital and absorbed ~15% share-count dilution in 2025 alone.
     That buys time and credibility but does not, by itself, make Intel
     Foundry a profitable business — it funds the bet that 14A lands real
     customers before the capital runs out.

SECTION 3 — THE FOUNDRY REVENUE ACCOUNTING ISSUE (THE INPUT THIS MODEL IS BUILT ON)
================================================================================

Every DCF in this folder has one input that does the most work: for AMD it
was the 2030 AI-accelerator TAM; for Intel it is a plainer but easy-to-miss
accounting fact.

INTEL FOUNDRY'S REPORTED SEGMENT REVENUE (~$17-18B/YR) IS OVERWHELMINGLY
INTERNAL. Intel Products (CCG+DCAI) buys its own wafers from Intel Foundry
at an internal transfer price; both segments book that transaction as
revenue on their own P&L, and it is eliminated when Intel consolidates.
Genuine third-party (external) Foundry revenue — actual TSMC-style customers
paying Intel to fabricate their chips — was reported at just ~$293M in a
recent quarter, up from ~$22M a year earlier, against ~$5.4-5.8B of total
quarterly Foundry segment revenue. Most of even that small increase reflects
Altera becoming a (former-subsidiary-turned-independent) customer after its
September 2025 deconsolidation, not new competitive wins against TSMC/
Samsung. As of mid-2026, Intel has ZERO committed external customers for its
next-generation 14A node, and has told investors it will not build out 14A
external capacity until a customer commits — with two prospects, and
decisions expected "in the second half of 2026 and extending into the first
half of 2027." Intel has also explicitly disclosed the risk that it may
pause or discontinue 14A investment if it cannot secure that demand.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE DCF: A model that took "Intel Foundry revenue" at
its reported ~$17-18B segment-level face value and grew it as if that were
a real, monetizable external business would be double-counting — that
revenue mostly already shows up inside Intel Products' own reported
revenue (as the products those wafers become). This model instead builds
CONSOLIDATED revenue as approximately:

  Consolidated revenue ~= Intel Products (CCG+DCAI) revenue
                          + All Other (Mobileye etc.) revenue
                          + Intel Foundry EXTERNAL (third-party) revenue

— and the entire Bull/Base/Bear spread in this model is driven by (a) how
Intel Products' CCG/DCAI competitive position evolves, and (b) whether that
Foundry EXTERNAL line ever becomes a real, multi-billion-dollar business
(the actual "Intel becomes a second TSMC" bull case) or stays a rounding
error (the "Foundry only ever serves itself" bear/base case). Management's
own stated breakeven bar is modest — "low- to mid-single-digit billions" of
annual EXTERNAL revenue — which this model treats as achievable even in the
Base case; the Bull case requires 14A to land marquee customers (Nvidia,
Apple, Microsoft, etc. have all been reported as evaluating 18A/14A) at a
scale that makes Intel Foundry a genuine second source to TSMC, which is a
much higher bar than merely "breakeven."

This analysis does not independently handicap whether 14A lands external
customers — it is the central uncertainty the three scenarios are built
around, and every intrinsic value in this file inherits that uncertainty.

TAKEAWAYS FROM THE SEGMENT VIEW
  - In the BEAR case, Intel Foundry essentially never becomes a real
    external business (14A investment paused or produces no meaningful
    external wins) — consolidated growth is driven entirely by Intel
    Products holding roughly flat share in a maturing PC/server market.
  - In the BASE case, Foundry clears management's own modest "low-to-mid
    single-digit billions" external-revenue breakeven bar and grows
    modestly beyond it, but never becomes a scale competitor to TSMC —
    Products still does ~84% of the work.
  - Only in the BULL case does Foundry become a meaningfully large external
    business (~$22B by 2030, ~$48B by 2036) — still well short of TSMC's
    scale (>$100B), but transformative for Intel and the scenario that
    would justify calling Intel "the second real leading-edge foundry."
  - Even in the Bull case, Intel Products (not Foundry) remains the
    majority of consolidated revenue throughout the forecast window — this
    is an important contrast with how the market narrative (dominated by
    Foundry/18A/14A headlines) is often framed.

SECTION 5 — DCF ASSUMPTIONS
================================================================================

MODELING APPROACH
- 10-year explicit forecast 2027E-2036E, off an FY2026E anchor built
  bottom-up from H1 2026 actuals + Q3 2026 guidance + a Q4 2026 estimate
  (Section 4A).
- Consolidated revenue and operating margin are forecast directly (not
  built up from the segment tables, for the reasons in Section 3) — the
  Section 4 segment split is presented for context/attribution, not as the
  forecast mechanism.
- Unlevered FCF: FCF = NOPAT + D&A - Net CapEx. A second metric, "SBC-
  adjusted FCF," further subtracts stock-based compensation as a real
  economic cost; SBC-adjusted FCF is what is discounted and used for
  terminal value (same convention as the AMD/other models in this folder).
- CAPEX IS NET of government incentives and SCIP/partner contributions,
  consistent with how Intel itself now reports "adjusted free cash flow" —
  i.e. the capex figures in Section 6 are NOT the >$20B gross number Intel
  guides to, they are the lower net-cash-cost figure after subsidies.
- NOPAT = GAAP operating income x (1 - 15% tax rate) IN YEARS WITH POSITIVE
  OPERATING INCOME; in years with a GAAP operating LOSS, NOPAT = operating
  income unchanged (no tax benefit assumed to be realized in a loss year —
  conservative, and consistent with Intel's own recent history of NOT
  realizing cash tax benefits from losses, per the FY2024 DTA valuation-
  allowance writedown).
- NET DEBT (not net cash) is SUBTRACTED from enterprise value to reach
  equity value: $20,810M (Q2 2026) — the opposite direction from the
  AMD/NVDA/PLTR models in this folder, which added net cash.
- Each scenario uses its own WACC, terminal growth rate, and exit multiple.

REVENUE — anchored to the Section 4 segment build, then decelerated to

SECTION 9 — KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. FOUNDRY EXTERNAL-CUSTOMER RISK (VERY HIGH — this model's foundational
   risk, analogous to AMD's TAM premise)
   Zero committed external 14A customers as of mid-2026. Management's own
   framing ("a signed name unlocks the next stage of the build-out," a
   disclosed risk of pausing/cancelling 14A) puts real, company-acknowledged
   odds on the Bear case. The Bull case requires converting "evaluating"
   customers (Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, etc., per public reporting) into
   disclosed, committed, at-scale design wins within roughly the next
   18 months.

2. NET DEBT AND CONTINUED DILUTION (VERY HIGH)
   Unlike AMD/NVDA/PLTR in this folder, Intel carries real net leverage
   ($20.8B) that widened, not narrowed, over H1 2026 as gross capex
   reaccelerated. Adjusted free cash flow has been negative every year
   since at least FY2024. Absent a durable FCF inflection, further equity
   issuance (beyond the government/SoftBank/Nvidia transactions already
   completed) is a realistic funding path, and every per-share value in
   this model already reflects a HELD-FLAT share count that may prove
   optimistic.

3. PARTNER (SCIP) CONTRIBUTION CONTINUITY (HIGH)
   SCIP-style partner co-investment fell from $12.7B (FY2024) to $4.9B
   (FY2025) as early commitments matured. This model's "net capex" figures
   assume incentive/partner funding continues at a meaningful scale as
   gross capex reaccelerates toward and past $20B/yr — if that support
   fades faster than gross capex grows, real net capex (and cash burn)
   could be materially worse than modeled.

4. VALUATION / MULTIPLE COMPRESSION (VERY HIGH)
   At $123 (~$620B market cap, ~10x FY2026E revenue on a business with a
   single-digit-percent GAAP operating margin), the stock is priced far
   above every scenario this model can construct, including sensitivity
   ranges more generous than the Bull case itself (Table B). A stock priced
   this far ahead of fundamentals is exceptionally exposed to any
   disappointment in the 14A customer-commitment timeline management has
   itself flagged for 2H26/1H27.

5. COMPETITIVE SHARE LOSS RESUMING IN CORE PRODUCTS (HIGH)
   The FY2021-2023 historical collapse (Section 2) was a genuine, multi-
   year share-loss story to AMD in both CCG and DCAI, not a one-time event.
   2026's DCAI reacceleration (+59% YoY) is encouraging but early; a
   reversion to share loss in either segment would undermine even the Base
   case, which already assumes Products merely holds serve.

6. GOVERNMENT/STRATEGIC-INVESTOR ALIGNMENT RISK (MEDIUM-HIGH)
   The US government's 9.9% stake, while structured as passive, ties
   Intel's fortunes to shifting US industrial and trade policy in ways
   ordinary shareholders don't control; Nvidia's $5B investment gives one
   of Intel's most important potential customers/competitors a stake in
   Intel's outcome, which cuts both ways (alignment on foundry demand,
   but also potential conflicts given Nvidia's own TSMC relationship).

7. GAAP TAX-RATE / DTA VOLATILITY (MEDIUM)
   The FY2024 deferred-tax-asset valuation-allowance writedown has made
   Intel's reported GAAP tax rate and net income extremely noisy and
   sometimes counter-intuitive (Q2 2026: positive operating income, large
   GAAP net LOSS). This model works from operating income for exactly this
   reason, but investors reading only headline EPS could be repeatedly
   whipsawed by items unrelated to the operating turnaround.

8. EXECUTION ON A GENUINELY HARD TECHNICAL TRANSITION (MEDIUM)
   18A is in early high-volume ramp; 14A is Intel's actual bid to be
   competitive with TSMC's leading edge. Yield, power, and cost
   competitiveness at that node are not proven at scale, and Intel's own
   multi-year history (Section 2) includes real precedent for leading-edge
   node delays and cost overruns.

9. CCG/PC MARKET CYCLICALITY (MEDIUM)
   CCG (still the largest single revenue segment even in the Bull case) is
   a mature, cyclical PC market; an AI-PC refresh supercycle failing to
   materialize at the pace bulls expect would remove one of the Bull case's
   supporting legs even if Foundry executes well.

10. HEADCOUNT/RESTRUCTURING EXECUTION AND MORALE (LOW-MEDIUM)
    A ~25% core-workforce reduction through 2025, office-return mandates,
    and continued cost discipline under CEO Lip-Bu Tan carry real execution
    and retention risk for a company simultaneously trying to win back
    leading-edge process credibility with skeptical external customers.

SECTION 10 — INVESTMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

POSITIVES (WHY THE BULL COULD BE RIGHT)
  + The operating inflection is real and reported, not purely promised:
    GAAP operating income swung from a $(3.2)B loss (Q2'25) to +$1.8B
    (Q2'26); DCAI revenue +59% YoY; 18A yields visibly improving.
  + The balance sheet has genuine, large, strategically-motivated backers
    (US government, SoftBank, Nvidia) who have already put ~$15B+ of real
    capital in and have incentives to see the turnaround succeed, reducing
    near-term bankruptcy-tail risk even in a difficult scenario.
  + Foundry's own stated breakeven bar (modest external revenue, ~2027) is
    achievable even in this model's Base case, and multiple credible
    potential customers (Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, others per public
    reporting) are at least evaluating 18A/14A.
  + Management (under Lip-Bu Tan since March 2025) has shown real capital
    discipline — cancelling European fab projects, monetizing Mobileye and
    Altera stakes, cutting ~25% of core headcount — that is directionally
    the right playbook for an overextended IDM, whatever one thinks of the
    stock price.

NEGATIVES (WHY THIS IS NOT REMOTELY A MARGIN-OF-SAFETY BUY AT $123)
  - Every scenario this model can construct — including a Bull case with
    Foundry becoming a genuine ~$48B external-revenue business by 2036 —
    values Intel well below its current price. The gap in the Base case is
    not a modest 20-30% "priced for perfection" premium like some of the
    other names in this folder; it is roughly an order of magnitude.
  - The reverse DCF (Section 7, Table D) shows the market's implied
    assumptions require ~4x this model's own Bull-case terminal free cash
    flow — i.e. an outcome beyond even an optimistic read of Intel's
    stated turnaround plan.
  - Unlike AMD/NVDA/PLTR in this folder, Intel's balance sheet is a real
    liability, not an asset: $20.8B of net debt against a business that
    has not generated positive adjusted free cash flow in any of the last
    two full fiscal years.
  - The Bear case is not merely "the stock falls" — it implies the
    business's enterprise value could fall below its net debt, which in
    practice means further dilution, asset sales, or restructuring, not a
    graceful multiple contraction.
  - Sell-side consensus (average target ~$116-117, median ~$110) already
    sits BELOW today's price — this is not a case where a skeptical DCF
    disagrees with an otherwise-aligned market; even Wall Street's own
    (typically less conservative) price targets don't support $123.

BOTTOM LINE
Intel's operating turnaround under Lip-Bu Tan is real, and this model's Bull
case — a genuine external-foundry business alongside a share-holding
Products franchise — would represent a legitimate, admirable corporate
recovery from the 2021-2024 collapse. But that Bull case is worth roughly
$39/share in this build, less than a third of the current $123, and the
reverse DCF shows the market is pricing something considerably more
aggressive than even that. At $123, INTC is a story stock riding
government/strategic-investor backing, real but early operating
improvement, and a supply-constrained AI-hardware moment — not a
discounted-cash-flow value. This is a SELL/AVOID on valuation for anyone
using intrinsic value as the anchor, independent of how the underlying
turnaround actually plays out. A price in the $15-25 range (roughly the
probability-weighted-to-simple-average band) would be the first zone where
this model's framework would call the stock merely fairly priced rather
than dramatically overpriced; the Bull-case ceiling of ~$39 would need
Foundry's external-customer bet to pay off in full AND a re-rating of the
discount rate toward mega-cap-quality levels to be reached on fundamentals
alone.

Conviction: MODERATE on business-quality trajectory (real, credible,
early-stage turnaround) / VERY LOW on valuation at $123. Would need: a
decline toward the low-$20s to high-$30s (this model's Bull-case ceiling)
to even begin to be supportable on a DCF basis, OR a committed, at-scale
external 14A customer disclosure that would justify materially raising the
Bull case's own Foundry assumptions.

DISCLOSURES & LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

- This is a standard bottom-up scenario DCF, not a prediction. It does not
  independently verify management's 2027 Foundry-breakeven target or the
  reported customer-evaluation pipeline (Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, etc.) —
  those are treated as the scenario inputs the three cases vary around.
- CONSOLIDATED REVENUE, NOT SEGMENT-SUM REVENUE, IS THE FORECAST BASIS
  (Section 3) — a reader who instead grows the reported ~$17-18B/yr
  "Intel Foundry" segment-revenue line as if it were mostly external,
  addressable revenue would be double-counting against Intel Products'
  own reported revenue, and would arrive at a much higher (and, this
  analysis believes, incorrect) valuation.
- FY2024/FY2025 GAAP operating income figures are Intel's own officially
  reported numbers; some third-party financial-data aggregators show
  different ("core," ex-impairment/restructuring) operating income figures
  for these years that this model does NOT use — see INTC-HISTORICALS-
  2021-2025.md for the specific discrepancy and reasoning.
  FY2021-FY2023 figures were corroborated via direct citations to Intel's
  own press releases/10-Ks found via web search, not independently
  re-tied to the primary filings for this personal file.
- Net capex figures (Section 5/6) rely on Intel's own "government
  incentives" and "SCIP/partner contributions" disclosures for FY2024-
  FY2025 only; earlier years and the full forecast period assume a
  continuation of similar-scale support that is NOT contractually
  guaranteed at the levels assumed — see Section 9, Risk #3.
- Diluted share count (5,043M) is a single point-in-time count (Jun 27,
  2026 common shares outstanding), held flat across the ten-year forecast.
  Given the pace of dilution over the prior 18 months and continued heavy
  capex needs, this is very likely an optimistic (too-low) share count for
  the out-years — see Section 9, Risk #2.
- WACC/TGR/exit-multiple selection is a judgment call; a reader more
  optimistic about Intel's competitive position and funding durability
  could reasonably use lower discount rates, which (per Table A) would
  still leave the Base case far below the current price — closing that gap
  requires materially more aggressive REVENUE/MARGIN assumptions than
  discount-rate assumptions, per the reverse-DCF exercise in Section 7.
- All modeling works from GAAP figures with an explicit, simplified NOPAT/
  tax convention (Section 5), not management's non-GAAP framing (which
  already shows Intel solidly profitable on FY2025/Q2 2026 non-GAAP
  operating income — see Section 1/2 for the GAAP-vs-non-GAAP contrast).
- Personal, standalone research file, outside the tech-buffett Init ->
  Deep Dive -> DCF pipeline. Does NOT qualify INTC for portfolio purchase
  under CLAUDE.md / the agent ruleset, and independent of that rule, this
  analysis would not support a purchase at the current price. Not
