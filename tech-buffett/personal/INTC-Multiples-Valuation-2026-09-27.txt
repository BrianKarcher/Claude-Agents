WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
----------------------
The DCF file found Intel worth $8.34/share (base case) against a $123.00
price — a ~93% gap. That result leans heavily on this model's own judgment
calls about margin trajectory, capex intensity, and (especially) whether
Intel Foundry's tiny external-revenue base ever scales. A pure multiples
approach asks a genuinely different question: it doesn't need a single
dollar of the DCF's 2027-2036 cash-flow forecast. It just asks "if the
market values comparable semiconductor product and foundry businesses at
N times revenue, what would INTEL'S SEGMENTS be worth once they look like
[2026E / 2030E / 2036E's projected financials]?" Because Intel is not one
business but two very different ones bolted together — a profitable,
mature chip-design franchise (CCG+DCAI) and an unproven, currently-mostly-
internal foundry — this file values it SUM-OF-THE-PARTS: Intel Products
against AMD-style comps, Intel Foundry's external revenue against TSMC/
GlobalFoundries-style comps, plus the small remaining Mobileye stake at its
own direct public market value.

THE HEADLINE FINDING, BEFORE THE NUMBERS
-------------------------------------------
Like the robotaxi multiples file in this folder, this method produces
LARGER values than the DCF for every scenario — often 2-5x larger — because
applying a market multiple implicitly assumes the business keeps growing
roughly the way the comp companies do, rather than decelerating to the
DCF's ~2.5% terminal growth rate. But UNLIKE the DCF, this method's Bear
case does NOT collapse toward zero: Intel Products alone stays a real,
profitable ~28-29%-EBITDA-margin business even under bearish assumptions,
so a sum-of-the-parts view puts a floor under Intel that the consolidated
DCF's net-debt arithmetic (Section 6 of that file) does not show as
clearly. And a separate cross-check — literally applying AMD's and TSMC's
OWN CURRENT market multiples to Intel's FY2026E segments — produces
$173-175/share, ABOVE today's $123 price. That result doesn't mean INTC is
cheap; it means the gap between the DCF's verdict and the market price is
best read as "the market is pricing Intel's turnaround close to as
generously as it already prices AMD's," which is a claim about relative
comp richness, not a claim that either company is fundamentally cheap (see
Section 5).

**The key adjustment before using any of this:** AMD and TSMC are both
priced today for CONTINUED rapid AI-driven growth (AMD's forward EV/Sales
of 15.28x assumes real revenue growth; TSMC's 13.9x sits on top of a
71%-EBITDA-margin, monopoly-adjacent leading-edge franchise). Intel
Products is a slower-growing, lower-margin, share-losing-in-parts business
(Section 2 of the DCF file), and Intel Foundry's external business is
currently near-zero and completely unproven at scale. Applying AMD's or
TSMC's multiples directly, undiscounted, would import those companies'
own growth and quality premia onto a business that has not earned them yet.
This file's PRIMARY method (Section 3) therefore applies HEAVILY DISCOUNTED
versions of these multiples, scaled up only as each scenario's own
fundamentals actually justify it — and Section 5 shows, as an explicit
cross-check, what happens if you don't discount them at all.

SECTION 3 — CHOSEN MULTIPLES & VALUATION, BY SEGMENT
================================================================================

PRIMARY METHOD: EV/Revenue, applied per segment. Unlike the robotaxi file
in this folder (which preferred EV/EBITDA because Cybercab's projected
"Revenue" was fare-equivalent, not commission-equivalent, and so wasn't
comparable to Uber's reported revenue), Intel Products revenue and Intel
Foundry EXTERNAL revenue are both already true, like-for-like external
revenue — there's no gross-bookings-vs-take-rate mismatch to correct for.
EV/Revenue is used as the primary method here instead because it stays
well-defined even where segment EBITDA is negative (Intel Foundry in the
Bear case, and in the very early FY2026E year across all scenarios), which
would otherwise force the same "defer to the DCF" workaround the robotaxi
file needed for its Bear case. EV/EBITDA is shown as a secondary cross-check
wherever segment EBITDA is positive.

market's actual current multiple for a comparable but faster-growing,
higher-margin, more AI-levered chip designer — used here only as a ceiling

file's chosen EV/REVENUE multiples for Foundry (Section 3 above) were set
by triangulating between TSMC and GlobalFoundries' REVENUE multiples, while
this file's Foundry EBITDA MARGIN assumptions (5-32%, well below TSMC's
71%) were set independently and conservatively, from Intel's own stated

thin-to-modest margin, still building credibility") but it does mean the
EV/Revenue-implied value is somewhat MORE GENEROUS than a pure EV/EBITDA
method would produce at the same margin assumptions — a real methodological
tension this file resolves in favor of EV/Revenue (Section 3's opening
rationale) but flags rather than hides.

SECTION 5 — CROSS-CHECK: WHAT IF INTEL TRADED AT TODAY'S ACTUAL AMD/TSMC
MULTIPLES, UNDISCOUNTED, RIGHT NOW?
================================================================================

Section 3's chosen multiples are all explicitly discounted well below
AMD's and TSMC's actual current multiples (Section 1), on the judgment
that Intel Products and Intel Foundry haven't earned those peers' growth/
quality premium yet. It's worth showing what happens if you DON'T apply
that discount — i.e., if the market decided Intel Products deserves to
trade exactly like AMD trades today, and Intel Foundry's tiny external
business deserves to trade exactly like TSMC (or, in a second version,
like GlobalFoundries) trades today, applied to Intel's FY2026E segment
revenue (no future growth assumed — this is a right-now, static
cross-check):

  Intel Products  @ AMD's actual fwd EV/Sales (15.28x): EV = $890.8B
  Foundry-external @ TSMC's actual EV/Sales (13.90x):   EV =  $13.9B
  Foundry-external @ GlobalFoundries' EV/Sales (3.5x):  EV =   $3.5B
  Mobileye stake (direct value):                        EV =   $0.4B

  Products=AMD, Foundry=TSMC:   EV $905.1B - net debt $20.8B = Equity
                                 $884.3B / 5,043M sh = $175.35/share
  Products=AMD, Foundry=GFS:    EV $894.7B - net debt $20.8B = Equity
                                 $873.9B / 5,043M sh = $173.29/share

BOTH FIGURES ($173-175/SHARE) ARE ABOVE TODAY'S $123 PRICE.

This is an important, honest tension with this file's own headline finding
and with the DCF file's conclusion, and it needs to be read carefully
rather than either dismissed or over-interpreted:

  1. IT DOES NOT MEAN INTC IS CHEAP. It means Intel Products, if the
     market decided to value it exactly as richly as it currently values
     AMD, would alone be worth more than Intel's entire current enterprise
     value. That is a statement about how expensive AMD's OWN multiple is
     right now (see the AMD DCF file in this folder, which found AMD's
     base-case intrinsic value of ~$306/share well below its then-price of
     $459 — AMD itself was flagged as priced for a "great outcome," not a
     cheap stock) more than it is a statement that Intel deserves that
     multiple. Anchoring to an expensive comp and finding your target
     "isn't as expensive as the comp" is a classic relative-valuation trap,
     not evidence of absolute cheapness.
  2. IT DOES HELP EXPLAIN WHY THE MARKET IS PAYING $123. If enough
     market participants believe Intel Products, post-turnaround, deserves
     something approaching an AMD-style multiple — a defensible if
     aggressive view, given DCAI's 2026 AI-driven reacceleration — then
     $123 stops looking like an obviously irrational price and starts
     looking like "AMD's multiple, at a real discount, plus a free call
     option on Foundry." That is a genuinely different (and less
     dismissive) read than the DCF file's "priced for an outcome beyond
     even an optimistic turnaround" framing.
  3. IT DOES NOT MOVE THIS FILE'S OWN CENTRAL ESTIMATE. Section 3's Base
     case still uses a 3.5-4.0x multiple for Products, not AMD's 15.28x,
     because this file's own judgment is that Intel Products has not yet
     earned that multiple — lower organic growth, lower margins, a
     multi-year history of share loss (DCF Section 2), and a segment
     (CCG) that is structurally maturing rather than AI-accelerating. A
     reader who instead believes Intel Products deserves to close most of
     the gap to AMD's multiple should treat this Section 5 cross-check,
     not Section 3's Base case, as their central estimate.

SECTION 6 — WHY THIS DIVERGES FROM THE DCF, AND WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT
================================================================================

           DCF IV/share      Multiples PV/share (2036 ref.)   Ratio
  BULL        $38.71                  $87.76                  2.27x
  BASE         $8.34                  $26.35                  3.16x
  BEAR        ~$0.00                   $6.67                  n/m (DCF ~0)

Three things stand out:

**1. The Bear-case gap is the most economically meaningful one.** The DCF's
Bear case shows Intel's enterprise value falling below its net debt — a
"the equity could be wiped out" result driven by discounting a shrinking,
barely-profitable consolidated free-cash-flow stream. This multiples file's
Bear case instead shows Intel Products staying a real, ~28-29%-EBITDA-
margin business worth something even if Foundry never works — a genuine
sum-of-the-parts floor the consolidated DCF doesn't surface as clearly,
because the DCF blends Products' profits and Foundry's losses (plus
corporate costs) into one consolidated FCF line before ever getting to
net debt. Reality is probably closer to the multiples file's Bear here: a
company doesn't typically let its whole equity value go to zero while one
of its two segments keeps generating billions of profitable EBITDA — the
more likely Bear outcome is a Foundry write-down/restructuring/spin-off
that STOPS the bleeding and preserves most of Products' standalone value,
which a sum-of-the-parts framing captures more naturally than a blended
consolidated DCF.

**2. The reference-year choice matters here too, though less dramatically
than in the robotaxi file.** Bull PV is nearly flat from 2030 ($85.47) to
2036 ($87.76) because Foundry's ramp is front-loaded in this model's own
assumptions (Section 2) — most of the Bull case's value is already visible
by 2030. Base and Bear, by contrast, show DECLINING PV from 2030 to 2036
($38.47->$26.35 Base; $17.26->$6.67 Bear) — the opposite of the robotaxi
file's pattern, where later reference years were always worth more. That
happens because Base/Bear growth decelerates enough by the early 2030s that
six more years of discounting outweighs six more years of (slowing) growth
— i.e., for the less-optimistic scenarios, THIS METHOD AGREES WITH THE
DCF'S OWN LOGIC that value gets pulled forward, not pushed out, once growth
genuinely decelerates.

**3. At every reference year and scenario, multiples > DCF, sometimes by a
lot (up to 4.6x for Base at the 2030 reference year) — but the size of
that gap is itself informative.** It's largest for Base (where the DCF's
low terminal exit multiple, 12x, is the most obviously conservative input
relative to even GlobalFoundries' ~13x actual EV/EBITDA), and relatively
smaller for Bull (where the DCF already uses a more generous 16x exit
multiple). This is consistent with the DCF file's own sensitivity tables
(that file's Section 7) showing Base-case IV is fairly insensitive to exit
multiple within a reasonable range — the multiples file's much larger gap
mainly comes from applying a GROWING multiple (Section 3's multiples expand
over time in Base/Bull) rather than the DCF's single fixed exit multiple,
plus this file crediting Foundry's revenue growth directly rather than
running it through the DCF's margin/capex/D&A machinery.

**Bottom line for using this file:** don't average this file's numbers with
the DCF's and call it a synthesized target — as the robotaxi file's own
Section 5 puts it, that would hide a real disagreement about growth
persistence and segment structure behind false precision. Use the DCF
(DCF-INTC-2026-09-27.txt) for the "can Intel actually fund this and keep
the lights on" question — its explicit FCF path and net-debt bridge are the
right tool for that, especially given Intel's real leverage (unlike the
net-cash AMD/NVDA/PLTR names in this folder). Use THIS file for "what would
a market willing to pay growth-company multiples value the pieces at once
they get there" — and use Section 5's cross-check, specifically, to
understand that today's $123 price is better explained as "the market is
pricing Intel's turnaround close to AMD's own (rich) multiple" than as
outright detached from any comp-based logic. Both files agree the price
is NOT supported by a conservative-to-moderate reading of Intel's own
projected fundamentals; they disagree on how large and how explicable the
gap is.

DISCLOSURES & LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
- Comp multiples (AMD, TSMC, GlobalFoundries, Mobileye) were pulled live via
  web search on Sept 27-28, 2026 from secondary aggregators (stockanalysis.
  com, GuruFocus, multiples.vc, valueinvesting.io, various financial news),
  not directly from primary filings for this file — treat as directionally
  right, not decimal-precise. AMD's TTM EV/EBITDA (106.74x) and some
  GlobalFoundries figures were flagged as noisy/inconsistent across sources
  and were either excluded (AMD) or averaged/rounded (GlobalFoundries).
- Segment EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA-margin assumptions (Section 3) are this
  file's own judgment calls, informed by but not mechanically derived from
  the comps — there is no formula that converts "AMD trades at 15.28x" into
  "Intel Products base case should trade at 3.5-4.0x"; that gap is a
  reasoned discount for lower growth, lower margin, and a multi-year share-
  loss history, not a derived number. A reader with a different view on how
  much of that gap should close (see Section 5) will get a different answer
  using the same underlying revenue figures.
- Segment EBITDA margins (used only for the EV/EBITDA cross-check figures
  shown alongside each EV/Revenue valuation) are this file's own estimates,
  not derived from Intel's actual segment-level D&A disclosures (which
  aren't broken out at the granularity this file would need) — treat the
  implied EV/EBITDA multiples as a rough sanity check, not a precise
  secondary valuation.
- Mobileye's exact current ownership percentage and share count held by
  Intel were not precisely pulled for this file (sources conflicted between
  an ~88% figure as of 2025 and a much smaller ~50M-share/~$400M position
  by mid-to-late 2026) — the direct stake value used is approximate and, in
  any case, immaterial to every total in this file.
- Reference years (2026E/2030E/2036E) match the DCF file's own Section 4
  structure for comparability, not because they're the "correct" points at
  which a market would actually re-rate Intel's segments — Section 6 shows
  this choice matters less here than in the robotaxi file, but still
  matters.
- Personal, standalone research file, outside the tech-buffett Init -> Deep
  Dive -> DCF pipeline. Does NOT qualify INTC for portfolio purchase under
