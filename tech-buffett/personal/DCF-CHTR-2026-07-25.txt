WHAT CHANGED SINCE THE JUNE 26 MODEL
-------------------------------------
1. STOCK PRICE COLLAPSED. Shares closed 7/24 at ~$123.29 (a 52-week low) and
   traded as low as ~$111 in the following premarket session — down ~40-45%
   from the ~$210-225 level the prior model was benchmarked against. This is
   the single biggest input change. Nothing else in this update comes close.
2. Q2 results confirm the deceleration is not stabilizing. Revenue -1.7% YoY
   (vs -0.6% for FY25), Adjusted EBITDA -4.3% YoY (worse than Q1's implied
   trajectory), Internet losses of 172K (worse than -120K in Q1). Management
   cut FY26 standalone EBITDA guidance to "around -1% YoY ex-transition costs."
3. Leverage ticked UP, not down: 4.18x net debt/LTM EBITDA at 6/30/26 vs
   4.15x at 3/31/26 — EBITDA is shrinking faster than debt is being repaid,
   even as management executes real deleveraging (see #4).
4. Real deleveraging actions are underway: $1.2B of debt repurchased in the
   open market in Q2 for ~$1.0B cash (~$243M gain on extinguishment), plus a
   newly announced capped exchange offer targeting $20B par of discounted
   investment-grade debt. Leverage target firmed up to a flat 3.5x long-term
   (from a prior 3.5-4.0x range framing).
5. Cox transaction terms refined slightly: cash consideration now specified
   as $3.5B (Equity Sale) + $650M (Contribution) = $4.15B total cash, and
   assumed Cox net debt narrowed to ~$12.4B (from ~$12.6B estimated). The
   $6.0B convertible preferred (6.875%, $477.41 conversion price) is
   unchanged. Pro forma shares at close now guided to ~177M (down from 179M).
6. Share buybacks are PAUSED through Q3 2026 (financing/liability management
   priority ahead of Cox close), expected to restart in Q4. Remaining board
   buyback authority was down to just $365M as of 6/30/26 before the pause.
7. Management explicitly disclosed combined post-Cox 2027 figures for the
   first time: ~$67B revenue, ~$28B EBITDA, ~37M customers, ~70M passings.
   This closely validates (rather than requires re-deriving) the prior
   model's 2027E assumptions.
8. NEW DISCLOSURE: the 10-Q flags that if the depressed stock price persists,
   Charter may need to run a goodwill/franchise impairment test. This was not
   a live risk in the June model and is now explicitly on the table.
9. Management's own FCF-yield framing moved with the stock: on the Q2 call
   they now cite "a bit over 2x" FCF multiple / "nearly 50%" FCF yield using
   2028 run-rate CapEx assumptions (vs "~3.8x / ~25%" cited on the Q1 call at
   the higher share price) — same underlying capex-cliff logic, mechanically
   re-priced.

NET EFFECT: the underlying operating model is only modestly worse (EBITDA
guide trimmed, sub losses continuing), while deleveraging is running slightly
AHEAD of the prior model's net-debt assumption. But the equity has been
repriced by ~40%+. The result: base-case intrinsic value is roughly FLAT to
the June estimate (~$670-680/share vs ~$646/share), while the market-implied
discount rate has jumped from ~12% to ~13-14%. Whether that gap is opportunity
or the market pricing in something this model doesn't capture (accelerating
impairment risk, an EBITDA guide that keeps sliding) is the central question
— see Section 9.

SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

Charter is the #2 US cable operator by subscribers (Spectrum brand), serving
homes across 41 states via a converged fiber/HFC network. Revenue mix
(H1 2026 actual):

  - Internet:                  $11,628M  42.9% of total  -2.3% YoY
  - Mobile service:              $2,147M   7.9%          +17.0% YoY
  - Video (linear, declining):   $6,401M  23.6%           -9.4% YoY
  - Voice (VoIP, declining):       $669M   2.5%           -4.7% YoY
  - Commercial (SMB + Enterprise): $3,704M  13.7%          +1.2% YoY
  - Advertising + Other:         $2,574M   9.5%          mixed (political tailwind)
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  Total H1 2026 Revenue:        $27,123M                  -1.4% YoY

The thesis is unchanged in structure, only in degree of conviction:
  1. Broadband is near-monopoly infrastructure in most of Charter's footprint
     — a fiber-powered HFC network with no viable fourth wire at scale.
  2. The CapEx supercycle (network evolution + rural buildout) is guided to
     end in 2027-28: standalone CapEx from ~$12.1B (LTM) to <$8B by 2028 —
     management now frames this as ~$30/share of FCF (up from ~$28/share
     cited last quarter, purely because the share count keeps shrinking).
  3. The Cox acquisition adds ~$12.5B of revenue, ~6.5M customers, and
     $800M+ (management now says "conservative," likely trending to $1B) in
     run-rate synergies. Expected to close mid-to-late August 2026.

Key pending events (as of July 2026):
  - Cox transaction close: management now targets mid-to-late August 2026
    (delayed from the "summer 2026" framing in June; California PUC process
    is resolved per the July call, hiring of 1,000+ frontline Cox-market
    staff has begun).
  - Liberty Broadband merger: closing simultaneously with Cox.
  - Nick Jeffrey (ex-Vodafone) joins as COO September 1, 2026.
  - Buybacks paused through Q3 2026; expected to restart Q4 2026.
  - New $20B capped exchange offer for discounted IG debt, launched
    July 24, 2026 (outcome/participation rate not yet known).

SECTION 4 — DCF ASSUMPTIONS (UPDATED)
================================================================================

MODELING APPROACH (unchanged methodology from June model)
- Standalone Charter modeled for 2026 (Cox now expected to close Aug 2026)
- Combined Charter + Cox modeled from 2027 onward
- 10-year explicit forecast period: 2026-2035
- Terminal value via both perpetuity growth and EV/EBITDA exit multiple
- FCFF = NOPAT + D&A - CapEx (ΔNWC ~zero; cable is broadly WC-neutral)
- NOPAT = EBIT x (1 - 22% tax rate)

REVENUE ASSUMPTIONS (2026E revised down slightly; 2027E now anchored to
management's own disclosed combined figure rather than derived)
2025A: $54,774M (actual)
2026E: $54,100M (-1.2%) — standalone; H1 actual ran -1.4%, modest H2
  improvement assumed from political ad revenue and the internet cost
  pass-through management flagged for late July/August
2027E: $67,000M — MATCHES management's own disclosed combined-entity figure
  almost exactly (this used to be a derived estimate; it's now a direct
  management data point, which increases confidence in this line)
2028E-2035E: UNCHANGED from the June model ($68,500M -> $79,200M, 2.0-2.3%
  annual growth) — no new information changes the long-run growth assumptions

EBITDA MARGIN ASSUMPTIONS (2026E revised down to reflect the Q2 miss)
2025A: 41.5% (actual)
2026E: 41.2% (REVISED DOWN from 41.6% in the June model) — H1 actual margin
  was only 40.9%; management's own guide is "FY26 EBITDA ex-transition down
  ~1% YoY," which nets to roughly this level once transition costs (running
  above plan, per the 10-Q) are included
2027E: 41.8% — closely matches management's own ~$28B EBITDA / ~$67B revenue
  disclosure (41.8% implied margin) rather than being independently assumed
2028E-2035E: UNCHANGED from the June model (42.5% -> 45.0%)

CAPEX ASSUMPTIONS — REAFFIRMED, UNCHANGED
2026E: $11,400M (21.1% of revenue) — management guidance unchanged
2027E: $13,400M (20.0%) — Cox integration capex + continued network evolution
2028E: $10,275M (15.0%) — the capex cliff; management explicitly reaffirmed
  this on the July call ("our outlook for a significant reduction in capital
  expenditures has not changed")
2029E-2035E: UNCHANGED (13.5% -> 12.0% of revenue, tapering to steady state)

D&A: 15.5% of revenue (unchanged assumption; H1 2026 actual ran slightly
  higher at 16.25% of revenue, expected to normalize toward the assumption
  as purchase-accounting effects and network-evolution capitalization settle)

TAX RATE: 22% effective on EBIT (cash taxes still guided at $500-800M for
  FY26, materially below book taxes due to bonus depreciation — unchanged)

WACC DERIVATION — recomputed given the much lower stock price
Cost of debt: 5.2% (management-stated, unchanged)
After-tax cost of debt: 5.2% x (1 - 22%) = 4.06%
Asset (unlevered) beta: 0.60 (unchanged, sector assumption)
Capital structure at CURRENT market price (~$123, pro forma basis):
  Pro forma net debt ~$110B; pro forma market cap ~177M sh x $123 = ~$21.8B
  D/EV = 83.5%, E/EV = 16.5% (MORE levered on a market-value basis than the
  June model's 75/25 split — because the equity fell much further than debt)
Levered beta = 0.60 x (1 + 0.78 x 83.5/16.5) = 0.60 x 4.95 = 2.97
Cost of equity = 4.5% + 2.97 x 5.5% = 20.8%
CAPM-mechanical WACC = 0.835 x 4.06% + 0.165 x 20.8% = 6.8%
(Nearly identical to the June model's ~6.9% mechanical WACC — the
Modigliani-Miller offset: higher market leverage lowers the weighted average
even as the cost of equity itself rises. This is a reminder that the
mechanical WACC is not very informative here; execution/leverage risk needs
to be layered on judgmentally, same as before.)

Base case WACC used in this model: 9.0% (unchanged from June, for the same
conservatism/execution-risk-premium reasoning)

MARKET-IMPLIED DISCOUNT RATE — recomputed at the new price
Solving for the WACC that reconciles this model's FCFF stream with a ~$123
share price and ~164.4M adjusted diluted shares: approximately 13.0-13.5%
(UP from ~12% implied in the June model). The market has gotten meaningfully
more pessimistic about Charter's cost of capital / execution risk over the
past month — more than the ~1 percentage point of fundamental deterioration
in this quarter's results would obviously justify on its own.

SECTION 7 — SCENARIO ANALYSIS (UPDATED)
================================================================================

BULL CASE (~$970/share; 8.0% WACC, 2.5% TGR)
  - Broadband subscriber losses stabilize by 2027 (no evidence of this yet —
    Q2 losses of 172K were WORSE than Q1's 120K, so this remains aspirational)
  - Mobile lines reach 20M+ by 2029; MVNO economics improve vs Verizon
  - Cox integration delivers $1B+ in synergies (management is now guiding
    toward this, an upgrade from "$800M+")
  - CapEx cliff happens exactly as guided; $20B exchange offer succeeds and
    accelerates deleveraging meaningfully beyond the 3-year target
  - Stock re-rates as leverage and impairment concerns fade

BASE CASE (~$671/share; 9.0% WACC, 2.0% TGR)
  - Broadband losses continue near current pace through 2027-28, then moderate
  - Mobile grows to 16-18M lines by 2030
  - Cox delivers the guided $800M-$1B in synergies; Spectrum rebrand in Cox
    markets proceeds on the delayed (Aug 2026) timeline without major hiccups
  - CapEx cliff happens as guided; combined entity CapEx ~$10B by 2028
  - Leverage declines toward 3.5x within 3 years of Cox close (guided)
  - EBITDA margin expands from ~41% to ~44.5% by 2031 despite the FY26 dip

BEAR CASE (~$115-135/share; 12.5-13.5% WACC, 1.5% TGR — i.e., roughly where
the stock trades today)
  - Internet losses keep accelerating (Q2 already worse than Q1) rather than
    stabilizing; ARPU stays under pressure from competitive promotions
  - EBITDA guidance gets cut again in Q3/Q4, as it already was this quarter
  - Cox integration disappoints or the Aug 2026 close slips further
  - A sustained low stock price forces a goodwill/franchise IMPAIRMENT

  - The $20B exchange offer under-subscribes, slowing the leverage glide path
  - Note: this is essentially where the market is pricing Charter TODAY —

SECTION 8 — KEY RISKS (UPDATED)
================================================================================

1. LEVERAGE (HIGH, ticked up this quarter)
   Standalone leverage rose to 4.18x (from 4.15x at Q1) despite real debt
   paydown, because LTM EBITDA is shrinking. Pro forma post-Cox leverage is
   guided to ~3.9x by end of Q3 2026, contingent on the new $20B exchange
   offer succeeding — an unproven, just-announced program.

2. NEW: GOODWILL / FRANCHISE IMPAIRMENT RISK (MEDIUM, newly disclosed)
   The Q2 10-Q explicitly states that if the depressed stock price persists,
   Charter "may need to evaluate whether this market perception represents a
   sustained decline in the market value of our business and perform a
   quantitative impairment assessment" on franchises ($67.5B carrying value)
   and goodwill ($29.7B carrying value). This is a new, material disclosure
   that did not exist in the June model. A large non-cash impairment would
   not change the DCF's cash-flow-based intrinsic value, but would likely
   further damage market sentiment and could pressure debt covenants
   indirectly via rating-agency reactions.

3. BROADBAND SUBSCRIBER LOSSES — ACCELERATING, NOT STABILIZING (HIGH)
   Q2 Internet loss of 172K was WORSE than Q1's 120K loss, contradicting the
   "stabilizing" narrative management offered on the Q1 call. Management now
   attributes part of this to a deliberate pullback of overly aggressive Q1
   retention offers (a self-inflicted, correctable issue per the CEO) plus
   ongoing fixed-wireless/fiber competition. The next 1-2 quarters are the
   key tell on whether this was temporary or a trend re-acceleration.

4. COX EXECUTION RISK (MEDIUM-HIGH, timeline slipped again)
   Close now targeted mid-to-late August 2026, later than the "summer 2026"
   framing from June. Transition expenses are "coming in a bit higher than
   expected" per the CFO (though total transition cost + capex is still
   expected at or better than plan). Frontline hiring in Cox markets could
   not begin until closing visibility improved, compressing the ramp window.

5. MOBILE MARGINS (MEDIUM, unchanged)
   Spectrum Mobile remains a Verizon MVNO. 12.5M lines now, WiFi/CBRS offload
   at 87-88% (temporarily dipped from ~89% this quarter due to a deliberate
   product change, per CEO explanation on the call — not a negative signal).

6. VIDEO (LOW/IMPROVING)
   Video losses improved sharply YoY (-21K vs -80K a year ago) on seamless
   app bundling and Xumo — one of the few clearly positive trend lines this
   quarter.

7. REGULATORY / POLITICAL (LOW-MEDIUM, unchanged)

8. CAPEX OVERRUN / TIMING RISK (MEDIUM, reaffirmed but unproven)
   Management explicitly reaffirmed the capex-cliff guidance on the July
   call with no change, which is reassuring, but the guidance itself has not
   yet been tested — 2026 is still a peak-spend year.

9. SATELLITE / LEO (LOW near-term, unchanged) — no meaningful share loss
   observed to Starlink per management, including in subsidized rural areas.

10. NEW: BUYBACK PAUSE REMOVES A NEAR-TERM SUPPORT/ACCRETION LEVER (LOW-MEDIUM)
    With buybacks paused through Q3 2026 at exactly the moment the stock is
    at a 52-week low, Charter is foregoing what would (on this model's
    numbers) be highly accretive repurchases, in favor of deleveraging
    ahead of the Cox close. Defensible capital allocation given the pending
    financing needs, but it means no repurchase-driven per-share support
    into a falling stock for at least one more quarter.

WHAT'S GENUINELY NEW AND POSITIVE
  + Deleveraging is real and ahead of the June model's net-debt assumption:

  + CapEx-cliff guidance was explicitly reaffirmed with zero change
  + Video trends continue to improve materially
  + Combined synergy guidance moved up ("$800M+" -> trending toward "$1B")

WHAT'S GENUINELY NEW AND NEGATIVE
  - Internet losses got worse quarter over quarter, not better — the
    "stabilizing" narrative from Q1 did not hold in Q2
  - FY26 EBITDA guidance was cut
  - A new, explicit impairment-risk disclosure exists that did not exist a
    quarter ago
  - Leverage ticked up on a trailing basis despite real paydown
  - Cox close timeline slipped again (June: "summer 2026" -> July: "mid-to-
    late August")
  - Buybacks paused at the worst possible moment optically (a 52-week low)

BOTTOM LINE
This model's base case says Charter is worth roughly what it said a month
ago (~$670-680/share) because the two changes that matter most for a
cash-flow valuation — deleveraging progress and reaffirmed capex guidance —
were both modestly POSITIVE. But the stock is down ~40%+ on a quarter that
was, on balance, modestly negative operationally (worse sub losses, a
guidance cut, a new impairment disclosure). That gap is either:
  (a) a market overreaction to a single disappointing quarter layered on
      top of an already-hated, highly levered stock, or
  (b) the market correctly pricing in that "worse sub losses two quarters
      running" plus "leverage still rising" plus "impairment now on the
      table" adds up to more than this model's linear, guidance-anchored
      assumptions capture — i.e., a regime change the DCF's smooth 10-year
      trajectory is not built to detect.

Given that Internet losses just accelerated for a second straight quarter
against management's own prior "stabilizing" claim, (b) deserves real
weight. The single most important thing to watch next quarter is simply
whether Internet net adds improve or deteriorate further — that data point
will do more to resolve this valuation gap than any model refinement.

Conviction level on base case: MODERATE (down from MODERATE-HIGH in June —
not because the numbers changed much, but because management's own prior
"stabilization" call was wrong this quarter, which should lower confidence
in taking their forward-looking commentary at face value until it's proven
out again).

Position sizing: unchanged guidance — size for the leverage risk, monitor:
  - Internet subscriber trend (the key swing factor right now)
  - Cox close (mid-to-late August) and post-close ARPU trajectory
  - $20B exchange offer participation/results
  - Whether an impairment charge is actually taken
  - Q3/Q4 buyback restart and pace
