WHAT THIS FILE IS (READ FIRST)
-----------------------------
This is a CONDITIONAL valuation. The user asked: "assume we reach these
numbers by 2030 in the Data Center segment for AI hardware TAM" — i.e. take
Lisa Su's Advancing AI 2026 framing at face value:

  - Data center AI ACCELERATOR TAM ........ ~$1.4 trillion by 2030
    (up from the "$500B by 2028" call made a year earlier; >45%/yr growth)
  - Server CPU TAM ........................ ~$200-220 billion by 2030
    (up from ~$60B a year earlier; >50%/yr growth)
  - Combined "high-performance & AI compute" TAM ~$2 trillion by 2030,
    ~40%/yr growth

This model holds the $1.4T accelerator TAM FIXED across all three scenarios
(per the instruction) and varies only AMD's SHARE of it. The server CPU TAM
is treated as management's number in the Bull case and haircut in Base/Bear,
since the instruction fixed only the AI-hardware TAM. Everything downstream
— margins, capex, discount rates, terminal value — follows the same
conventions as the other models in this folder (NVDA 2026-08-27, PLTR
2026-08-29, AMD 2026-07-09, CHTR, HIMS).

Because the TAM is an input, not an output, the intrinsic values here are
STRUCTURALLY HIGHER than the July 9, 2026 AMD model in this same folder
(that one used generic top-down growth rates and produced Base ~$167 / Bull
~$357). The gap between the two files is almost entirely the TAM assumption
plus two strong quarters of actual execution since — NOT a change of view
about the business. Treat this file as "if the AI-hardware TAM Lisa Su
describes actually shows up, and AMD executes, what is AMD worth?" — and
treat that "if" as doing a very large amount of work.

CONTEXT / SANITY CHECK: a $1.4T accelerator TAM in 2030 is ~3.5-4x the
current (2026) run-rate of the entire merchant + custom AI-silicon market.
NVIDIA data center alone is already ~$350-380B annualized. For the pool to
reach $1.4T by 2030, AI infrastructure capex has to compound at ~45%/yr for
four more years with no digestion pause — a pace no hardware cycle in
history has sustained for that long. This model does NOT independently
endorse the $1.4T figure; it adopts it as the user's stated premise and
tests what AMD is worth conditional on it. Every intrinsic value in this
file inherits the fragility of that premise.

TAKEAWAYS FROM THE SEGMENT VIEW
  - Even in the "TAM-reached" world, ~30-40% of AMD's revenue in 2030 is
    NOT AI accelerator revenue: it is server CPU (part traditional), PC
    CPUs, consoles, and embedded/FPGA. AMD is an AI-levered company, not a
    pure-play AI-accelerator company, and the non-AI base both cushions the
    downside and dilutes the upside relative to a pure-play like NVIDIA.
  - The single biggest swing factor is AMD's Instinct share of the $1.4T
    pool: 3.3% vs 8.8% is a ~$77B revenue difference in 2030 alone — larger
    than AMD's entire Data Center segment today.
  - "AI-attached EPYC" is a real category (Lisa Su's own rationale for the
    CPU TAM raise is agentic-AI CPU orchestration), but it is an analyst
    estimate here; if one insists that only Instinct counts as "AI
    hardware," AMD's AI revenue in the 2030 Base case is ~$77B, ~45% of the
    company, not ~68%.

SECTION 7 — SCENARIO ANALYSIS
================================================================================

BULL CASE (~$824/share; 10.0% WACC, 3.5% TGR, 26x exit)
  - The $1.4T accelerator TAM materializes on schedule; AMD Instinct reaches
    ~8-9% share (~$123B) by 2030 as MI450/Helios and successors ship
    competitively at RACK scale, not just chip level.
  - EPYC reaches ~44% of a full $220B server CPU TAM (~$97B).
  - The ~14 GW of committed capacity (Meta/OpenAI/Anthropic/Oracle) converts
    to revenue with follow-on expansion and new AI-native customers.
  - GAAP operating margin scales to ~31% as amortization runs off and mix
    shifts hard toward Data Center.
  - 2036 revenue ~$567B. This is a genuine "AMD becomes a top-3 global
    company" scenario — the model can produce the number, but the reader
    should weight the plausibility of a semiconductor challenger sustaining
    45-55% growth for four consecutive years accordingly.

BASE CASE (~$306/share; 11.5% WACC, 3.0% TGR, 20x exit)
  - The TAM largely appears, but AMD holds only ~5.5% accelerator share
    (~$77B) as NVIDIA's CUDA moat and hyperscaler custom silicon absorb the
    bulk of the $1.4T; EPYC ~41.5% of a haircut $165B CPU TAM (~$68.5B).
  - Helios execution is real but bumpier than guided; client/gaming grow
    modestly off a memory-cost-pressured 2026.
  - GAAP operating margin plateaus at 25%.
  - 2036 revenue ~$356B (~7.2x FY2026E) — still an extraordinary outcome,
    and still ~33% below today's price.

BEAR CASE (~$90/share; 13.5% WACC, 2.0% TGR, 14x exit)
  - The $1.4T TAM still shows up (fixed premise) but AMD's accelerator share
    stalls at ~3.3% (~$46B): ROCm never reaches CUDA parity, and custom
    ASICs (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft MAIA, Meta MTIA, an OpenAI
    in-house part) take the "everything that isn't NVIDIA" bucket.
  - Server CPU TAM growth disappoints (~$130B, not $220B); Intel's
    competitive position stabilizes.
  - Memory/HBM cost inflation compresses client and gaming; China export
    policy tightens further.
  - Helios slips on yield/power/supply, pushing the AI-margin inflection out
    1-2 years.
  - The Meta AND OpenAI warrants vest at scale into a disappointing
    fundamental backdrop — realized dilution on top of a weak business.

SECTION 8 — KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. THE TAM PREMISE ITSELF (VERY HIGH — this model's foundational risk)
   Every intrinsic value here is conditional on a ~$1.4T 2030 accelerator
   TAM — a ~45%/yr compound off the current market for four more years with
   no digestion. If AI infrastructure capex pauses or the TAM lands at, say,
   $700-900B instead, the Base case revenue path is roughly halved and the
   Base IV falls well below $200 even before any share or margin
   disappointment. This is not a modeling nuance; it is the whole model.

2. AMD'S SHARE OF THE POOL (VERY HIGH)
   Instinct at 3.3% vs 8.8% of $1.4T is a ~$77B revenue swing in 2030 alone.
   AMD's realized accelerator share depends on ROCm/software parity, rack-
   scale execution vs NVIDIA's Rubin cadence, and how much of each
   hyperscaler's spend is diverted to that customer's own custom silicon.

3. HYPERSCALER CUSTOM SILICON (HIGH)
   AMD's largest potential customers (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta,
   OpenAI) are simultaneously building their own accelerators. The merchant
   "not-NVIDIA" bucket AMD is competing for may be structurally smaller than
   the headline TAM implies — custom ASICs are the real competition for
   AMD's share, more so than NVIDIA.

4. VALUATION / MULTIPLE COMPRESSION (HIGH)
   At $459 (~$762B market cap, ~15x FY2026E revenue) the stock is priced at
   ~1.5x this model's Base IV and ~1.24x the probability-weighted estimate.
   A stock priced near a bullish fair value reacts violently to any data
   point that isn't bullish.

5. THE META + OPENAI WARRANTS (HIGH)
   Combined ~320M shares (~16% of the count) at ~$0.01 strike, vesting on
   the customers' own purchase milestones and AMD's stock price. This hands
   two of AMD's largest customers significant pricing leverage while AMD
   shareholders absorb the dilution. Not in the share count used above.

6. GROSS-MARGIN DILUTION FROM THE MI450 RAMP (MEDIUM, near-term)
   Management has flagged MI450/Helios as below corporate-average gross
   margin during the H2 2026 ramp — a real quarter-to-quarter risk to
   consensus through 2027, already partly reflected in the ~17% FY2026E and
   19% 2027E Base margins.

7. MEMORY / HBM COST INFLATION (MEDIUM)
   An industry-wide dynamic (hits NVIDIA and every AI-infra buyer too).
   Management flagged H2 2026 client/gaming demand risk specifically from
   rising DRAM/HBM prices; a sustained shortage could also cap the pace of
   accelerator deployment industry-wide.

8. CYCLICALITY OF THE NON-AI BASE (MEDIUM)
   Client + Gaming (~32% of FY2025 revenue, ~33% of FY2026E) are cyclical
   PC/console businesses. A consumer/enterprise IT slowdown hits these
   before the committed-capacity Data Center segment.

9. CHINA / EXPORT CONTROLS (MEDIUM)
   Q1 2026 data center AI revenue actually declined sequentially on "the
   China transition"; the FY2025 10-K carried ~$440M of MI308
   export-control-related charges. A real, non-hypothetical swing factor.

10. EXECUTION ON A GENUINELY HARD PRODUCT (MEDIUM)
    Helios is a rack-scale, multi-generation, co-engineered platform (GPU +
    EPYC + networking, integrated down to named deployments). Far more
    points of failure than a standalone chip launch.

SECTION 9 — INVESTMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

POSITIVES (WHY THE BASE/BULL COULD BE RIGHT)
  + The near-term inflection is already reported, not promised: Data Center
    segment operating margin swung from a loss to 31%+ in a year; corporate
    GAAP operating margin 7.4% -> 17.3%; FCF positive and growing.
  + Q2 2026 Data Center revenue +107% YoY to $6.7B, with Q3 guided to ~$13B
    total (+41% YoY) — the ramp is visibly accelerating into H2.
  + ~14 GW of multi-year committed accelerator capacity across
    Meta/OpenAI/Anthropic/Oracle gives real revenue visibility that did not
    exist 12 months ago.
  + EPYC server share continues to compound, and a real (if hard to size)
    slice of CPU demand is now AI-driven — a second growth vector
    independent of Instinct.
  + Fabless model: FCF conversion on incremental revenue is structurally
    high; net-cash balance sheet (~$9.9B) means zero balance-sheet risk.
  + Unlike the July model, the Bull case ($824) now sits well above the
    market — there IS a scenario in which today's buyer does very well.

NEGATIVES (WHY THIS IS NOT A MARGIN-OF-SAFETY BUY AT $459)
  - The Base case ($306) and the probability-weighted estimate ($371) are
    both below the current price. The market is already paying for the
    TAM-reached premise plus solid AMD execution; the upside from here
    requires the Bull case, i.e. AMD becoming a top-3 global company.
  - The whole model rests on a $1.4T 2030 accelerator TAM this analysis does
    NOT independently endorse — it is the user's stated premise. Strip 30%
    off that TAM and the Base IV is closer to $200.
  - ~16% unmodeled dilution from the Meta + OpenAI warrants would take the
    probability-weighted estimate to ~$312 — roughly 32% below the price.
  - Higher variance than the portfolio's actual holdings: AMD's 3-year path
    depends on a hard multi-generation ramp against a far larger competitor
    AND on custom-silicon programs not eating its addressable share.

BOTTOM LINE
Give AMD the entire $1.4T accelerator TAM and a full server-CPU TAM raise,
let it execute, and you can build a Bull case worth ~$824 — but the central
estimate is ~$306 and the probability-weighted value is ~$371, both below
today's $459. So even in the generous "TAM-reached" framing the user asked
for, AMD at $459 is roughly fair-to-rich, not cheap: you are paying today
for a great outcome and hoping for a spectacular one. The stock becomes
genuinely interesting on this framework in the low-$300s (Base-case value)
and a high-conviction buy in the low-$200s (where even a haircut TAM works).
At $459 it is a HOLD/PASS on valuation, notwithstanding a materially better
business than the July analysis described.

Conviction: MODERATE-HIGH on business quality / LOW on valuation at $459.
Would need: a pullback toward ~$300 or below, OR 2-3 quarters of clean
Helios execution that justifies moving the Bull discount rate below 10% and
shifting probability weight from Bear to Base.
