SECTION 1 — BUSINESS SNAPSHOT
================================================================================

NVIDIA designs GPUs and full-stack AI infrastructure (compute, networking,
and the CUDA software ecosystem) and is the dominant supplier of AI training
and inference silicon. Starting in fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended April 2026),
NVIDIA collapsed its historical five-way reporting (Data Center, Gaming,
Professional Visualization, Automotive, OEM & Other) into two segments:

  - Data Center: GPUs, networking (NVLink, InfiniBand, Ethernet), and
    systems for AI training/inference — now ~92-93% of total revenue
  - Edge Computing: gaming (GeForce), professional visualization
    (workstations/RTX), automotive, and robotics — the former "everything
    else" bucket, now folded into one line

Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended July 26, 2026) revenue mix:
  - Data Center:      $89.0B   92.5%   (~+117% YoY per company disclosure)
  - Edge Computing:     $7.2B    7.5%   +27% YoY, +13% QoQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Total Q2 FY2027 revenue: $96.2B, +106% YoY, +18% QoQ — beat the company's
  own $91.0B guide by ~5.7% and consensus (~$92.2B) by ~4.5%

Customer mix disclosed on the call: hyperscale customers ~$49B of Data
Center revenue (+13% QoQ, Blackwell-driven); "ACIE" (AI Compute
Infrastructure & Enterprise — NeoClouds, industrial, sovereign AI,
enterprise) ~$40B (+25% QoQ, +138% YoY), now roughly HALF of total revenue
and growing at a reported ~100% annualized rate — a genuine and welcome
diversification away from a handful of hyperscaler contracts, though still
concentrated in AI-capex-cycle demand broadly.

The thesis in three sentences:
  1. NVIDIA is not just riding the AI infrastructure buildout, it effectively
     IS the AI infrastructure buildout for training and most inference
     workloads today — management framed the revenue opportunity per
     gigawatt of deployed compute at ~$18B (Hopper) -> ~$25B (Blackwell) ->
     ~$40B (Vera Rubin, next-gen), a monetization curve moving up and to
     the right with each platform generation.
  2. Growth is currently supply-constrained, not demand-constrained:
     management stated supply supports roughly 70% growth while demand
     exceeds that — the opposite problem of most businesses, and the reason
     this quarter (again) beat a raised guide.
  3. The business generates extraordinary operating leverage (Q2 FY2027
     GAAP operating margin ~66%, gross margin 75.0%) even as it scales
     capex-light relative to its hyperscaler customers — NVIDIA's own FY2026
     capex was just $6.0B against $215.9B of revenue (2.8%), a structurally
     different capital intensity than the companies buying its chips.

Key near-term dynamics (as of the Aug 26, 2026 print):
  - Q3 FY2027 guidance: revenue $108.0B ±2% (+12.3% QoQ at midpoint), GAAP
    and non-GAAP gross margin 74.0% ±50bps — down from Q2's 75.0%.
  - Management guided Q4 FY2027 gross margin to BOTTOM at 71-72%, citing
    rising memory (DRAM/HBM) costs — a real, quantified near-term margin
    headwind shared with every other AI-hardware buyer of memory.
  - A press summary referenced management guiding to roughly 70% revenue
    growth for FY2028 (next fiscal year) — consistent with the "supply
    supports ~70% growth" framing above; this model treats 70% as closer to
    a supply-ceiling/Bull-case anchor for FY2028E rather than the Base case
    (see Section 4).
  - China: NVIDIA's revenue share from China fell to a single-digit
    percentage of total revenue due to export controls; recent H200 export
    approvals were cited as a potential (not yet realized at scale) re-entry
    path. This model does NOT assume a material China recovery in any
    scenario except partially in the Bull case.
  - Balance sheet: as of July 26, 2026, NVIDIA held $56.6B of cash + cash
    equivalents + marketable DEBT securities, plus a separately disclosed
    $42.8B of marketable EQUITY securities (strategic stakes in AI-ecosystem
    companies, e.g. OpenAI/xAI/CoreWeave-type investments) — the latter is
    treated as a memo item, NOT added to the DCF equity bridge (see
    Section 4), a deliberately conservative choice. Total debt, however, is
    NOT flat: it rose from $8.5B (Jan 25, 2026) to $33.4B (Jul 26, 2026), a
    real new bond issuance during H1 FY2027 (likely financing the AI
    infrastructure buildout and/or the strategic equity stakes above) —
    net cash used in this model is $23.2B, not the ~$48.1B a flat-debt
    assumption would imply.
  - Cash-flow nuance worth flagging up front: Q2 FY2027 free cash flow was
    $21.4B against $59.7B of GAAP net income — a much lower conversion
    ratio (~36%) than NVIDIA's historical norm, driven by inventory build
    and supplier prepayments to secure Blackwell/Rubin supply chain
    capacity. This is a real, current dynamic (not a modeling assumption)
    and is discussed further in Section 3 and Section 8.

SECTION 3 — STRATEGIC CONTEXT: SUPPLY-CONSTRAINED GROWTH, MEMORY COSTS, AND
THE INVENTORY BUILD
================================================================================

WHY THIS QUARTER MATTERED: NVIDIA beat its own guide by ~5.7% and issued
Q3 guidance (+12.3% QoQ at the midpoint) that itself implies continued
acceleration, not deceleration — an unusual pattern for a company already
generating triple-digit YoY growth off a $215.9B prior-year base. The stock
traded up ~8.7% the session of/after the print (from $209.66 to $227.98),
and Deutsche Bank raised its price target to $220 around the same window
(i.e., a sell-side target that the stock had already exceeded by the time
of this analysis) — a reminder that sell-side targets can lag a fast-moving
tape in either direction and should not be treated as a valuation anchor.

SUPPLY, NOT DEMAND, IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT: management's own framing —
"supply chain is broadly constrained... supply sufficient to support
approximately 70% growth, while demand exceeds that level" — is the single
most important qualitative data point in this model. It reframes the
company's growth ceiling as a manufacturing/packaging/HBM capacity problem,
not a customer-willingness-to-pay problem. That is a genuinely different
risk profile than a typical hardware cyclical, but it also means near-term
results are more sensitive to TSMC/memory supply chain execution than to
NVIDIA's own execution.

THE PER-GIGAWATT MONETIZATION LADDER: management quantified the revenue
opportunity per gigawatt of deployed AI compute at ~$18B (Hopper) -> ~$25B
(Blackwell) -> ~$40B (Vera Rubin). This is the clearest articulation yet of
why NVIDIA's growth doesn't necessarily require a 1:1 relationship with
"number of chips sold" — each successive platform generation extracts more
revenue per unit of power/compute deployed, a structurally favorable dynamic
IF the company can keep executing the platform cadence.

MEMORY COST PRESSURE — A REAL AND QUANTIFIED HEADWIND: gross margin is
guided to step down from 75.0% (Q2) to 74.0% (Q3) to a 71-72% bottom in
Q4 FY2027, explicitly attributed to rising DRAM/HBM prices — an
industry-wide dynamic affecting every AI hardware buyer, not an NVIDIA-
specific execution issue, but a real near-term margin drag nonetheless.

THE INVENTORY/PREPAYMENT BUILD: Q2 FCF of $21.4B against $59.7B of GAAP net
income is a meaningfully lower conversion ratio (~36%) than NVIDIA's
historical 90%+ norm. This reflects deliberate working-capital investment — building
inventory and prepaying suppliers (TSMC packaging capacity, HBM memory) to
secure the Blackwell/Rubin ramp. This is arguably a sign of discipline
(securing scarce capacity ahead of demand) but is also a real near-term
cash-flow and balance-sheet risk if demand were ever to disappoint after
inventory has been built at scale — a dynamic worth tracking each quarter.

CUSTOMER DIVERSIFICATION IS IMPROVING BUT CONCENTRATION REMAINS REAL: ACIE
(enterprise/NeoCloud/sovereign AI, ex-hyperscaler) is now ~half of revenue
and growing ~100% annualized — a genuine diversification signal versus the
historical narrative of "NVIDIA is just a call option on 4 hyperscalers'
capex budgets." That said, the hyperscaler cohort (Microsoft, Google,
Amazon, Meta, Oracle, and now increasingly OpenAI/xAI-adjacent NeoClouds)
still represents the majority of dollars, and any pause or digestion in
that cohort's capex plans remains the single largest swing factor for
NVIDIA's near-term results — see Section 8.

COMPETITIVE CONTEXT: AMD's Instinct/Helios platform (see the AMD DCF in this
same folder) is a credible, well-capitalized #2 with real hyperscaler
commitments (Meta, OpenAI) but is explicitly targeting the low-hundreds-of-
billions revenue range by the mid-2030s in even its own Bull case — an order
of magnitude below what this model's NVDA Base case implies, consistent with
NVIDIA's continued CUDA software-ecosystem moat and multi-generation
execution lead. Hyperscaler custom silicon (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium,
Microsoft Maia, reported OpenAI silicon ambitions) is a structural,
longer-duration risk to NVIDIA's TAM capture rather than a near-term one —
these programs reduce the ceiling on how much of each hyperscaler's capex
NVIDIA can ultimately capture, even if they don't show up in quarterly
results yet.

- 10-year explicit forecast: FY2028E-FY2037E, off an FY2027E anchor built

  quarter (below)
- Unlevered FCF (FCFF) approach: FCF = NOPAT + D&A - CapEx; SBC-adjusted FCF
  further subtracts stock-based compensation as a real economic cost —
  SBC-adjusted FCF is what is discounted and used for terminal value,
  consistent with the AMD/CHTR/HIMS models in this folder
- NOPAT = GAAP operating income x (1 - 15% effective tax rate), consistent
  with the tax-rate convention used across this folder's other models
- Net cash (not net debt) added to enterprise value: $23.2B, computed as
  $56.6B of cash + marketable debt securities MINUS $33.4B of total debt
  (CONFIRMED as of Jul 26, 2026 — debt more than tripled from $8.5B at
  FY2026 year-end via a real new bond issuance; see Section 2). NVIDIA's
  separately disclosed $42.8B of marketable EQUITY securities (strategic
  stakes in AI-ecosystem companies) is DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED from this
  bridge — a conservative choice, since those holdings are volatile,
  illiquid in practice, and not core operating cash. Treat all per-share
  values here as understating true asset backing by roughly $1.75-1.80/
  share if those stakes are given even a 50% haircut and added back.

DISCOUNT RATE DERIVATION
This portfolio's other models anchor a mature, diversified mega-cap software
compounder (MSFT) at a 10% Base WACC and a smaller, higher-risk semiconductor
challenger (AMD) at 11.5% Base. NVIDIA sits between these two reference
points but closer to MSFT than to AMD: it has a far larger, better-proven,

SECTION 7 — SCENARIO ANALYSIS
================================================================================

BULL CASE (~$843/share; 9.0% WACC, 3.5% TGR, 28x exit)
  - FY2028E growth (65%) approaches management's own ~70% supply-ceiling
    framing; supply chain constraints ease meaningfully via TSMC/HBM
    capacity expansion
  - Vera Rubin's higher per-gigawatt monetization (~$40B/GW vs. Blackwell's
    ~$25B) sustains high growth even as unit/gigawatt deployment growth
    naturally slows
  - ACIE/enterprise/sovereign AI demand continues compounding near its
    recent ~100% annualized pace for several more years, further
    diversifying the customer base away from hyperscaler capex cycles
  - Operating margin holds in the high-50s to low-60s throughout the
    forecast as the CUDA software moat continues commanding full-stack
    platform pricing power
  - China re-opens meaningfully via H200-class export approvals, restoring
    a currently near-shut-off revenue pool
  - No material share loss to AMD Instinct or hyperscaler custom silicon
    over the forecast window

BASE CASE (~$330/share; 10.5% WACC, 3.0% TGR, 22x exit)
  - Growth remains very fast (45% FY2028E) but meaningfully below
    management's own supply-ceiling framing, reflecting the view that
    sustaining near-70% growth for multiple consecutive years at this
    revenue base would be a historically unprecedented outcome
  - Memory-cost pressure (already guided into Q3-Q4 FY2027) persists as a
    multi-year margin headwind, holding operating margin in the low-to-mid
    50s rather than the 60%+ level seen in early FY2027 quarters
  - ACIE diversification continues but at a decelerating pace as it scales
    off a larger base
  - China remains largely closed; no material recovery assumed
  - Some competitive share pressure from AMD and hyperscaler custom silicon
    caps upside without derailing growth

BEAR CASE (~$83/share; 13.0% WACC, 2.0% TGR, 14x exit)
  - One or more major hyperscalers pause or materially slow AI capex
    spending — a real risk given that hyperscaler capex still represents
    the majority of NVIDIA's revenue even after ACIE diversification
  - Hyperscaler custom silicon (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft
    Maia) and/or AMD's Instinct/Helios platform capture meaningfully more
    share than the Base case assumes, particularly in inference workloads
    where NVIDIA's moat is thinner than in training
  - Memory/HBM cost inflation proves structural rather than transitory,
    permanently compressing gross margin below the guided 71-72% floor
  - China export controls remain in place indefinitely with no H200-class
    recovery
  - The current inventory/prepayment build (Q2 FCF conversion well below
    historical norms) proves to have been over-earnest positioning ahead of
    demand that doesn't fully materialize, creating a working-capital
    unwind and margin pressure in a downturn
  - The AI infrastructure buildout proves more cyclical than structural —
    a multi-year digestion period follows the current capex surge, similar
    to prior computing infrastructure cycles (telecom fiber buildout,
    cloud capex cycles)

SECTION 8 — KEY RISKS
================================================================================

1. TERMINAL VALUE / LAW-OF-LARGE-NUMBERS RISK (VERY HIGH — the dominant
   risk in this specific model)
   NVIDIA is already generating a $384B annualized revenue run-rate (Q2 FY27
   x 4). This model's Base case has that figure compounding to $1.93
   TRILLION by FY2037E — a ~4.75x increase over a decade, on top of the
   ~3.5x increase NVIDIA has already delivered from FY2024 to the current
   run-rate. Small changes to the out-year growth/margin assumptions swing
   intrinsic value by tens to hundreds of dollars per share (see Table A) —
   more than the entire margin of safety this model calculates. This is
   fundamentally a bet on whether a company already this large can keep
   compounding at rates historically reserved for much smaller companies.

2. CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION AND HYPERSCALER CAPEX CYCLICALITY (HIGH)
   Even with ACIE now ~half of revenue, the majority of NVIDIA's dollars
   still flow from a relatively small number of hyperscaler/NeoCloud
   buyers whose own capex plans are set by their boards, not by NVIDIA.
   Any coordinated pause or "AI capex digestion" narrative taking hold
   among 2-3 of these buyers would hit NVIDIA's growth rate immediately
   and disproportionately, given how much of current growth is
   Data-Center-concentrated (92.5% of Q2 revenue).

3. MEMORY COST / GROSS MARGIN COMPRESSION (HIGH, and already realized)
   This is not a hypothetical risk — management has already guided gross
   margin down from 75.0% (Q2) to a 71-72% floor (Q4 FY2027) explicitly
   citing DRAM/HBM cost inflation. The Base case assumes this pressure
   persists for years rather than proving transitory; if memory costs
   normalize faster than assumed, this model's margins (and thus IV) would
   prove conservative; if they worsen, the Base case could still be
   optimistic.

4. INVENTORY / WORKING-CAPITAL BUILD (MEDIUM-HIGH, near-term)
   Q2 FY2027 FCF conversion (~40% of net income) was well below NVIDIA's
   historical norm, driven by inventory and supplier prepayments securing
   Blackwell/Rubin capacity. This is a real, current balance-sheet dynamic:
   if demand growth decelerates faster than this inventory build assumed,
   NVIDIA could face write-downs or a working-capital unwind — echoing
   (at a much larger scale) the $4.5B H20 inventory charge NVIDIA already
   took in Q1 FY2026 when China demand was cut off by export controls.

5. COMPETITIVE SHARE LOSS — AMD AND CUSTOM SILICON (MEDIUM-HIGH,
   longer-duration)
   AMD's Instinct/Helios platform is a credible, well-capitalized #2 with
   real multi-generation hyperscaler commitments (see the AMD DCF in this
   folder). Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, and Microsoft Maia are all
   maturing custom-silicon programs at NVIDIA's largest customers — meaning
   NVIDIA's most important customers are simultaneously becoming its
   longer-term competitors in silicon, a structural risk to how much of the
   growing AI compute TAM NVIDIA can ultimately capture, even if it doesn't
   show up in near-term results.

6. VALUATION / STOCK VOLATILITY (MEDIUM-HIGH, independent of the
   fundamental thesis)
   At ~$5.5T market cap, NVIDIA is among the largest companies in market
   history. Stocks of this size and growth profile have historically
   proven capable of both sustained re-rating higher (this model's Bull/
   Base cases) AND sharp multiple compression on any disappointment,
   given how much of the current price already embeds continued
   triple-digit-adjacent growth. The magnitude of the one-day move on this
   quarter's print (+8.7%) illustrates how much the market's expectations
   can swing on a single data point.

7. CHINA / EXPORT CONTROLS (MEDIUM)
   Revenue share from China has fallen to a single-digit percentage of
   total revenue. Recent H200-class export approvals are a potential (not
   yet realized at scale) re-opening path this model treats conservatively
   (largely excluded from Base, partially included in Bull) — but policy
   risk cuts both ways and could tighten further rather than ease.

8. GAAP/NON-GAAP AND DISCLOSURE TRANSPARENCY (LOW-MEDIUM, a modeling
   caveat more than a business risk)
   This model relies on press-release-level and secondary-source figures
   rather than a line-by-line 10-Q read (see Disclosures). Q1 FY2027
   operating/net income remains unverified. The Q2 FY2027 EPS
   reconciliation was resolved on a second pass ($2.46 GAAP / $2.22
   non-GAAP, confirmed directly from NVIDIA's press release), but that a
   first pass carried the wrong net income figure ($53.95B instead of the
   correct $59.7B) for a company this closely followed is itself a reminder
   to treat every secondary-sourced figure in this model with appropriate
   skepticism until corroborated by a primary source.

9. NEW DEBT ISSUANCE (LOW-MEDIUM as a per-share DCF effect, but a genuine
   capital-structure signal worth tracking)
   Total debt more than tripled during H1 FY2027, from $8.5B to $33.4B —
   NVIDIA's first material new borrowing in years. The direct effect on
   this DCF's per-share output is small (~$1/share, since net cash is a
   small fraction of enterprise value at this scale), but the SHIFT itself
   is a data point worth watching: it could reflect financing for owned
   AI infrastructure, the growing book of strategic equity stakes
   (OpenAI/xAI/CoreWeave-type investments), or accelerated shareholder
   returns funded partly by debt rather than pure FCF. None of these were
   independently confirmed as the specific use of proceeds for this model;
   flagged as an open question rather than assumed.

SECTION 9 — INVESTMENT ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

POSITIVES (WHY THE BASE/BULL CASES COULD BE RIGHT)
  + This is not a "story stock" — the numbers are already extraordinary and
    getting more extraordinary: Q2 FY2027 revenue +106% YoY off a base that
    was already the largest in the semiconductor industry, beating its own
    guide by ~5.7% for at least the second consecutive quarter of this
    magnitude
  + Growth is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained — a genuinely
    unusual and favorable position, meaning near-term results are more a
    function of TSMC/HBM capacity ramping than of NVIDIA convincing anyone
    to buy more
  + The CUDA software moat, full-stack platform strategy, and per-gigawatt
    monetization ladder ($18B->$25B->$40B across Hopper->Blackwell->Rubin)
    give NVIDIA real, demonstrated pricing power that competitors have not
    matched despite years of trying
  + ACIE/enterprise/sovereign AI diversification (now ~half of revenue,
    growing ~100% annualized) meaningfully reduces the "4 hyperscalers"
    concentration risk that dominated the bear case a year or two ago
  + Still net-cash-positive despite a new $33.4B debt load: $23.2B net
    cash in this model's conservative treatment, PLUS a separately-excluded
    $42.8B of strategic equity stakes not even counted here — real
    optionality on top of the core DCF, though the balance sheet is
    meaningfully more levered than a quarter ago (see Risk #9)
  + Even the Bull case's aggressive assumptions (65% FY2028E growth) sit
    at or below management's own stated ~70% supply-ceiling framing — this
    is not a scenario built on assumptions more optimistic than the
    company's own guidance

NEGATIVES (WHY THIS SHOULD NOT BE TAKEN AT FACE VALUE)
  - The Base case requires NVIDIA's revenue to compound at a declining-but-
    still-very-high rate for a full decade, off a base that is ALREADY
    larger than most economies' individual industries — this is the
    single largest source of model risk, and the sensitivity tables show
    the "undervalued" conclusion is not robust to modest changes in
    long-run assumptions
  - Q2 FY2027's FCF conversion (~40% of net income) was a real, current
    warning sign about working-capital intensity that this model's
    forward capex/D&A assumptions may not fully capture if the pattern
    persists
  - Every dollar of this model's upside assumes continued AI infrastructure
    capex growth from a customer base that is still meaningfully
    concentrated, even after diversification progress
  - This model deliberately excludes $42.8B of marketable equity securities
    from the equity bridge for conservatism, but their existence also
    signals NVIDIA is increasingly playing a financial/strategic-investor
    role in its own customer ecosystem (stakes in AI-ecosystem companies)
    — a dynamic that adds complexity to reading the "real" underlying
    hardware business economics
  - Data gaps remain (Q1 FY2027 operating income — see Disclosures) and the
    fact that this model's own first draft initially misstated Q2 FY2027
    net income and total debt (both since corrected against a primary
    press release) is itself evidence that parts of this analysis rest on
    secondary-source synthesis that warrants independent verification
    before acting on it

BOTTOM LINE
This model's headline conclusion — that NVDA at $227.98 is not obviously
overvalued, and may even carry a real margin of safety versus a
semi-conservative Base case — is a genuinely different outcome than the
AMD model in this same folder, and it should be read with commensurate
skepticism precisely because it is the more comfortable, less contrarian
conclusion to reach. The single biggest reason to distrust it is scale:
this is a 10-year forecast for what is very likely about to be the world's
most valuable company, built on the assumption that a business already
generating a $384B annualized run-rate can nearly quintuple over a decade.
The Bear case ($84/share) is a genuine reminder of what happens if AI
capex proves more cyclical than structural, or if competitive share erodes
faster than assumed — and it implies more than 60% downside from here.
The honest takeaway is not "NVDA is cheap," it is "NVDA's price today is
broadly consistent with continued (not accelerating) execution against
its own recently-issued guidance, priced by a market that has already seen
this company beat its guide in most recent quarters" — which is a very
different, much more fragile claim than a traditional margin-of-safety
value case.

Conviction level on base case: MODERATE (business quality and near-term
momentum are both genuinely exceptional) / LOW-MODERATE (as a valuation
call — the terminal-value sensitivity is simply too large relative to the
apparent margin of safety to treat this as a high-conviction "cheap" call)
Would need to see: at least 2-3 more quarters confirming FCF conversion
recovers toward historical norms (i.e., the inventory build was tactical,
not a sign of slowing demand), and continued ACIE growth at scale (proof
the hyperscaler-concentration risk is genuinely structurally declining) —
before treating the Base case's growth assumptions as more than
plausible-but-unproven.
