## PART 1 — THE HOOK

### Slide 1 — Title
- **Tesla Robotaxi: What Is It Worth on Its Own?**
- Subtitle: Spin it off, make it buy its cars and its software at market prices, then value it
- "As of Oct 14, 2026 · TSLA $375.23 · ~$1.48T market cap"

### Slide 2 — The Question
- The bull thesis says robotaxi is most of Tesla's value. Is that true?
- The usual mistake: valuing robotaxi as if it gets its cars at cost and its software for free
- This video's rule: **Robotaxi is a separate company.** It buys Cybercabs from Tesla Auto at market
  prices, licenses the self-driving software, insures itself, and raises its own capital
- Teaser: the answer splits in two. What the *network* is worth and what *Tesla* earns from it are
  very different numbers

### Slide 3 — Answer Up Front (for viewers who won't stay)
| | Enterprise value | Per TSLA share | % of TSLA mkt cap |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear (35%) | −$33.5B | −$8.47 | n/m |
| **Base (45%)** | **$171.3B** | **$43.38** | **11.6%** |
| Bull (20%) | $1,414.7B | $358.19 | 95.5% |
| Probability-weighted | $348.3B | $88.19 | 23.5% |
- One line: "A real, big business, but as a standalone operator it covers about an eighth of
  Tesla's market cap in the Base case."

## PART 2 — WHERE ROBOTAXI STANDS TODAY

### Slide 4 — Live Operations (Oct 2026)
- Driverless public rides in **6 metros**: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa
- SF Bay Area: safety driver required (CA charter/limo permit); Phoenix staged; Nevada approved
  for up to 5,000 AVs (Aug 2026)
- Texas fleet **739** = 420 Model Y (held flat) + **319 Cybercabs** (Oct 12)
- Tesla, Oct 10: "300+ Cybercabs serving Austin, an **8x increase in 30 days**"
- Visual: US map with dots for live / staged / permitted cities

### Slide 5 — Cybercab: The Purpose-Built Bet
- 2 seats, no steering wheel or pedals, ~50 kWh pack
- Production started Q2 2026; Giga Texas capacity **>125,000/yr** (first time disclosed)
- Musk price target: **"$30,000 or less," before 2027**
- Caveat: the Q1 deck's "mass production in 2026" language was dropped from the Q2 deck

### Slide 6 — Revenue Today Is a Rounding Error
- ~**2.4M cumulative paid miles** through June 30, 2026 (~0.9M in Q2)
- Austin pricing: **$3.00 base + $1.40/mi** (raised from $1.00/mi in Mar 2026)
- ~$2M of fares per quarter *(derived)*, against Tesla's $28.2B Q2 revenue
- Robotaxi isn't a reported segment. It's buried in "Services & other" ($4.58B in Q2, +50% YoY),
  next to FSD subscriptions, Supercharging, and insurance
- Takeaway: **everything we value is the ramp**

### Slide 7 — Context: Tesla's Core Business Is Under Pressure
- FY2025 revenue ~$94.8B (−3%, first annual decline); operating income $4.35B (−38%)
- Q2 2026 operating margin **1.4%** ($398M) as R&D rose +49% YoY to $2.37B
- Q3 2026 deliveries 486,532 (beat consensus by ~5%, −2% YoY)
- Why it matters: the market is paying ~$1.48T for a company earning low-single-digit operating
  margins today, so the future (robotaxi, Optimus) is doing the work

## PART 3 — THE TAM

### Slide 8 — How Big Is the Robotaxi Market? (Depends Who You Ask)

|---|---|

| MarketsandMarkets | $45.7B by 2030; $105B by 2035 |
| Meticulous Research | $1.49T by 2035 (outlier) |
- Talking point: a 30x spread between estimates means "TAM" here is a story, not a number

### Slide 9 — Reality Anchor: What People Spend on Rides Today
- Uber Mobility gross bookings **$29.0B in Q2 2026** (+20%), about **$116B/yr** annualized
- Lyft FY2025 gross bookings $18.5B
- Total Uber platform: $58.0B quarterly gross bookings, 3.9B trips/quarter
- Robotaxi's real upside is beyond ride-hail: converting **personal car miles** into paid miles.
  That's the bull case's TAM, and the hardest part to prove

### Slide 10 — Our Model vs. the TAM (the sanity check)
- Base-case revenue: **$200B in 2035**, **$259B in 2036**
- Versus Goldman's 2035 global market of $415B: Tesla alone is **~48%** *(derived)*; versus global
  ex-China (~$368B *(derived)*) it is **~54%**
- Bull-case 2035 revenue ($515B) is **~124% of Goldman's entire global market**. Bull assumes
  robotaxi eats into car ownership, not just ride-hail
- Base 2036 revenue ≈ **2.2x Uber's entire Mobility business today** *(derived)*
- Honest framing: even the Base case assumes Tesla becomes the dominant global player

## PART 5 — THE THOUGHT EXPERIMENT: SPIN IT OFF

### Slide 17 — Four Things Change When Robotaxi Leaves Tesla
1. **Cars at market price, not cost.** $35k (2026) → $30k (2027, Musk's target) → $27k (2032+).
   The previous "inside Tesla" model used $20k
2. **Software is licensed.** Tesla keeps the FSD stack (it ships in every consumer Tesla), and
   RobotaxiCo pays **10% of fares** (Base)
3. **No in-house insurance.** Add a ~30% load on the per-mile loss cost
4. **Its own cost of capital.** It needs ~$28B of outside funding, so the discount rate is **12%**
   vs ~10% for Tesla
- Visual: one box ("RobotaxiCo") with arrows to Tesla Auto ($ for cars), Tesla AI ($ for
  license), insurers, and capital markets

### Slide 18 — Why 10% for the Software? (the biggest judgment call)
- No market exists for driverless software. Reference points:
  - Consumer FSD (Supervised): $99/mo. That doesn't remove the driver, so it's a **floor**
  - Ride-hail networks keep ~25–30% of fares for demand + network + software combined
  - The software is the reason there's no driver, and the driver is the biggest cost in ride-hail
- Alternative: a flat **$200/car/month** license → **+$55.8B** to Base value (~$227B)
- Invite the audience: "Comment what you think Tesla would actually charge"

## PART 6 — THE MODEL

### Slide 19 — How It's Built
- 10-year forecast (2027–2036) + terminal value; 3 scenarios, each with its own WACC, growth and
  exit multiple
- Revenue = cars on the road × miles per car × fare per mile
- Costs: per-mile operating costs (energy, maintenance, cleaning, depots, remote operators,
  insurance, support), license, R&D, SG&A, stock comp
- Cash flow discounted **after** stock-based comp; 15% tax only in profitable years
- Terminal value = average of perpetuity growth and EV/EBITDA exit multiple

### Slide 20 — Three Scenarios
| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 2036 production | 430k | **2.0M** (Musk's target, 10 yrs late) | 4.0M (stretch target, fully met) |
| 2036 avg fleet | 1.24M | **5.2M** | 10.5M |
| Mature miles/car/yr | 55k | **75k** | 95k |
| Long-run car price | $31k | **$27k** | $25k |
| License | 12% | **10%** | 8% |
| WACC / growth / exit | 14% / 2% / 6x | **12% / 3% / 10x** | 10.5% / 3.5% / 16x |
- Weights: **35% Bear / 45% Base / 20% Bull**. Bear is heavy because Cybercab has run publicly for
  ~6 weeks, in one city

### Slide 21 — Base Case P&L: The Ramp
| $B | 2028 | 2030 | 2032 | 2034 | 2036 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Revenue | 1.8 | 15.5 | 59.8 | 144.9 | **258.6** |
| EBITDA | −1.0 | 3.2 | 17.7 | 44.3 | **79.6** (30.8%) |
| EBIT | −1.2 | 1.4 | 11.0 | 27.4 | **47.9** (18.5%) |
| License paid to Tesla | 0.2 | 1.6 | 6.0 | 14.5 | **25.9** |
- Visual: stacked bar of where each fare dollar goes in 2036: opex ~56%, license 10%, D&A ~12%, insurance load + R&D + SG&A ~4%,
  profit ~18.5%

### Slide 22 — The J-Curve: Profitable in 2030, Cash Positive in 2035
- SBC-adjusted free cash flow ($B): 2027 −1.5 · 2029 −3.5 · 2031 −5.2 · 2033 −3.8 · 2035 **+4.4** ·
  2036 **+10.8**
- **Peak funding need: −$27.5B** (2034)
- Why: profitable from 2030, but every new car costs $27–30k up front at market price
- Visual: line chart of cumulative FCF dipping to −$27.5B then turning up

### Slide 23 — The Valuation Bridge (Base)
- PV of 10 years of cash flow: **−$11.0B** (it's negative!)
- PV of terminal value: perpetuity $114.5B, exit multiple $250.1B, average **$182.3B**
- **Enterprise value $171.3B → $43.38 per TSLA share**
- Terminal value is **106%** of the total: all of the value sits after 2036
- The two terminal methods disagree by ~2.3x, and that gap is itself a finding

## PART 7 — WHO REALLY CAPTURES THE VALUE?

### Slide 24 — Standalone vs. Inside Tesla
| Base case | Standalone | Inside Tesla |
|---|--:|--:|
| Enterprise value | $171.3B | $307.6B |
| 2036 EBITDA | $79.6B | $108.8B |
| Cumulative FCF 2026–36 | −$12.3B | +$85.3B |
- Gap = **$136.3B**. What drives it: the license (+$107B), insurance load (+$24B), car markup (+$22B)
- **Most of it isn't lost value.** The license (~$26B/yr by 2036) and car margin (~$9B/yr) become
  Tesla revenue
- Inside Tesla at a Tesla-like 10% discount rate: ~**$409B**

### Slide 25 — The Supplier vs. the Operator
- Value split (Base): **operator $171B**, **software owner ~$107B**, **car maker ~$22B**
- At a 15–20% license, the software owner would capture *more* than the operator
- Every 5 points of license rate ≈ **$53.5B**; every $2,500 of car price ≈ **$12B**. The software
  terms matter ~4x more than the car price
- Tie back to Waymo ($126B, owns its software) and Uber (operator, licenses others' software):
  **Tesla is the only player that is both**

### Slide 26 — Sensitivity: What Moves the Number
- **Fare: ±$0.10/mi ≈ ±$140B**, the most powerful single input
  (−$0.10 → $29.6B · Base $171.3B · +$0.10 → $313.1B · +$0.20 → $454.7B)
- Discount rate: 10% → $227.5B · 12% → $171.3B · 14% → $132.9B
- Exit multiple 12x instead of 10x: +$25B
- Visual: tornado chart (fare, license %, WACC, exit multiple, car price)

## PART 8 — CAN ROBOTAXI CARRY TSLA?

### Slide 27 — Reverse DCF
| To be worth… | Fares every year would need to be… | …or the 2036 fleet would need to be |
|---|---|---|
| $500B | +$0.23/mi | 14.1M cars (2.7x Base) |
| $741B (½ of TSLA) | +$0.40/mi | 20.6M cars (4x Base) |
| **$1.48T (all of TSLA)** | **+$0.93/mi ($1.58 in 2036)** | **40.7M cars (7.8x Base)** |
- 40.7M cars is ~3.5x the Bull case, which already assumes Musk's 4M/yr target is fully met
- $1.58/mi in 2036 is ~35–45% *above* Tesla's own current Austin pricing, after ten years of
  competition

### Slide 28 — So Where Does the Rest of $1.48T Come From?
- Standalone robotaxi: **12% (Base) to 24% (prob-weighted)** of TSLA's market cap
- The remainder has to come from Auto, Energy, FSD software (consumer + licensing robotaxi), Optimus,
  and Tesla's supplier profits on robotaxi itself
- Framing: "If you own TSLA for robotaxi, you're really betting on Tesla as an **autonomy
  supplier**, not only as a taxi company"

## PART 9 — RISKS & BOTTOM LINE

### Slide 29 — The Bear Case Isn't "Small," It's Negative
- Bear: 430k cars/yr by 2036, never reaches positive EBITDA, burns **$89B** over a decade → EV
  **−$33.5B**
- Why: at market car prices plus a 12% license, a sub-scale fleet loses money on every car
- A real standalone company would shut down by ~2029. Flooring Bear at $0 lifts the
  probability-weighted value to $360B

### Slide 30 — Key Risks
1. **License terms are made up.** No market exists; license 0–20% × car price ±$5k spans $40–302B of Base value
2. **Fare war:** Waymo's premium is collapsing, Uber aggregates 30+ AV fleets, Tesla already cheapest
3. **Safety / regulatory:** one bad incident can freeze a no-steering-wheel car nationwide (Cruise 2023)
4. **Musk timelines:** Base needs 2M cars/yr by 2036, and every prior target slipped
5. **Utilization:** Base assumes 75k miles/car/yr, ~2x Waymo today
6. **Funding:** $28B (Base) to $89B (Bear) of outside capital needed

### Slide 31 — Bull vs. Bear, Side by Side
| The bull says | The bear says |
|---|---|
| Cybercab fleet up 8x in 30 days; capacity >125k/yr | Waymo has ~5x the cars, 14 cities and 500k rides/week |
| Cheapest fares in the market, by a lot | Those fares were subsidized and with safety drivers |
| Bull EV $1.41T, ≈ all of TSLA's market cap | Base covers only 12%; Bear is negative |
| Tesla is both software owner and operator | Licensing value only counts if others use the software too |

### Slide 32 — Bottom Line
- **Robotaxi as a standalone company: ~$171B Base, ~$348B probability-weighted**, a top-tier
  business but nowhere near a trillion
- **To Tesla as a whole it's worth more (~$308–409B Base)** because Tesla keeps the car margin
  and the software license
- The market at $1.48T is pricing something close to the **Bull case plus** big value from Optimus
  and from licensing beyond Tesla's own fleet
- What would change the view: Cybercab in 5+ cities with paid driverless rides, a real
  utilization disclosure, or a third-party license deal that puts a price on the software
