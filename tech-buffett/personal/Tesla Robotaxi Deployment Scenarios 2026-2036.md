# Tesla Robotaxi Deployment Scenarios, 2026–2036

_Analysis date: 2026-09-06. Revised 2026-09-06 after city-by-city + Cybercab-sighting research
(see companion files: "Tesla Robotaxi Cities - Operating Status" and
"Tesla Robotaxi - Next Cities (Spotted and Testing)")._

## Revision note (2026-09-06)

City-by-city and Cybercab-sighting research did **not** materially change the framework or the
long-run curves. Three things got clearer and largely cancel:

- **Manufacturing is confirmed as not the near-term constraint.** Hundreds of Cybercabs staged at
  Giga Texas since April 2026, but only ~45 registered/deployed. The built-vs-deployed gap shows
  deployment is gated by the safety case, operations, and regulatory comfort — as the tables assume.
- **Metro permits are coming faster than modeled** — 2 metros in Jan 2026, 7 by Sept, with
  Phoenix / Las Vegas / New Orleans next and Nevada pre-clearing 5,000 vehicles.
- **…but per-metro fleets are thinner than modeled** — Florida geofences are 4–20 sq mi and the
  no-monitor fleet is still "low tens" after 15 months. Fast metro count × thin fleet-per-metro
  nets close to the prior totals.

**Changes applied:** bull 2026 trimmed (we are in September and nowhere near 1,000 no-monitor
vehicles); base 2027–2028 nudged up ~10–15% for the faster metro cadence. Bear values unchanged,
but the **unresolved NHTSA probe** into Cybercab self-certification makes the bear scenario more
*probable* even though its point values don't move. 2029+ figures unchanged — those are governed by
the regulatory regime and ops scaling, which this research does not resolve.

Would move the numbers materially (none has happened yet): the NHTSA probe resolving either way;
evidence a single metro's no-monitor fleet breaks from "low tens" into the thousands; a real
federal AV framework replacing the state-by-state patchwork.

**Also added 2026-09-06:** a *Revenue & fleet operating profit* section (after the Bull table),
built off these fleet numbers × the utilization and cost-per-mile work in the companion file
*Tesla Cybercab - Operating Cost per Mile (Scaled)*.

**Metric:** vehicles in paid driverless service (no in-car safety monitor), at least part of the time.
If you instead count every safety-monitored pilot car, roughly double every number below.

**Owner-supplied cars** (customers renting their FSD Teslas into the network) are treated as upside
optionality and are only material in the bull case from ~2030; the base and bear numbers are
Tesla/partner-operated fleet.

**Regulatory assumption:** base and bull assume NHTSA does **not** force the Cybercab back under the
2,500-vehicle/year FMVSS exemption cap. Bear assumes recurring regulatory, safety, and operations
friction.

## Current state (Sept 2026), for grounding

- **7 operating metros:** Austin, Dallas, Houston (TX), Miami, Orlando, Tampa (FL), SF Bay Area (CA).
  Six run driverless; the Florida cities and Dallas/Houston skipped the supervised phase entirely.
  Bay Area still uses a safety driver (CA limo permit).
- ~420 *authorized* vehicles in Texas. Genuinely *unsupervised* fleet still only low tens; most of
  the fleet is safety-monitored retrofitted Model Y. Service areas are small (4–20 sq mi in Florida).
- **Next up:** Phoenix (~60 Model Ys staged), Las Vegas (Nevada cleared up to 5,000 vehicles, 120-day
  launch clock), New Orleans (fleet logging miles). Cybercab test units also seen in Philadelphia —
  no geofence anywhere in PA.
- Cybercab: continuous production began April 2026; hundreds staged at Giga Texas but only ~45
  registered/deployed; paid Cybercab rides started Sept 3 in Austin. Tesla removed Cybercab from its
  "volume production in 2026" list in July, citing battery-pack capacity.
- Tesla self-certified the Cybercab to FMVSS (for ~1,000 units) rather than take an NHTSA exemption;
  NHTSA opened a probe into that self-certification in Sept 2026. Biggest near-term swing factor.
- Morgan Stanley published model: ~1,000 operational by end-2026, ~30,000 by 2030, ~1M by 2035.
  Musk framing: "millions by 2027."

## Revenue & fleet operating profit (added 2026-09-06)

### Method

`Revenue = avg active fleet × miles/vehicle/yr × fare/mi`.
`Fleet operating profit = Revenue − (miles × fully-loaded cost/mi)`.

- **Fleet** = average of prior and current year-end "cumulative active" from the tables above
  (new vehicles added mid-year don't earn a full year).
- **Miles/vehicle/yr** = the utilization ramp from the companion file
  *Tesla Cybercab - Operating Cost per Mile (Scaled)* (~30k early → ~75–80k mature in the base;
  lower in bear, higher in bull).
- **Fully-loaded cost/mi** = that file's opex + vehicle depreciation + payment processing,
  mapped to calendar years and adjusted per scenario.
- **Fare/mi** = judgment. Tesla undercuts Waymo (~$1.80–2.50/mi) from the start and drives price
  down to take share and grow the market, but holds well above Musk's aspirational $0.30–0.40/mi
  through 2036. **This is the single most judgment-laden input** — see sensitivity below.

**Excluded from "profit":** Tesla corporate autonomy R&D, charging/depot **infrastructure** capex
and its D&A, fleet SG&A above local ops, income tax, catastrophe-year insurance tails, and any
platform-licensing or owner-fleet revenue-share economics. This is a fleet-level EBIT-like figure,
**not** a net-income contribution.

### Read

- **Breakeven** (fully loaded, incl. vehicle depreciation): base **~2028**, bull **~2028**,
  bear **never durably** — it stays a strategically maintained near-breakeven business.
- **2026–27 loses money in every scenario** — promotional fares, tiny scale, overhead not yet
  spread. That matches where Tesla actually is now.
- **Fare sensitivity dominates the outer years.** At 2036 base fleet-miles (~103B), every
  **$0.10/mi** of fare = **~$10B** of revenue and, since cost is unchanged, **~$10B** of profit.
  Base 2036 profit of ~$22B is really a ~$12–32B range on a ±$0.10 fare.
- **Sanity check on scale:** base 2036 ≈ 103B robotaxi miles ≈ ~3% of US vehicle-miles and ~2×
  today's total US ride-hail. Bull 2036 ≈ 382B miles ≈ ~11–12% of US VMT and revenue larger than
  Tesla's entire 2025 company revenue — a "robotaxi replaces car ownership" world, not a base case.
- **What could still make base profit zero:** the NHTSA probe going badly, a safety-event fare
  collapse, faster fare competition from Waymo/others, or utilization stalling near Waymo's current
  ~30–35k mi/yr instead of climbing to ~75k.

## What's binding, by phase

**2026–2028 — the unsupervised safety case and federal certification.**
Manufacturing is irrelevant here — confirmed by the Cybercab built-vs-deployed gap (hundreds
staged at Giga Texas, ~45 deployed). The fleet is small because regulators and Tesla's own risk
tolerance keep it small. As of Sept 2026 the no-monitor fleet is still "low tens" after 15 months —
that data point is why the near-term numbers are low across all three cases, even as the operating-
metro count climbs quickly.

**2029–2032 — legalization pace, operations, and capital.**
Where base and bull diverge hard. The Cybercab line is designed for up to ~2M/yr across factories,
so supply is not the limit. Limits are: how fast states legalize driverless commercial operation
(TX/FL/AZ/NV early; CA, NY, IL, MA late or never on Tesla's timeline), how fast Tesla stands up
depots/charging/cleaning/remote-assist/insurance per metro, and competition from Waymo/Zoox/Amazon
for the best markets.

**2033–2036 — saturation, share, and unit economics.**
Premium metros are largely covered; growth comes from mid-size cities, fleet replacement, and taking
share from human ride-hail. Base adds flatten; bull keeps compounding only if robotaxis displace a
large share of private second cars, not just ride-hail.

**Constraint ranking (all cases):** 1) unsupervised FSD safety case → 2) federal/state regulation →
3) operations scaling → 4) manufacturing (least binding this decade).

## Caveats

10-year forecasts here are scenario-planning, not prediction. Compounding uncertainty means the
2033–2036 numbers could be off by ~5x either way. The whole curve resets on any of: a high-profile
fatal-crash cluster, a federal restriction (current NHTSA probe going badly), a state-level ban wave,
or Tesla being slower at unglamorous operations work than at building cars.

The revenue & profit section adds one more layer of uncertainty on top of the fleet uncertainty:
the fare path is a judgment call, and at 2036 scale a ±$0.10/mi fare move is a ±~$10B profit swing
in the base case.

**One-number summary:** ~100–150k active Tesla robotaxis by end of 2030 (base); the fleet turns a
fully-loaded operating profit around 2028 and reaches ~$20B/yr on ~$68B revenue by 2036 in the base
case — with genuine odds of being off by 3x in either direction.
