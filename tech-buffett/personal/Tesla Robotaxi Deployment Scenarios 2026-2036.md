# Tesla Robotaxi Deployment Scenarios, 2026–2036

_Analysis date: 2026-09-06_

**Metric:** vehicles in paid driverless service (no in-car safety monitor), at least part of the time.
If you instead count every safety-monitored pilot car, roughly double every number below.

**Owner-supplied cars** (customers renting their FSD Teslas into the network) are treated as upside
optionality and are only material in the bull case from ~2030; the base and bear numbers are
Tesla/partner-operated fleet.

**Regulatory assumption:** base and bull assume NHTSA does **not** force the Cybercab back under the
2,500-vehicle/year FMVSS exemption cap. Bear assumes recurring regulatory, safety, and operations
friction.

---

## Current state (Sept 2026), for grounding

- ~420 *authorized* vehicles in Texas across 7 metros (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando,
  Tampa, SF Bay Area). Genuinely *unsupervised* fleet still only low tens; most of the fleet is
  safety-monitored retrofitted Model Y.
- Cybercab: continuous production began April 2026; ~45 in fleet by early Sept; paid Cybercab rides
  started Sept 3 in Austin. Tesla removed Cybercab from its "volume production in 2026" list in July,
  citing battery-pack capacity.
- Tesla self-certified the Cybercab to FMVSS (for ~1,000 units) rather than take an NHTSA exemption;
  NHTSA opened a probe into that self-certification in Sept 2026. Biggest near-term swing factor.
- Morgan Stanley published model: ~1,000 operational by end-2026, ~30,000 by 2030, ~1M by 2035.
  Musk framing: "millions by 2027."

## What's binding, by phase

**2026–2028 — the unsupervised safety case and federal certification.**
Manufacturing is irrelevant here. The fleet is small because regulators and Tesla's own risk
tolerance keep it small. As of Sept 2026 the no-monitor fleet is ~25 vehicles after 15 months —
that data point is why the near-term numbers are low across all three cases.

**2029–2032 — legalization pace, operations, and capital.**
Where base and bull diverge hard. The Cybercab line is designed for up to ~2M/yr across factories,
so supply is not the limit. Limits are: how fast states legalize driverless commercial operation
(TX/FL/AZ/NV early; CA, NY, IL, MA late or never on Tesla's timeline), how fast Tesla stands up
depots/charging/cleaning/remote-assist/insurance per metro, and competition from Waymo/Zoox/Amazon
for the best markets.

**2033–2036 — saturation, share, and unit economics.**
Premium metros are largely covered; growth comes from mid-size cities, fleet replacement, and taking
share from human ride-hail. Base adds flatten; bull keeps compounding only if robotaxis displace a
large share of private second cars, not just ride-hail.

**Constraint ranking (all cases):** 1) unsupervised FSD safety case → 2) federal/state regulation →
3) operations scaling → 4) manufacturing (least binding this decade).

---

## Caveats

10-year forecasts here are scenario-planning, not prediction. Compounding uncertainty means the
2033–2036 numbers could be off by ~5x either way. The whole curve resets on any of: a high-profile
fatal-crash cluster, a federal restriction (current NHTSA probe going badly), a state-level ban wave,
or Tesla being slower at unglamorous operations work than at building cars.

**One-number summary:** ~100–150k active Tesla robotaxis by end of 2030 (base), with genuine odds
of being off by 3x in either direction.
